Skilled Work Regional Visa (Subclass 491): Points, Regional Areas and the Path to PR

The Skilled Work Regional visa (subclass 491) is a five-year provisional visa for skilled workers who are nominated by a state or territory, or sponsored by an eligible relative, to live and work in regional Australia. The nomination adds 15 points, and after three years you can apply for permanent residence through the subclass 191. This guide explains how it works and who it suits.

The Basics

What Is the Skilled Work Regional Visa (Subclass 491)?

A provisional visa that lets skilled workers live and work in regional Australia, with a pathway to permanent residence.

The subclass 491 is a points-tested provisional skilled visa, valid for five years. You submit an Expression of Interest (EOI) in SkillSelect, the Department of Home Affairs' online system. To be invited, you need either a nomination from a state or territory government or sponsorship from an eligible relative who lives in a designated regional area. Either one adds 15 points to your score, which is why the 491 suits candidates whose points are too low for the 189 or the 190.

The trade-off is location. A condition of the visa requires you to live, work and study only in a designated regional area. In return, you can include your partner and children, and after three years you may apply for the Permanent Residence (Skilled Regional) visa, subclass 191. That makes the 491 a two-step route: a regional visa first, then permanent residence.

Eligibility

Subclass 491 Visa Eligibility Requirements

You need to meet every requirement below. A state's own rules, or the family sponsorship rules, sit on top of these national criteria.

  • Nomination or sponsorship: a nomination from a state or territory government, or sponsorship from an eligible relative who lives in a designated regional area.
  • Eligible occupation: an occupation that is eligible for the stream you use. Each state and territory publishes its own list, and the family sponsored stream has its own occupation requirements.
  • Positive skills assessment: the relevant assessing authority must confirm your skills for that occupation.
  • Age: you must be under 45 when you are invited to apply.
  • English: at least Competent English, shown through an approved test such as IELTS, PTE Academic, TOEFL iBT, OET or Cambridge C1 Advanced, or an accepted passport.
  • Points: at least 65 points on the points test, which includes the 15 points for nomination or sponsorship.
  • Invitation: an invitation to apply issued through SkillSelect, which you must act on within 60 days.
  • Health and character: meet the Australian health requirement and provide police certificates for the character requirement.

Lists and criteria change. State occupation lists and nomination criteria are updated often, and being on a list does not guarantee nomination or an invitation. We check the current criteria and your assessing authority before we advise you.

How the 15 points change your options

Because nomination or sponsorship adds 15 points, a profile with 50 points on the table above reaches the 65-point pass mark. That puts the 491 within reach of candidates who would struggle in a 189 or 190 queue. The pass mark is only a minimum, though. States and territories choose from the profiles that meet their own criteria, and many prefer higher scores, current work in the region or occupations facing local shortages.

Two Ways In

State-Nominated and Family-Sponsored 491: Which Stream Applies to You?

Both streams add 15 points and both need an invitation through SkillSelect, but the rules differ.

Stream 1

State or territory nominated

A state or territory government nominates you from its own regional program. Each jurisdiction sets its own occupation list, minimum requirements and opening dates, and some look for current work, study or a job offer in the region. Programs open and close during the year as their allocations are used.

Stream 2

Family sponsored

An eligible relative sponsors you. The sponsor must be an Australian citizen, permanent resident or eligible New Zealand citizen, be aged 18 or over, and live in a designated regional area. Eligible relatives include a parent, child or step-child, sibling, aunt or uncle, niece or nephew, grandparent and first cousin. Home Affairs must approve the sponsorship.

Check the sponsor's details early. The family stream depends on the relative's status and where they live, and the rules for how long they must have lived in the regional area are strict. We check the sponsor's eligibility before you commit to an assessment or test.

Where You Can Live

Designated Regional Areas: Where You Can Live and Work on a 491

The visa is tied to location, so check your target address before you apply.

For the 491, designated regional areas are all of Australia except Sydney, Melbourne and Brisbane. That means cities such as Perth, Adelaide, Canberra, Hobart, Darwin, Newcastle, Wollongong, Geelong and the Gold Coast count as regional. Home Affairs maintains the official list by postcode, so confirm your exact address on that list before you accept a job offer or sign a lease.

Condition 8579 on the visa requires you to live, work and study only in a designated regional area, and it applies to your family members on the visa as well. You must also tell the Department of Home Affairs within 14 days if your address changes (condition 8580). Breaching these conditions can lead to cancellation and can end your pathway to permanent residence. Short trips to a major city for a holiday or an appointment are different from living or working there.

Permanent Residence

From the 491 to Permanent Residence: The Subclass 191 Pathway

Most applicants choose the 491 because of what comes after it.

The Permanent Residence (Skilled Regional) visa, subclass 191, is the permanent visa for 491 holders. You can apply once you have held the 491 (or the related 494) for at least three years, and you must apply before the 491 expires at the five-year mark. The three years run from the date of grant.

Plan from day one. Keep payslips, tax returns and proof of your regional address from the first month. Until you have held the 491 for three years you generally cannot be granted certain other permanent visas, including the 189 and 190. Income rules for the 191 can be changed by the government, so we recheck them before you lodge.

Step Zero

Skills Assessment for the 491 Visa

Your skills assessment is the first formal gate, and the longest lead time you control.

A skills assessment is an independent check that your qualifications and work experience meet Australian standards for your nominated occupation. Each occupation is linked to a specific assessing authority, for example Engineers Australia for engineering occupations, the Australian Computer Society for ICT roles, VETASSESS for many professional and trade occupations, CPA Australia or CA ANZ for accountants, and the Australian Nursing and Midwifery Accreditation Council for nursing and midwifery.

Each authority sets its own documents, fees and timeframes. For the 491, check that the occupation you assess for is also eligible for the stream you plan to use, because an assessment for an occupation the state does not invite will not support a nomination. We help you match your degree and job duties to the right ANZSCO occupation before you pay for an assessment.

Match

Pick the right occupation

Your job duties, not your job title, decide which ANZSCO code fits, and the stream you use must also accept that occupation.

03

Take your English test

Book IELTS, PTE Academic or another approved test, and check whether your target state asks for more than Competent.

04

Submit your Expression of Interest

Create your SkillSelect EOI with accurate claims and select the 491 stream and the states you are interested in. Everything you claim must be provable later.

05

Get nominated or sponsored

Apply to the state or territory under its regional program, or ask your eligible relative to sponsor you through the family stream.

06

Receive an invitation

Once you are nominated or sponsored, 15 points are added to your EOI and the Department of Home Affairs can invite you to apply.

07

Lodge within 60 days

Submit your online application through ImmiAccount with identity, qualification, employment, English, health and character evidence.

08

Health checks, biometrics and grant

Complete medicals and any requested checks, then respond quickly to Department requests until a decision is made. After grant, live and work in a designated regional area.

Get Organised

Documents to Prepare Before You Apply

Not every applicant needs every item, but this list covers what most 491 files require.

  • Passport and national ID for every person on the application.
  • Positive skills assessment outcome letter.
  • English test result report that is still valid.
  • Degree certificates and academic transcripts.
  • Employment references showing duties, hours and dates.
  • Payslips, tax returns or bank statements supporting employment claims.
  • Police certificates from each country you have lived in for the required period.
  • Relationship evidence and partner documents, if applicable.
  • Birth certificates for dependent children.
  • For the family stream, evidence of your relationship to the sponsor and of the sponsor's status and regional residence.

Fit Check

Is the Skilled Work Regional Visa Right for You?

The 491 tends to suit you if

  • Your points reach 65 only with the extra 15 points.
  • You are happy to live and work outside Sydney, Melbourne and Brisbane for at least three years.
  • You have an eligible relative in a regional area, or an occupation a state or territory wants.
  • You are under 45 with Competent English or better and a positive skills assessment.

Consider another route if

  • You need to live in Sydney, Melbourne or Brisbane for work or family.
  • Your score is high enough for a 189 or a 190 nomination.
  • You want permanent residence from day one rather than after three years.
  • An employer is willing to sponsor you.
Avoid These

Common Subclass 491 Mistakes That Cost Applicants a Year

Breaking the regional condition

Living or working in Sydney, Melbourne or Brisbane on a 491 can lead to cancellation and end your path to the 191.

Over-claiming points

Points in your EOI must be provable when you apply. A claim you cannot back up can lead to refusal, and later visa applications may be affected.

Assuming a relative qualifies

The family stream depends on the relationship, the sponsor's status and where they live. Check these before paying for an assessment.

Not lodging tax returns

The 191 needs ATO notices of assessment, so a missed tax return can stall your permanent residence application.

Missing the 60-day window

You cannot extend the deadline after your invitation. Gathering police certificates and medicals late is the usual reason files run behind.

Ignoring the 191 deadline

You must apply for the 191 before the 491 expires, so track your three-year and five-year dates from the grant letter.

Common Questions

Subclass 491 Visa FAQs

How many points do I need for the subclass 491 visa?

The minimum is 65 points, and that total includes the 15 points added by state or territory nomination or by an approved family sponsor. So you need at least 50 points on the points test before the 15 are added.

Can the 491 lead to permanent residence?

Yes. After holding the 491 for at least three years you can apply for the Permanent Residence (Skilled Regional) visa, subclass 191. You must have lived in a designated regional area for that time, complied with your visa conditions, and provide ATO notices of assessment for three of the five income years. You must apply before the 491 expires.

What does designated regional area mean for the 491?

For the 491, designated regional areas are all of Australia except Sydney, Melbourne and Brisbane. Cities such as Perth, Adelaide, Canberra, Hobart, Darwin, Newcastle, Wollongong, Geelong and the Gold Coast count as regional. Boundaries and postcodes are set by Home Affairs, so check your exact address on its list before you commit.

Can I live in Sydney, Melbourne or Brisbane on a 491 visa?

No. Condition 8579 requires you to live, work and study only in a designated regional area. Breaching it can lead to visa cancellation and loss of the pathway to the 191. Short visits for holidays or appointments are different from living or working there.

What is the difference between state-nominated and family-sponsored 491?

A state or territory government nominates you in the first case, using its own occupation list and criteria. In the second, an eligible relative who is an Australian citizen, permanent resident or eligible New Zealand citizen, lives in a designated regional area and is aged 18 or over sponsors you. Both add 15 points and both need an invitation through SkillSelect.

How long do I have to apply after I am invited?

You have 60 days from the date of the invitation to lodge your visa application. Prepare your documents before the invitation arrives, because the deadline cannot be extended.

What is the age limit for the 491 visa?

You must be under 45 when you are invited to apply.

Is the points test changing?

The Australian Government has announced changes to the permanent skilled points test, but as at 26 October 2026 no new points table is in force. The current Schedule 6D table and the 65-point pass mark still apply to the 491.

Can I include my partner and children?

Yes. Eligible family members can be included, each with their own visa application charge, and they must follow the same regional living condition. A partner can also add points if they are under 45 and have a suitable skills assessment and Competent English.
