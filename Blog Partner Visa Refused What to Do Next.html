Partner Visa Refused: What to Do Next

Quick Answer: If your partner visa is refused, read the refusal letter today. In many cases you have 28 days to ask the Administrative Review Tribunal (ART) to review the decision, or 14 days in immigration detention. The ART cannot extend this. Your other options are a new application, court review or ministerial intervention. The ART fee is A$3,727.

A refusal is hard news. You may feel shocked, angry or scared. That is normal.

But a refusal is not always the end. Many people have real options. The key is to move quickly and choose the right one.

This guide shows what a refusal means and why it happens. It then walks you through the deadline, your options, the costs and how to build a stronger case.

What Does It Mean When a Partner Visa Is Refused?

A refused partner visa means Home Affairs decided you did not meet the rules. You do not get the visa. The decision is made in writing, in a refusal letter.

That letter is the most important paper you will receive. It lists the reasons. It also says if you can ask for a review, and by when.

What Happens to Your Right to Stay?

Your next steps depend on the visa you hold. Check it in VEVO, the Home Affairs online checker.

  • If you hold a Bridging Visa A: it generally continues for a short time. If you apply to the ART, it generally runs until 35 days after the ART decides. If you do not, it runs until 35 days after the Home Affairs decision.
  • If you had a temporary partner visa and your permanent one is refused: Home Affairs says you no longer hold the temporary visa.
  • If you are outside Australia: you may not be able to enter or stay until you have a new visa.

Because time is short, check your status on the day you get the letter.

Refused Is Not the Same as Banned

A refusal alone does not ban you from applying again. But it can limit where and when you apply. Some refusals, such as those for false information, carry a ban. We explain this later.

What to Do in the First 48 Hours

  1. Read the whole refusal letter, twice.
  2. Write down the date you were notified.
  3. Check your visa status in VEVO.
  4. Find the deadline for review.
  5. Stop travel plans until you know your status.
  6. Speak with a MARA-registered agent or lawyer.

Why Partner Visas Get Refused

Most refusals come down to one question. Does the department believe your relationship is genuine and ongoing? The other grounds are about paperwork, sponsors, health and character.

How Many Partner Visas Get Refused?

There is no single reliable, current official refusal rate for partner visas. Rates vary by visa type, evidence and who lodges.

Older department data showed that applications lodged without a migration professional were refused far more often. That data is historical and does not predict your case.

The honest answer is this. Refusal rates vary by visa type, country, evidence and who lodges. Home Affairs publishes program data, so check it for the latest numbers.

Why Would a Spouse Visa Be Refused?

These are the most common grounds:

  • Relationship not accepted: the evidence does not show a genuine, continuing relationship.
  • De facto relationship under 12 months: unless an exception applies.
  • No substantive visa: this can bring extra Schedule 3 rules.
  • Sponsor not approved: the sponsor has limits, or a character issue.
  • False or misleading information: this can lead to a ban, known as PIC 4020.
  • Health or character not met.
  • Interview problems: answers that do not match the written case.

Warning Signs in a Weak File

Case officers look for gaps. These are common ones:

  • Dates that do not match between you and your partner.
  • Only photos and chat messages, with no shared money or home.
  • Living apart with no proof of visits or contact.
  • A past marriage or relationship that was not disclosed.
  • No update after a big change, such as a new job or address.

If you see your own case here, fix it before you choose your next step.

Partner Visa Refused: The Deadline You Cannot Miss

Time is the one thing you cannot get back. Read this section first.

How Long Do You Have to Ask for a Review?

You must apply to the ART within 28 days of being notified. If you are in immigration detention, it is 14 days. The ART cannot extend these dates.

Check your own letter, because it states your deadline.

When Does the Clock Start?

The clock starts when you are notified, not always on the date printed on the letter. These rules generally apply:

  • An email counts as received at the end of the day it was sent.
  • A posted letter in Australia counts as received 7 working days after posting.
  • A posted letter overseas counts as received 21 days after posting.

Do not wait to find out. Count from the earliest possible date.

Who Lodges the Review?

  • After a refused onshore partner visa (subclass 820): you lodge the review yourself, while you are in Australia.
  • After a refused offshore partner visa (subclass 309): you, the applicant, lodge it. You can be inside or outside Australia. Your sponsor can help represent you.

Your refusal letter says whether you have review rights. Some refusals, such as certain character decisions, follow a different path.

Option 1: Ask the ART to Review the Decision

The ART is independent of Home Affairs. It looks at your case again, with fresh eyes. It can consider new evidence up to the hearing.

The ART can affirm the refusal, vary it, set it aside or send it back. Many reviews go to a hearing, but not all. Partner reviews take a long time, so keep your evidence updated while you wait.

Option 2: Make a New Partner Visa Application

This can be faster, but it is not always allowed. Two rules matter.

Section 48 (onshore): if you hold no substantive visa and your partner visa was refused since you last entered Australia, a new onshore partner application is not valid. If you still hold another substantive visa, the bar does not apply.

Offshore (subclass 309): you can apply from outside Australia. But a refusal for false information can bar you for 3 years (10 years where identity was the issue), unless a waiver applies. After a removal, you may need to wait 12 months.

A new application should fix the weak points. Do not send the same file again.

Option 3: Go to Court (After the ART Decision)

If the ART affirms the refusal, you may apply to the Federal Circuit and Family Court within 35 days. The court only looks for legal error. It does not decide if you should get the visa.

Option 4: Ask for Ministerial Intervention

The Minister may replace a decision if it is in the public interest. It is personal and discretionary. You have no right to request it, so treat it as a last resort.

Is It Possible to Get a Visa After Refusal?

Yes, it can be. You may succeed through a review, a new application or another visa that fits your life. The path depends on the reasons for refusal and your visa status.

How Long Will It Take?

No one can promise a date. Home Affairs does not give a fixed time for reviews. Partner reviews can take a long time, and times change.

For new applications, Home Affairs says onshore partner visas (820 and 801) get the highest priority. Offshore ones (309, 100 and 300) get lower priority and are likely to take longer.

So a new application is not always faster than a review. Ask your agent which route fits.

Your Status While You Wait

If you hold a Bridging Visa A and apply to the ART, it generally continues until 35 days after the ART decides. Check your conditions in VEVO, including your work rights.

If you want to leave Australia, you may need a Bridging Visa B first. Leaving without one can end your bridging visa. Get advice before you book a flight.

What If the Relationship Changes During the Review?

Sometimes a relationship ends while a review is pending. In certain cases, such as family violence, a case may still continue. Report big changes the right way, and speak with an agent quickly.

How to Build a Stronger Case After a Partner Visa Refusal

A stronger case answers each reason in the refusal letter. It does not repeat the same documents.

Step-by-Step: Rebuild Your Evidence

  1. List every refusal reason. Write them one by one.
  2. Match each reason to new evidence. For example, add joint bills if the problem was shared finances.
  3. Fix inconsistent dates. Make sure every form and statement agrees.
  4. Explain any gaps. Add notes for long periods apart, with proof of visits and contact.
  5. Add fresh statements. Use statutory declarations (Form 888) from people who know you as a couple.
  6. Translate records. Have non-English chats and documents translated by a certified translator.
  7. Prepare for questions. Review your story with your partner so answers match.

Four Types of Evidence to Collect

  • Financial: joint accounts, shared bills, insurance.
  • Household: shared lease, property, photos of daily life.
  • Social: declarations from friends and family, photos at events.
  • Commitment: call and message history, and joint future plans.

Mistakes to Avoid After a Refusal

  • Missing the deadline. A late review is invalid.
  • Resubmitting the same file. It will likely fail again.
  • Leaving Australia without advice. You could lose rights.
  • Hiding a past issue. False information can lead to a ban.
  • Waiting to see what happens. Options shrink with time.
  • Using out-of-date advice. Names, fees and deadlines have changed since 2023 and 2024.

Frequently Asked Questions About a Refused Partner Visa

How Many Partner Visas Get Refused?

There is no single reliable, current official rate. Rates vary by visa type, evidence and who lodges. Check the latest Home Affairs program data.

What Does It Mean When a Visa Is Refused?

It means Home Affairs decided you did not meet the rules. You do not get the visa. Your refusal letter lists the reasons and says if you can seek review and by when.

Why Would a Spouse Visa Be Refused?

The most common reason is that the relationship is not accepted as genuine and continuing. Other reasons include a de facto relationship under 12 months, sponsor problems, false information, health or character issues, and interview answers that do not match.

Is It Possible to Get a Visa After Refusal?

Yes, it can be. You may succeed through an ART review, a new application or another visa. The right path depends on your refusal reasons and your visa status, so seek advice quickly.

How Long Do I Have to Appeal a Partner Visa Refusal?

You have 28 days from notification to apply to the ART, or 14 days in immigration detention. The ART cannot extend this. Your refusal letter states your own deadline.

How Much Does an ART Review Cost?

The fee is A$3,727 since 1 July 2026, with a 50% reduction if hardship is approved. Half the fee is refunded if the decision is overturned or sent back. Confirm with the ART before you pay.

Can I Stay in Australia While the ART Reviews My Case?

If you hold a Bridging Visa A and apply to the ART, it generally continues until 35 days after the ART decides. Check your conditions in VEVO.

Can I Reapply Right Away After a Refusal?

Sometimes. Section 48 can block a new onshore partner application if you hold no substantive visa. An offshore application may be possible, but a refusal for false information can bring a ban.

Can I Leave Australia While My Review Is Pending?

Be careful. You may need a Bridging Visa B first, and leaving without it can end your bridging visa. Get advice before booking a flight.

Does My Sponsor Need to Do Anything After a Refusal?

For an offshore refusal, the applicant lodges the review and the sponsor can help represent them. A sponsor can also help by giving a statement and new evidence.

In Short

A refused partner visa is serious, but you may still have options. Read your letter today and note your deadline. You usually have 28 days to ask the ART for review, and the fee is A$3,727.

Check your status in VEVO and gather evidence for each refusal reason. Do not travel or reapply until you understand section 48. Speak with a MARA-registered agent before you decide.
