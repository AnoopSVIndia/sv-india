Sponsored Family Stream Guide

Sponsored Family Stream (Subclass 600): Visiting Relatives in Australia

If an eligible relative in Australia is willing to formally sponsor your visit, the sponsored family stream may suit you better than the general tourist stream. This page explains who can sponsor you, what the security bond actually is, and how the evidence differs from a standard tourist application.

The Sponsor

Who Can Sponsor Your Visit?

Your sponsor must be an Australian citizen, an Australian permanent resident, or an eligible New Zealand citizen living in Australia, and must fall within an accepted relationship category.

  • A partner, parent, child, sibling, grandparent or grandchild of the applicant.
  • An aunt, uncle, niece or nephew of the applicant.
  • A step-equivalent of any of the above relationships.
  • The sponsor must be at least 18, generally settled in Australia, and expected to meet reasonable character requirements.
  • If a relative would rather not take on formal sponsor obligations, the tourist stream with an informal invitation letter may be a better fit instead.
What Is a Security Bond?

Understanding the Security Bond

This is one of the most commonly misunderstood parts of the sponsored family stream, so it is worth explaining clearly.

A security bond is an amount of money the Department can ask the sponsor to pay as assurance that the visitor will comply with their visa conditions and leave Australia on time. It is refunded if the conditions are met. Importantly, a bond is not charged automatically for every application. The Department decides whether to request one based on the individual circumstances of the case, which can include the visitor's travel history, ties to their home country, and the sponsor's own history. If a bond is requested, we will explain exactly how much is required and how it can be paid before you commit to anything.

Get Organised

Document Checklist for Both Sides

Because two people are effectively assessed on this stream, both the applicant and the sponsor need to prepare evidence.

  • Evidence of the sponsor's Australian citizenship or permanent residency, such as a passport or visa grant.
  • Proof of the family relationship, such as birth certificates, marriage certificates, or other official records.
  • A signed sponsorship form from the sponsor, including their undertaking to support the visit.
  • Evidence of the sponsor's own financial capacity, if they intend to support the visitor's costs.
  • The applicant's own passport, financial evidence, and any evidence of ties to their home country.
  • A short letter from the sponsor describing the purpose and expected length of the visit.
  • Health and character documents, if requested for the applicant's circumstances.

Common Questions

Sponsored Family Stream FAQs

Can one sponsor support more than one visitor at a time?

Generally each visitor lodges their own application with their own sponsorship form, though one sponsor can sponsor different relatives over time, subject to the Department's assessment of each case.

What happens if the sponsor changes their mind after lodgement?

Sponsorship is a genuine commitment. If a sponsor withdraws support, it can affect the outcome of the application, so this should be discussed thoroughly with your relative before lodging.

Can the sponsored visitor work while in Australia?

No. Like the other visitor visa streams, this visa does not permit ongoing paid work in Australia.

Is the security bond the same amount for everyone?

No. If requested, the amount depends on the Department's assessment of the individual application, and is not a fixed figure applied to every sponsored family stream case.

Can I use the tourist stream instead if my relative would rather not sponsor me formally?

Yes. Many family visits are lodged under the tourist stream with an informal invitation letter, rather than formal sponsorship, and this can be a simpler option for some families.
