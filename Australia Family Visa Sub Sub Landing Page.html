Family Migration Guide

Family Visa Australia: Partner, Child, Parent and Relative Visas Explained

Australia's Family stream lets citizens, permanent residents and eligible New Zealand citizens bring close family members to live in Australia. Some family visas are processed in months, while others sit in a queue for decades, so choosing the right one matters. This guide explains each family visa, what is capped, and what you can realistically expect.

The Basics

What Is a Family Visa in Australia?

The Family stream is one part of the permanent Migration Program, run by the Department of Home Affairs.

A family visa is a visa that lets a person live in Australia because of their relationship to an Australian citizen, permanent resident or eligible New Zealand citizen. It is not points tested and does not need a skills assessment. Instead, it depends on the relationship, the sponsor's status, and the criteria set for each category. Most family visas also need health and character checks, and some need an Assurance of Support.

The big difference between categories is how they are managed. Partner and Child visas are demand-driven, so eligible applications are assessed without a fixed annual cap. Parent and Other Family visas are capped and queued, so a valid application receives a queue date and waits for a place in a future program year.

Partners

Partner Visas: The Largest Part of the Family Stream

Most family visas granted each year are Partner visas.

The Partner visa pathway is for the spouse or de facto partner of an Australian citizen, permanent resident or eligible New Zealand citizen. Applicants in Australia apply for the subclass 820 and then the permanent 801, in a single application. Applicants overseas apply for the 309 and then the 100. Engaged applicants overseas can apply for the Prospective Marriage visa (subclass 300). The main applicant charge for the 820/801 is AUD 11,710 from 1 July 2026.

Because partner visas have their own evidence rules, sponsor limits and two stages, we cover them in full on a separate page.

The Balance of Family Test

Most parent visas require your family to pass the Balance of Family test. Generally, at least half of your parent's children must be Australian citizens or permanent residents living in Australia, or more of their children must live in Australia than in any other single country. All children count, including step-children and adopted children, and the test can be checked again at decision. The temporary 870 does not use this test.

Assurance of Support

Most parent visas require an Assurance of Support, usually for ten years. It is a financial guarantee that your parent will not rely on certain social security payments, and it is secured by a bond. Contributory parent visas also carry a second instalment of the visa charge, which runs to tens of thousands of dollars per applicant. Confirm the current amounts before you plan.

Be realistic about timing. The estimated waits are Home Affairs figures based on current annual places. They are not a forecast for any one application and can change, and the 2026-27 Parent planning level was cut. Many families plan around a temporary option while a permanent application waits.

Processing Order

How Family Visas Are Prioritised for Processing

Ministerial Directions decide which family applications are assessed first.

The Department of Home Affairs processes family visas in an order set by Ministerial Direction, and it publishes the current priorities on its website. A processing direction that came into effect in July 2026 is reported to place applicants who are in Australia, and who can be granted a visa in Australia, ahead of applicants who are offshore. Within each group, Partner and Dependent Child applications rank ahead of the other family categories.

Priority only changes processing order. It does not change who is eligible, and it does not remove the caps and queues on Parent and Other Family visas. Because directions are updated from time to time, we check the current one before we advise you on lodging onshore or offshore.

Get Organised

Documents Most Family Visa Applications Need

The exact list depends on the visa, but most files include these.

  • Passports and identity documents for every applicant.
  • Evidence of your sponsor's citizenship, permanent residence or New Zealand status.
  • Birth certificates and family records that prove the relationship.
  • Marriage certificates, or evidence of a de facto relationship, for partner applications.
  • For parent visas, family composition details for the Balance of Family test.
  • Evidence of dependency, such as financial support records, for aged dependent or child applications.
  • Medical evidence for carer applications, from the relative's treating doctor.
  • Police certificates for each country where you have lived for 12 months or more in the last 10 years.
  • Health examination results where required.
  • Financial documents for the sponsor and any Assurance of Support assurer.

Family Visa Costs: What to Expect

Charges vary a lot between categories, and some are paid in two instalments.

Family visa costs in brief
Visa groupWhat to know
Partner (820/801, 309/100)AUD 11,710 for the main applicant from 1 July 2026, AUD 5,860 for each additional applicant aged 18 or over and AUD 2,935 for each applicant under 18. The charge covers both stages.
Child, Carer, Remaining Relative, Aged Dependent Relative, Orphan RelativeEach has its own charge. Check the amount for your subclass on the Department of Home Affairs visa pricing estimator before you plan.
Parent (103, 804)A lower charge than contributory visas, but a very long queue.
Contributory Parent (143, 173, 864, 884)A first instalment at lodgement and a second instalment, which runs to tens of thousands of dollars per applicant, paid before grant.

Beyond the visa charge. Plan for health examinations, police certificates, translations, any Assurance of Support bond and professional fees. A bond for an Assurance of Support has been about AUD 10,000 for one person and AUD 14,000 for a couple at last review.

Charges are set by the Australian Government and normally change each 1 July. Confirm every amount in ImmiAccount or on the Department of Home Affairs pricing tool before you pay. Application charges are generally not refunded if an application is refused.

Avoid These

Common Family Visa Mistakes

Underestimating the Queue

Parent and Other Family visas are measured in decades. Plan how your family will live and travel in the meantime.

Skipping the Balance of Family Test

A parent application can fail if your family does not pass it. Check before paying any instalment.

Assuming Temporary Means Permanent

The Sponsored Parent (870) visa and visitor visas do not lead to permanent residence.

Ignoring Sponsor Eligibility

Sponsorship rules and limits can stop an application. Check your own status and history early.

Weak or Mismatched Evidence

Dates, names and relationships that do not match across documents raise doubts. Check everything lines up.

Relying on Outdated Guides

Fees, planning levels and queue estimates change each year. Use current Home Affairs figures.

Common Questions

Family Visa FAQs

What counts as a family visa in Australia?

Family visas are the permanent and temporary visas in the Family stream of the Migration Program. They cover partners, dependent children, parents, carers, remaining relatives, aged dependent relatives and orphan relatives. Each category has its own sponsor rules, and some are capped and queued.

How many family visa places are there in 2026-27?

The Family stream has a planning level of 52,460 places for 2026-27. Within that, the planning levels are about 41,500 for Partner, 7,060 for Parent, 3,500 for Child and 400 for Other Family. Partner and Child visas are demand-driven, so their levels are indicative, while Parent and Other Family places are capped.

How long does a parent visa take?

Parent visas are capped and queued, so waits are very long. Home Affairs estimates, as at 31 August 2026, about 18 years for new Contributory Parent applications and about 42 years for new Parent and Aged Parent applications. These are estimates based on current annual places, not a forecast for any one application.

Is there a faster way to have my parents with me in Australia?

The Sponsored Parent (Temporary) visa, subclass 870, is a temporary option for up to three or five years at a time, with a maximum of ten years in total. It does not lead to permanent residence, and the sponsor must be approved first. Some families use it while a permanent parent application waits in the queue.

What is the Balance of Family test?

It is a test for most parent visas. Generally, at least half of the parent's children must be Australian citizens or permanent residents living in Australia, or more of the children must live in Australia than in any other single country. All of the parent's children count, and the test can be checked again at decision.

How long do Carer, Remaining Relative and Aged Dependent Relative visas take?

These visas are capped and queued. Home Affairs estimates, as at 31 August 2026, about 16 years for new Carer applications and about 26 years for new Remaining Relative and Aged Dependent Relative applications.

Can my child get a visa to live in Australia with me?

If you are an Australian citizen, permanent resident or eligible New Zealand citizen, your dependent child may be eligible for a Child visa, or an Adoption visa if the child is adopted. Age and dependency rules apply. If you hold a temporary partner visa, a child can usually be included in the permanent partner stage instead.

Are partner visas part of the family visa stream?

Yes. Partner visas are the largest part of the Family stream, with a planning level of 41,500 places for 2026-27. We cover them in detail on our Partner Visa (820/801) page.
