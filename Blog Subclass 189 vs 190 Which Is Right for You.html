Subclass 189 vs 190: Which Is Right for You?

Quick answer: The subclass 189 visa needs no nomination. The subclass 190 visa needs a state or territory government to nominate you first. That nomination adds 5 points to your score. Both visas give you permanent residency from the day they are granted.

Choosing between the two is a big step in your Australian PR plan. The visas look alike. But the road to each one is very different.

This guide explains the difference in plain words. You will see points, cost, speed, occupation lists and state rules. Then you will see which type of person suits which visa.

Figures in this guide are current as of 29 October 2026. Rules, fees and state programs change often. So always check the Department of Home Affairs before you act.

What Is the Subclass 189 Visa?

The subclass 189 is the Skilled Independent visa. It is a permanent visa. You do not need an employer, a state or a family member to sponsor you.

You start by sending an Expression of Interest (EOI) through SkillSelect. This is a free online profile. It tells Home Affairs about your age, English, work and study.

Home Affairs then ranks all EOIs by points. It runs invitation rounds and invites the top-ranked people. If you get an invitation, you have 60 days to lodge your visa application.

Once granted, you can:

  • Live, work and study anywhere in Australia
  • Enrol in Medicare
  • Sponsor eligible relatives for permanent residency
  • Apply for citizenship later, if you meet the rules
  • Travel in and out of Australia for five years from the grant date

After five years, you need a Resident Return visa or another visa to re-enter. This is a travel rule only. Your permanent residency does not end.

What Is the Subclass 190 Visa?

The subclass 190 is the Skilled Nominated visa. It is also a permanent visa. The big difference is that a state or territory must nominate you first.

Each state and territory runs its own nomination program. It sets its own occupation list and its own rules. It picks people whose skills it needs.

After a state nominates you, Home Affairs invites you to apply. The nomination adds 5 points to your score, as long as the state does not withdraw it. The PR rights are the same as the 189.

Here is a simple way to see it. With the 189, Home Affairs picks you from a national pool. With the 190, a state picks you first.

1. Nomination: Who Picks You?

With the 189, no one nominates you. You compete on points alone. Home Affairs ranks every EOI and invites the highest scores for each occupation.

With the 190, a state or territory must nominate you first. Each state sets its own rules. It looks at your occupation, work history and plans to live there.

State nomination is not a given. States open and close their programs during the year. They also set caps on how many people they can nominate.

2. Points: How Much Does Nomination Help?

Both visas use the same points test. Points come from age, English, skilled work, study and partner skills. The only change is nomination. The 190 adds 5 points.

Here is an example. Take a 30-year-old with Proficient English, a bachelor degree, three years of overseas skilled work and no partner.

Example points score
Age 3030 points
Proficient English10 points
Bachelor degree15 points
Three years of overseas skilled work5 points
Single10 points
Total70 points (75 with a 190 nomination)

That is 70 points. Under the 189, the score stays 70. Under the 190, it becomes 75.

You must be able to prove every point you claim. So do not guess your score. Check each item against the Home Affairs points tables.

3. Is 65 Points Enough?

Sixty-five is the lowest score you can have to be invited. But it rarely wins an invitation on its own. In the 4 June 2026 round, the lowest invited scores were:

  • 65 points for bricklayers, carpenters and plumbers
  • 75 points for aged care registered nurses and secondary school teachers
  • 85 points for architects
  • 90 points for actuaries
  • 95 points for biotechnologists

So the score you need depends on your job. A round that suits one occupation may not suit another. For the 190, each state also sets its own selection rules. Your score is just one factor.

4. Occupation Lists

Your job must be on the right skilled occupation list. For the 189, you need an occupation on the list that applies to the 189. Many guides call this the Medium and Long-term Strategic Skills List (MLTSSL).

For the 190, your job must be on the nominating state's list. States can draw from a wider set of occupations. So a job that is not eligible for the 189 may still work for a 190 in one state.

Lists are updated from time to time. Always check your occupation on the Home Affairs website before you plan. If your work experience is strong but your qualification is not, Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL) may help with your skills assessment.

5. Where You Must Live

With the 189, you can live and work anywhere. There is no promise to any state.

With the 190, the rule is more subtle. Home Affairs does not list a visa condition that limits where you live. But each state asks you to commit to living and working there when it nominates you. Many states ask for around two years.

States take this commitment seriously. So read your state's terms before you accept a nomination. Only accept if you truly plan to move there.

Subclass 189 vs 190: Cost and Processing Time

The government charge is almost the same for both visas. As of October 2026, the main applicant charge starts from about AUD 6,135 for the 189 and AUD 6,140 for the 190. Extra charges apply for each family member.

These charges are indexed, so they can rise each year. Use the Home Affairs visa pricing estimator to get your exact total. Also plan for other costs: skills assessment, English test, health checks and police certificates.

Some states also charge a fee for their nomination service. For example, Victoria has said fees for service apply from 2026-27. So add that to your 190 budget.

Which One Is Faster?

After you lodge, the 189 is currently faster. Home Affairs data reported on 8 October 2026 shows:

  • 189: 50% of applications finished in 61 days, and 90% in 8 months
  • 190: 50% of applications finished in 4 months, and 90% in 16 months

But this is only half the story. Your total wait has two parts. First, you wait for an invitation, and for the 190 also a state nomination. Second, Home Affairs processes your application.

The Home Affairs timeframes cover only the second part. So a fast processing time does not always mean a fast pathway.

Your priority group also matters. Home Affairs processes applications in an order set by Ministerial Direction No. 122. Applicants in priority sectors such as construction, healthcare and teaching are handled first. Applicants who are in Australia when they lodge also get priority over most offshore applicants.

What Is Happening with Invitations in 2026-27?

The 189 depends on invitation rounds, and these are not fixed. Home Affairs held a 189 round on 4 June 2026. It invited 10,000 EOIs. Then no 189 invitations were issued for July, August and September 2026.

Home Affairs held the first 189 round of the 2026-27 program year on 15 October 2026. Official results by occupation may take time to appear, so check the SkillSelect invitation rounds page.

State programs are also at different stages. As of mid-October 2026, reports show:

  • ACT held a round on 12 October 2026 that invited people for the 190
  • The Northern Territory was not taking new 190 applications for 2026-27
  • New South Wales was handling 190 nominations by invitation only, with no round dates set
  • Victoria had not yet opened its 2026-27 program
  • Queensland and South Australia said their 2026-27 settings would follow

These details change fast. Check each state's website for the latest.

What About the Subclass 491 Visa?

Many people compare the 189 and 190 with a third option, the 491. It is the Skilled Work Regional (Provisional) visa. It is not a permanent visa.

The 491 lasts five years. You must live, work and study in a designated regional area. A state nomination or a relative's sponsorship adds 15 points.

After three years, you may apply for permanent residency through the subclass 191 visa. If you already hold a 491, you need to have held it for at least three years before you can apply for a 189 or 190.

The 491 may help if your score is too low for the 189 or 190. But it asks for more time and a regional move. Read our subclass 491 visa guide if this sounds like you.

Subclass 189 vs 190: Which Should You Choose?

Neither visa is better for everyone. The right one depends on your points, your job and how flexible you are. Use the lists below as a guide.

The 189 May Suit You If:

  • Your occupation is eligible for the 189
  • Your score is high enough to compete in invitation rounds
  • You want to live anywhere in Australia with no state promise
  • You are happy to wait for rounds that Home Affairs runs on its own timing

The 190 May Suit You If:

  • You need the 5 nomination points to become competitive
  • You are happy to live and work in the nominating state
  • Your job is not eligible for the 189 but is on a state list

Three Simple Examples

These are general examples only. They are not advice for your case.

  • High score, flexible plans: A software engineer with a very high score and an eligible occupation may aim for the 189. This gives full freedom to live anywhere.
  • Mid score, in-demand job: A nurse with a moderate score may find a state wants her skills. The 5 extra points from a 190 nomination could help.
  • Low score, open to regional life: A person whose score is too low for both may look at the 491. It adds 15 points but is a provisional visa.

Is the 189 Harder to Get than the 190?

Often, yes, but not always. The 189 is a national contest. Scores for some jobs run very high. And rounds can pause for months, as we saw from June to October 2026.

The 190 is also not easy. You must win a state nomination first, and states open and close programs. For some jobs, a state is the easier door. For others, the 189 is.

Can You Apply for Both the 189 and the 190?

Yes. You can keep one EOI active for both visas. Choose the 189 and also tick the states you are interested in for nomination.

If you receive a 189 invitation and a 190 nomination, you can choose which one to accept. Many people do this. It keeps more doors open while you wait.

How to Decide in Five Simple Steps

  1. Check your occupation. See if it is eligible for the 189 and which states list it for the 190.
  2. Get a skills assessment. Most applicants need a positive result from the right assessing authority. See how RPL can support it.
  3. Work out your real points. Count only what you can prove with documents.
  4. Compare your score with recent rounds. Look at the cut-off for your occupation, not the 65-point minimum.
  5. Decide where you can live. If a state nominates you, be ready to keep your promise.

Then lodge your EOI and keep it up to date. Update it when your English score, work years or age points change.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Claiming points you cannot prove. Home Affairs checks every claim.
  • Waiting only for the 189. Rounds can pause. Look at state options in parallel.
  • Accepting a nomination you do not want. The state expects you to live there.
  • Ignoring your age. If you turn 45 before an invitation, you will not be invited.
  • Missing the 60-day deadline. Once invited, the lodgement date cannot be extended.
  • Using old information. Fees, lists and state programs change each year.

Subclass 189 vs 190: Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a 189 and 190 visa?

The 189 needs no nomination. The 190 needs a state or territory to nominate you. Nomination adds 5 points. Both visas are permanent.

Is the 189 harder than the 190?

Often the 189 is harder, because you compete with everyone in the national pool and scores for some jobs run high. But the 190 needs a state to pick you first, and states open and close programs. Which is harder depends on your job, your score and your state options.

Is a 189 visa faster than a 190?

After you lodge, yes. Home Affairs data reported on 8 October 2026 shows the 189 at 61 days for half of applications. The 190 was at 4 months. But the 189 waits for invitation rounds, and none ran from July to September 2026. So the full journey can take longer than the processing time suggests.

Should I apply for 189 or 190?

It depends on your points, your occupation and where you can live. The 189 suits people with competitive scores who want freedom to live anywhere. The 190 suits people whose job a state wants and who are happy to settle there. A MARA-registered agent can compare both for your profile.

Can I apply for both the 189 and 190 at the same time?

Yes. One EOI can be active for both. If you get a 189 invitation and a 190 nomination, you can choose which to accept.

Do I have to live in the nominating state on a 190 visa?

Home Affairs does not list a visa condition that limits where you live on the 190. But each state asks you to commit to living there when it nominates you. Many states ask for around two years. Read your state's terms before you accept.

How many points do I need for the 189 or 190?

The lowest score to be invited is 65 points for both. In practice, invitations often go to higher scores. The needed score depends on your occupation and, for the 190, on the state.

Does the 190 give permanent residency?

Yes. The 190 is a permanent visa. You become a permanent resident when it is granted. But a state nomination alone does not guarantee a visa. You must still meet all Home Affairs rules.

I hold a 491 visa. Can I move to a 189 or 190?

Yes, but not straight away. You must have held the 491 for at least three years when you apply. The same rule applies to the 494 visa.

Should I wait for a 189 invitation or accept a 190 nomination?

This is a common question on forums. There is no one answer. It depends on your score, how long you can wait, and whether you truly want to live in that state. Weigh your timing and plans with a registered agent before you decide.

In Short

The 189 gives you freedom but asks for a high score and patience for invitation rounds. The 190 adds 5 points but asks you to commit to a state. Both lead to permanent residency.

Your best choice depends on your own points, your job and your plans. Check the latest Home Affairs and state information before you decide.
