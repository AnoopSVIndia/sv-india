Working Visa (Subclass 482 and 186): How Employer Sponsorship Leads to Permanent Residence

The Skills in Demand visa (subclass 482) lets an approved Australian employer sponsor a skilled worker for up to four years. After a period of work, the Employer Nomination Scheme (subclass 186) can turn that job into permanent residence. This guide explains both visas, the salary thresholds, what employers must do and how the two steps connect.

The Basics

What Are the 482 and 186 Working Visas?

Two employer sponsored visas that work as a two-step pathway: temporary first, then permanent.

The Skills in Demand visa (subclass 482) is a temporary work visa. It replaced the Temporary Skill Shortage (TSS) visa on 7 December 2024 and kept the 482 number. An Australian employer that is an approved sponsor nominates a skilled position and you apply to fill it. You work for that sponsor in the nominated occupation, and the visa can last up to four years.

The Employer Nomination Scheme (subclass 186) is a permanent visa. An employer nominates you, and there are separate streams for people who are already working for that employer on a 482 and for people who are not. Together, the two visas form a common pathway: work on a 482 and, once you meet the requirements, move to permanent residence through the 186.

Eligibility

482 Visa Eligibility: What You Need as the Worker

Your employer must qualify, and so must you.

  • Sponsor and nomination: an approved employer must nominate you for a genuine position in an eligible occupation.
  • Work experience: generally at least one year of relevant experience at the required skill level, within the last five years.
  • Skills and licences: any skills assessment, licence or registration your occupation requires, for example for trades, engineering or health roles.
  • English: the minimum English level set by the Department for your stream and occupation, shown through an approved test or an accepted passport.
  • Salary: an offered salary at or above the threshold for your stream and the market salary rate.
  • Health and character: meet the Australian health requirement and provide police certificates for the character requirement.
  • Work for your sponsor: work in the nominated occupation for the sponsor, unless a new sponsor lodges a new nomination for you.

The 482 has no upper age limit. Requirements differ by occupation and stream, so we check your own occupation, experience and any licensing before an employer invests in nominating you.

For Employers

What Employers Must Do to Sponsor a 482 Worker

Sponsorship comes with legal duties, and the employer carries most of the cost.

Main employer steps and obligations
Step or dutyWhat it involves
Become an approved sponsorA standard business sponsor approval generally lasts five years, and the sponsorship application has its own fee.
Nominate a genuine positionThe employer nominates the role and occupation, shows a genuine need and meets the salary rules.
Pay the right salaryThe salary must be at least the income threshold for the stream and at least the market salary rate, which is what an equivalent Australian worker would earn in the same job and location.
Labour market testingFor the Specialist Skills and Core Skills streams, employers generally need to advertise the role, usually for at least 28 days on at least two national platforms, unless an exemption applies.
Pay the SAF levyThe Skilling Australians Fund levy is paid by the employer up front. It is AUD 1,200 per year of visa duration for businesses with turnover under AUD 10 million and AUD 1,800 per year for others.
Meet sponsor obligationsKeep records, tell the Department about changes such as the worker leaving, and not recover sponsorship costs from the worker.

The employer pays the nomination and sponsorship fees and the SAF levy, and these costs cannot be passed on to the worker. Breaching sponsor obligations can lead to sanctions on the business.

Employer costs. The employer pays the sponsorship application, the nomination fee and the Skilling Australians Fund levy. The 482 levy is AUD 1,200 per year of visa duration for businesses with turnover under AUD 10 million and AUD 1,800 per year for others. The 186 levy is higher, at AUD 3,000 for businesses with turnover under AUD 10 million and AUD 5,000 for others. Fees for nomination and sponsorship are separate charges and also change over time.

Plan separately for health examinations, police certificates, English tests, skills assessments and professional fees. Charges are set by the Australian Government and normally change each 1 July. Confirm the current amounts in ImmiAccount or on the Department of Home Affairs visa pricing tool before you pay. The visa application charge is generally not refunded if an application is refused.

On the Visa

Changing Employer, Conditions and Your Family on a 482

What happens if your job or circumstances change.

A 482 is tied to your sponsor and your nominated occupation. If you stop working for your sponsor, you generally have up to 180 days at a time to find a new approved sponsor who lodges a nomination for you, apply for another visa or leave Australia, with a cap of 365 days in total. Working for someone other than your sponsor in the meantime can breach your conditions.

Your partner and dependent children can be included in your application, and the fee for each is separate. Because rules for family work and study rights, sponsor changes and the 186 timeline are detailed, we plan them with you before you accept a job offer.

Fit Check

Is a Working Visa Right for You?

Employer sponsorship tends to suit you if

  • You have a job offer, or a realistic route to one, from an employer who can sponsor.
  • Your occupation is on the CSOL, or your salary reaches the Specialist Skills threshold.
  • You have the experience, licences and English the occupation needs.
  • You want to work first and plan permanent residence through the 186 later.

Consider another route if

  • You have no employer willing to sponsor you.
  • Your points are strong enough for a skilled visa such as the 189.
  • Your salary would fall below the threshold for your stream.
  • You want to stay flexible between employers without lodging new nominations.
Avoid These

Common Working Visa Mistakes That Cost Workers and Employers Time

Starting before the visa is granted

Work for the sponsor only once you hold a visa that allows it, and in the nominated occupation.

Salary that falls short

The offer must meet both the income threshold and the market salary rate. The higher figure applies.

Skipping labour market testing

Where it is required, a missing or weak advertising record can sink the nomination.

Choosing the wrong occupation

An occupation that does not match your real duties or the employer's role invites refusal.

Losing track of the 186 timeline

The Temporary Residence Transition stream needs a period of work with the nominating employer. Plan your dates from the start.

Sponsor non-compliance

Employers who fail their obligations, or ask workers to pay sponsorship costs, risk sanctions and damage workers' pathways.

Common Questions

Working Visa (482/186) FAQs

What is the difference between the 482 and the 186 visa?

The Skills in Demand visa (subclass 482) is a temporary work visa, valid for up to four years, that lets an approved employer sponsor you for a skilled job. The Employer Nomination Scheme (subclass 186) is a permanent visa that an employer nominates you for. Many people move from a 482 to a 186 through the Temporary Residence Transition stream.

What are the streams of the Skills in Demand 482 visa?

There are three working streams: Core Skills, for occupations on the Core Skills Occupation List with a salary at or above the Core Skills Income Threshold; Specialist Skills, for higher-paid roles with a salary at or above the Specialist Skills Income Threshold and no occupation list; and the Labour Agreement stream. An Essential Skills stream has been announced but is not yet operating.

What salary does the employer have to pay?

From 1 July 2026 the Core Skills Income Threshold is AUD 79,423 and the Specialist Skills Income Threshold is AUD 146,576. The employer must also pay at least the Annual Market Salary Rate, which is what an equivalent Australian worker in the same job and location would earn, so the higher of the two figures applies.

How much work experience do I need for the 482?

Generally at least one year of relevant work experience at the required skill level, within the last five years, plus any skills assessment, licence or registration your occupation needs. The employer must also be an approved sponsor and nominate a genuine position.

How long can I stay on a 482, and can I change employer?

The visa can last up to four years. If you stop working for your sponsor, you generally have up to 180 days at a time to find a new sponsor, leave Australia or apply for another visa, with a cap of 365 days in total. The new employer must be an approved sponsor and lodge a new nomination.

How does a 482 lead to permanent residence?

Through the Employer Nomination Scheme (subclass 186). The Temporary Residence Transition stream generally needs two years of full-time work with the nominating employer in the same occupation, and no new skills assessment. The Direct Entry stream is for people who have not transitioned from a 482 with that employer, and needs a positive skills assessment and about three years of relevant experience.

Is there an age limit for the 482 and the 186?

There is no upper age limit for the 482. The 186 generally requires you to be under 45 when you apply, with limited exemptions, for example for some academics and for some high-earning 482 holders.

What costs does the employer pay?

The employer pays the sponsorship and nomination fees and the Skilling Australians Fund (SAF) levy, and these costs cannot be passed on to you. You pay the visa application charge for you and your family, plus your own checks such as health examinations and police certificates.
