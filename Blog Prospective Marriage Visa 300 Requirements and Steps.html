Prospective Marriage Visa 300: Requirements and Steps

Quick Answer: The prospective marriage visa (subclass 300) lets you come to Australia to marry your Australian fiancé or fiancée. You apply from outside Australia and must marry while the visa is in effect. The fee is A$11,710 from 1 July 2026. The wait is about 18 to 31 months. After the wedding, you apply for a partner visa.

You may be engaged to someone who lives in Australia. You may want to marry there. But you are not yet married, so a partner visa is not the first step.

That is the gap this visa fills. It is a temporary visa for engaged couples.

This guide shows you who can apply and what to prepare. It also covers the 2026 cost, the long wait, and the steps after your wedding. Read it before you spend any money.

What Is the Prospective Marriage Visa 300?

The prospective marriage visa is a temporary visa for people who are engaged to an Australian. Its subclass number is 300. People often call it the fiancé visa.

It lets you enter Australia, marry your partner, and then apply to stay as a partner. You must marry while the visa is in effect. If you do not, the visa ends and you must leave.

Who the Fiancé Visa Is For

This visa suits couples who are engaged but not yet living together. Many are in long-distance relationships. Others want to marry in Australia, in front of family.

The key point is simple. You do not need to be married yet. You do not need to have lived together for 12 months either.

Do You Need to Be Married to Apply for a Partner Visa?

No. You can also apply for a partner visa if you are in a de facto relationship, which usually needs proof of a long-term relationship. But if you are engaged and want to marry first, the prospective marriage visa is the usual path.

Think of it as step one. The partner visa is step two.

Who Can Be Your Sponsor?

Your fiancé or fiancée must be one of these:

  • An Australian citizen.
  • An Australian permanent resident.
  • An eligible New Zealand citizen.

This person is called your sponsor, or your "prospective spouse" in the law.

Where You Can Apply From

You apply from outside Australia. In some cases the visa can be granted while you are inside Australia, but most couples plan to travel after the grant. Check the current rules on the Home Affairs website before you book flights.

Prospective Marriage Visa Requirements

Home Affairs wants proof that your plan is real. It also wants to know you are both free to marry. Below are the main requirements.

Requirements for the Applicant

  • You are at least 18 years old.
  • You are free to marry under Australian law.
  • You are not closely related to your partner.
  • You genuinely intend to marry within the visa period.
  • You intend to live together as a married couple.
  • You have met your partner in person since you both turned 18.
  • You meet health and character requirements.
  • You owe no unpaid debts to the Australian government.

Requirements for the Sponsor

Your sponsor must be an eligible citizen or resident. There are also limits on how many people a sponsor can bring.

In general, a sponsor cannot have sponsored more than one other partner or fiancé. They also cannot have sponsored someone in the last five years. Exceptions exist for compelling reasons. Police checks may apply, and a serious criminal history can stop sponsorship.

So ask your partner about their past sponsorships before you lodge.

Evidence You Should Gather

Strong evidence tells one clear story. Collect items in these groups:

  • Intent to marry: a letter from both of you, wedding venue quotes, celebrant emails, and invitations.
  • Real relationship: photos together, travel records, chat history, call logs, and shared plans.
  • Meeting in person: flight tickets, passport stamps, and hotel bookings.
  • Support from others: statutory declarations from friends or family who know you as a couple.
  • Identity: passports and birth certificates.
  • Money: bank statements and proof of income to show you can support yourselves.
  • Health and character: panel doctor checks and police certificates from countries where you lived 12 months or more.

Common Evidence Gaps

Most weak files miss the same things. They have too few photos from different dates. They show no proof of meeting in person. They have vague wedding plans. Fix these before you lodge.

How to Apply for the Prospective Marriage Visa 300

People often ask, "How do I apply for a fiancé visa?" The process is online and has clear steps. The hard part is the evidence, not the form.

Step-by-Step Application Process

  1. Check you both qualify. Confirm your fiancé can sponsor, and that neither of you is married to someone else.
  2. Gather your evidence. Start with the list above. Add more as you go.
  3. Create an ImmiAccount. This is the Home Affairs online portal.
  4. Complete the application. Answer every question and match your documents to your answers.
  5. Ask your sponsor to complete their part. They give their details and sponsor you.
  6. Pay the visa application charge. See the cost section below.
  7. Do your health checks and get police certificates. Use an approved panel doctor.
  8. Wait, and reply fast to any request. Home Affairs may ask for more documents or an interview.
  9. Travel after the grant and marry. Keep your wedding inside the visa period.
  10. Lodge your partner visa. Do this while your 300 visa is still in effect.

A Simple Timeline to Plan Around

No one can promise dates. But a rough plan helps.

  • Months 1 to 2: gather evidence and prepare forms.
  • Month 3: lodge and pay.
  • Months 4 onward: health and police checks, then the long wait.
  • After grant: fly, give your Notice of Intended Marriage to a celebrant, and marry.
  • Soon after the wedding: lodge the partner visa.

Tips to Make Your Application Stronger

  • Keep proof of each visit, with dates.
  • Use one story across every document.
  • Explain gaps, such as a break between visits.
  • Add statements from people who know you both.
  • Keep copies of everything you send.

Prospective Marriage Visa Cost and Processing Time

Cost and time are the two biggest worries. Here is where things stand on 27 November 2026.

How Much Does the Prospective Marriage Visa Cost?

From 1 July 2026, the visa application charge for the main applicant is A$11,710. The older A$9,095 charge no longer applies.

Applicant

Charge from 1 July 2026

Main applicant

A$11,710

Each additional applicant aged 18 or over

A$5,860

Each child under 18

A$2,935

Please check the current fees on the Home Affairs website before you pay.

Other Costs to Plan For

The visa charge is not the only bill. Plan for these as well:

  • Health examinations with an approved panel doctor.
  • Police certificates from each country where you lived 12 months or more.
  • Translations of documents not in English.
  • Flights to meet your partner and to travel after the grant.
  • Your wedding costs and celebrant fees.
  • The partner visa charge after you marry.

What Does the Next Visa Cost?

If you lodge the partner visa while your 300 visa is still in effect, the charge is lower, at about A$1,955 for the main applicant. If your 300 has ended, you may pay the full A$11,710.

This is a big saving, so lodge on time. Confirm the amount with Home Affairs first.

How Long Does the Prospective Marriage Visa Take?

The wait is long. Based on Home Affairs figures to 30 September 2026, 50% of applications finish in about 18 months and 90% finish in about 31 months.

Home Affairs also publishes a combined median of 21 months for the temporary partner group (subclasses 300, 309 and 820). Do not plan on a wait of only 12 to 15 months.

Why the Wait Can Be Longer than You Expect

Since 25 July 2026, a new ministerial direction ranks onshore partner visas first. The 300 visa sits lower in the order. So the wait may stretch.

Missing documents, health or character checks, and busy periods can also slow things down.

How to Avoid Delays

  • Lodge a complete application with all evidence.
  • Book health checks early.
  • Answer Home Affairs requests quickly.
  • Update your details if your address or passport changes.

What Happens After the Prospective Marriage Visa Is Granted?

The grant is only the start. The next year matters most. Here is what to expect.

Visa Conditions and Your Rights

The visa generally lasts 9 to 15 months from the grant. It cannot be extended. Your grant letter shows your exact dates, so read it closely.

While it is in effect, you can:

  • Work in Australia.
  • Study, but at your own cost.
  • Travel in and out of Australia.

You must also follow all Australian laws and your visa conditions. You can check them in VEVO, the Home Affairs online checker.

Marry Within the Visa Period

You must marry your sponsor while the visa is in effect. In some cases the wedding must happen within nine months of arrival. So plan the date early and confirm the exact limit in your grant letter.

To marry, you give a Notice of Intended Marriage to a celebrant. Do this well ahead of the date, as the law requires a waiting period.

Apply for the Partner Visa (820 and 801)

After the wedding, you apply for the onshore partner visa, subclass 820. It leads to the permanent partner visa, subclass 801.

Lodge it while your 300 visa is still in effect. If you wait, you can lose your lawful status and pay a higher fee.

What Happens After 5 Years of Permanent Residency?

This is a common question. After you hold permanent residency for some years, new options open up.

  • Citizenship: you may apply once you meet the time-in-Australia rules and pass the citizenship test. The usual rule is four years of lawful residence, with at least one year as a permanent resident.
  • Travel: a permanent visa carries a five-year travel facility. After that, you need a Resident Return visa to come back if you travel.
  • Rights: you keep work rights, Medicare access and the right to sponsor eligible family.

Check the current citizenship rules with Home Affairs, as they can change.

Why Prospective Marriage Visas Get Refused

Home Affairs can refuse a 300 visa. Most refusals link back to proof or paperwork. Knowing the usual reasons helps you avoid them.

Common Refusal Reasons

  • The couple cannot show a genuine intention to marry.
  • There is no clear proof they have met in person.
  • The sponsor has past sponsorship limits.
  • Health or character checks fail.
  • The applicant owes a debt to the Australian government.
  • Documents conflict with each other.
  • False or misleading documents are sent.

False information is the most serious. It can lead to refusal and a ban on future visas.

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Rushing the application. A thin file rarely survives a long wait.
  • Copying a template story. Case officers read many files. Be specific.
  • Ignoring requests. Missing a deadline can sink the case.
  • Booking the wedding too early. Do it only after the grant.
  • Forgetting the partner visa. Lodge before the 300 visa ends.
  • Trusting old fee and time guides. Numbers changed on 1 July 2026.

What to Do If Your Visa Is Refused

Read the refusal letter first. It lists the reasons and any review deadline. Some refusals can be reviewed, and strict time limits apply.

Speak with a MARA-registered agent or lawyer quickly. Do not travel or book your wedding until you have advice.

Frequently Asked Questions About the Prospective Marriage Visa

Do You Need to Be Married to Apply for a Partner Visa?

No. You can apply as a married couple or as a de facto couple. De facto usually needs proof of a long-term relationship. If you are engaged and want to marry in Australia, the prospective marriage visa is the usual first step.

How Do I Apply for a Fiancé Visa?

You apply online through ImmiAccount for the prospective marriage visa (subclass 300). Your fiancé sponsors you. You send evidence, pay the charge, complete health and police checks, and wait for a decision.

What Happens After 5 Years of Permanent Residency in Australia?

You may apply for citizenship once you meet the residence rules and pass the test. Your permanent visa also has a five-year travel facility. After it ends, you need a Resident Return visa to re-enter if you have been overseas.

How Long Does the Visa Process Take?

Based on Home Affairs figures, 50% of cases take about 18 months and 90% about 31 months. Home Affairs also lists a combined median of 21 months for the temporary partner group. Your own time may differ.

How Much Does the Prospective Marriage Visa Cost?

The charge is A$11,710 for the main applicant from 1 July 2026. Extra adults pay A$5,860 and children under 18 pay A$2,935. Confirm the amounts on the Home Affairs site before you pay.

How Long Is the Prospective Marriage Visa Valid?

Generally 9 to 15 months from the grant. It cannot be extended. Your grant letter shows your exact end date.

Can I Work on a Prospective Marriage Visa?

Yes. You can work and also study at your own cost while the visa is in effect.

Can I Apply from Inside Australia?

You must apply from outside Australia. In some cases it can be granted while you are inside the country. Check Home Affairs before you plan travel.

What If We Do Not Marry in Time?

The visa ends and you must leave Australia. It can also harm future applications. Always plan the wedding early within the visa period.

Can I Bring My Children?

Yes. Dependent children can be included if they meet health and character requirements. They pay their own charge.

In Short

The prospective marriage visa 300 lets an engaged couple marry in Australia. You need a genuine plan, strong proof, an eligible sponsor and A$11,710 from 1 July 2026. The wait can reach 18 to 31 months.

Gather your evidence early and plan your wedding inside the visa period. Then lodge your partner visa on time. If your case is complex, speak with a MARA-registered agent before you apply.
