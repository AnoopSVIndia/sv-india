Student Visa (Subclass 500): Requirements, Cost and the 2026 Changes

The Student visa (subclass 500) lets international students study full time at a registered Australian education provider. Since 2 October 2026 the rules for onshore applications and for bringing family have tightened, and the Genuine Student requirement decides many outcomes. This guide explains what you need, what it costs and how to avoid the mistakes that lead to refusal.

The Basics

What Is the Student Visa (Subclass 500)?

The main visa for international students studying in Australia.

The Student visa (subclass 500) is a temporary visa that lets you stay in Australia while you study a registered course full time. The course must be offered by a provider registered on the Commonwealth Register of Institutions and Courses for Overseas Students (CRICOS). You apply with a Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE) from the provider, and the visa normally lasts for the length of the course.

The Department of Home Affairs decides your visa, and your education provider must also meet its own rules. The visa suits school students, English language (ELICOS) students, vocational education and training (VET) students, higher education students, postgraduate research students and non-award students, with some differences by sector. Study is meant to be your main purpose, which is why the Genuine Student requirement is central.

Eligibility

Student Visa 500 Eligibility Requirements

You need to meet every requirement below. Missing one is enough for a refusal.

  • Enrolment: a valid Confirmation of Enrolment for a full-time course at a CRICOS-registered provider.
  • Genuine Student requirement: you must show that you genuinely intend to study, and that study is your main purpose in coming to Australia.
  • Financial capacity: enough funds for your course fees, living costs and travel, and for any family members who are allowed on the visa.
  • English: the English level your course and provider require, usually shown through an approved test such as IELTS, PTE Academic, TOEFL iBT or OET, unless you are exempt.
  • Health insurance: Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC) for the whole time you hold the visa, in most cases.
  • Health: meet the Australian health requirement, with a medical examination if requested.
  • Character: provide police certificates and meet the character requirement.
  • Location: check whether you can apply from inside Australia, since onshore applications are now restricted for most applicants.

Criteria change often. Student visa rules, planning levels and processing priorities have changed several times in recent years. We check the current rules for your course, provider and circumstances before we advise you.

Latest Changes

Student Visa Changes from 2 October 2026

The Department of Home Affairs confirmed new rules for applications lodged on or after 2 October 2026.

What changed for applications lodged on or after 2 October 2026
AreaWhat the rule now says
Onshore applicationsMost temporary visa holders in Australia can no longer lodge a Student (subclass 500) or Student Guardian (subclass 590) application while in Australia.
Current Student visa holdersApplying for another Student visa in Australia is restricted unless an exemption applies. This includes current dependants who want to apply as primary applicants. Your existing visa is not affected.
If you cannot apply onshoreYou must be outside Australia when you lodge and when the visa is granted.
Student Guardian holdersCurrent holders can still lodge a further Student Guardian application while in Australia.
Family membersNew applicants generally cannot include a partner or dependent children unless an exemption applies. Current holders can no longer add family members after the visa is granted, and there are no exemptions to that second rule.
Earlier applicationsApplications lodged before 2 October 2026 are assessed under the rules in place when they were lodged.
Exemptions for a further onshore Student visa
You may qualify if you
Need up to 12 more months after your course end date to finish your main course with the same provider
Are sponsored by the Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade (DFAT) or Defence
Are studying for a PhD
Are doing primary or secondary school studies
Completed a main course with an AQF level and are moving to a higher AQF level course (a higher education course must be followed by another from a higher education provider)
Completed school and are moving to a course without an AQF level, or completed a course without an AQF level and are moving to one with an AQF level
Cannot continue because your education provider defaulted

Exemptions for including family at application. Studying a PhD, being sponsored by DFAT or Defence, receiving a foreign government scholarship, or being an eligible student from a Pacific or ASEAN country. If family members are already on your current Student visa, you may be able to include them in your next application, and everyone must be in the same location when you lodge.

Visitor visa and Temporary Graduate visa holders have already been unable to apply for a Student visa in Australia since 1 July 2024. A wrongly lodged application, or one that includes an ineligible family member, can cost you the application charge, so get advice before you lodge.

Key Test

The Genuine Student Requirement Explained

Many refusals come from this test, not from the paperwork.

The Genuine Student requirement replaced the Genuine Temporary Entrant test in March 2024. It asks you to show that you genuinely intend to study in Australia, and that study is your main purpose. You answer targeted questions about your circumstances at home and in Australia, why you chose your course and provider, how the course benefits your future, and anything else that is relevant, and you support your answers with documents.

The Department compares your story with your evidence. A clear link between your past study or work, your chosen course and your plans afterwards usually works better than general statements. Answers that look copied, or that do not fit your documents, can lead to refusal. We help you present your own circumstances accurately and clearly.

Funds and English

Financial Capacity, English and Health Cover for the Student Visa

The three practical requirements that need the most preparation.

Practical requirements for the Student visa
RequirementWhat to expect
Financial capacityShow you can pay your tuition for the first 12 months (or the whole course if shorter), living costs and travel. The living cost figure for a single student has been AUD 29,710 for 12 months at the last published level, and the Department indexes the amounts, so check the current figure. Extra funds apply for family members who are allowed on the visa and for school-age children.
Evidence of fundsLiquid savings, bank statements, loans or scholarships, and evidence of a sponsor's income if a relative supports you. Funds should show a stable history, so a large deposit just before you apply can raise questions.
EnglishYour provider and course set the required level, shown by an approved test taken at a secure test centre, unless you are exempt. Direct entry to higher education usually needs a higher score than a packaged English course, and providers can set higher requirements than the visa minimum.
Health coverOverseas Student Health Cover (OSHC) from an approved insurer for the whole length of your stay, for you and any family members on the visa.

Financial and English requirements are set by the Department of Home Affairs and by your provider, and they change. We check the current figures and your provider's own requirements before you commit to a course.

While You Study

Student Visa Work Rights and Conditions

Study comes first, and your visa has conditions you must follow.

Main conditions of the Student visa
ConditionWhat it means
Work hoursGenerally up to 48 hours per fortnight while your course is in session. A fortnight is any 14-day period starting on a Monday.
Course breaksNo limit on hours during scheduled course breaks.
Research studentsStudents who have started a masters by research or a doctorate are not subject to the 48-hour limit.
When work startsYou can only start work once your course has started.
Enrolment and progressStay enrolled in your registered course, make satisfactory academic progress and keep your attendance up.
Health coverKeep OSHC for the whole time you hold the visa.
Changing courses or providersCourse and provider changes are restricted, so check the rules before you transfer.

Breaching your visa conditions, such as working over the limit, can lead to cancellation and can affect future visa applications. Check the current conditions on your visa grant letter.

Get Organised

Documents to Prepare for a Student Visa Application

Not every applicant needs every item, but this list covers what most files require.

  • Passport and national ID for every person on the application.
  • Confirmation of Enrolment from your CRICOS-registered provider.
  • English test result report that is still valid, or evidence of an exemption.
  • Academic transcripts and certificates from your previous study.
  • Evidence of your financial capacity, such as bank statements, loan or scholarship letters.
  • Evidence of any sponsor's income and relationship, if a relative supports you.
  • Proof of Overseas Student Health Cover.
  • Documents that support your Genuine Student answers, such as employment letters and study plans.
  • Police certificates for each country where you have lived for 12 months or more.
  • Relationship and birth documents for any family members who are eligible to be included.

Budget beyond the visa charge. Plan for tuition and living costs, OSHC, your English test, any health examination, police certificates, translations and professional fees. Additional charges apply for other applicants, and some applications such as school or English-language courses have different charges.

Charges are set by the Australian Government and normally change each 1 July. Confirm the current amount on the Department of Home Affairs visa pricing estimator before you pay. The visa application charge is generally not refunded if an application is refused.

These times are a guide, and the Department updates them regularly. Check the live processing times tool before you plan your arrival date. Entry is usually allowed up to a set time before your course starts, which your visa grant letter states.

For offshore applications, Ministerial Direction 115, in effect since 14 November 2025, sets processing priority based on how each provider is managing its new international student numbers against its allocation. The National Planning Level for 2026 is 295,000 new overseas student commencements. This can affect how quickly your application is processed, so provider choice matters.

After You Study

What Happens After Your Course: Post-Study Options

A Student visa does not lead directly to permanent residence, but it can open later options.

The Student visa is temporary, and study must stay your main purpose. When you finish, you may be able to apply for a Temporary Graduate visa (subclass 485), which gives you time to work in Australia after eligible study, or to move to an employer-sponsored or skilled visa if you meet the criteria at that time. Australian study can also add points on the skilled visa points test.

Post-study rules change often, so plan with current rules and not with what a friend or an older website describes. We map your course choice to your later options before you enrol.

Avoid These

Common Student Visa Mistakes That Lead to Refusal

Generic Genuine Student Answers

Answers that could belong to anyone, or that do not match your documents, are a common reason for refusal.

Weak or Sudden Funds

Large deposits made just before applying, or funds you cannot explain, raise questions about financial capacity.

Applying Onshore When You Cannot

Most temporary visa holders can no longer lodge a Student visa in Australia. A wrongly lodged application can cost you the charge.

Planning to Bring Family

From 2 October 2026, most new applicants cannot include a partner or children. Check before you plan.

Choosing a Course That Does Not Fit

A course that looks unrelated to your background and goals invites questions about your real purpose.

Working Over the Limit

Working more than your hours limit can lead to cancellation and affect future applications.

Common Questions

Student Visa (Subclass 500) FAQs

What is the Student visa (subclass 500)?

The Student visa (subclass 500) lets you stay in Australia to study full time in a CRICOS-registered course. You need a Confirmation of Enrolment, you must meet the Genuine Student requirement, show you can afford your stay, hold Overseas Student Health Cover and meet health and character requirements.

Can I apply for a Student visa from inside Australia in 2026?

For applications lodged on or after 2 October 2026, most temporary visa holders in Australia can no longer apply onshore for a Student visa, and current Student visa holders applying for another Student visa are also restricted unless an exemption applies. If the rules stop you applying onshore, you must be outside Australia when you apply and when the visa is granted.

Can I bring my partner and children on a Student visa?

From 2 October 2026, new Student visa applicants generally cannot include a partner or dependent children unless an exemption applies. Exemptions include PhD study, DFAT or Defence sponsorship, a foreign government scholarship, and eligible students from a Pacific or ASEAN country. Current holders can no longer add family members after the visa is granted. Applications lodged before 2 October 2026 are assessed under the rules at the time of lodgement.

What is the Genuine Student requirement?

The Genuine Student requirement replaced the Genuine Temporary Entrant test in March 2024. You answer targeted questions about your circumstances at home and in Australia, why you chose your course and provider, how the course benefits your future, and other relevant information, and support your answers with documents.

How many hours can I work on a Student visa?

Generally up to 48 hours per fortnight while your course is in session, with no limit during scheduled course breaks. Students who have started a masters by research or a doctorate are not subject to the limit. You can only start work once your course has started.

Do I need health insurance?

Yes. You must hold Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC) for the whole length of your stay, for you and any family members on the visa.

How long does a Student visa take to process?

It depends on the education sector and on the provider's priority status. Processing times published by Home Affairs on 29 September 2026 had most higher education applications finalised within about 49 days to four months, and vocational education applications taking longer. These are a guide only, and incomplete applications take longer.

Can I work towards permanent residence after I study?

A Student visa does not lead directly to permanent residence, and you must be a genuine student whose main purpose is study. After you finish, you may be able to use a Temporary Graduate visa or a skilled visa if you meet the criteria at that time. Rules change, so check them before you plan.
