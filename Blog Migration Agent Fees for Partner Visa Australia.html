Why Do Agent Fees Vary So Much?

Two agents can quote very different prices for the same visa. These are the usual reasons:

  • Scope: One quote may cover both stages. Another may cover stage 1 only.
  • Case complexity: A short relationship, past visa refusals or a complex visa history take more work.
  • Number of applicants: Adding children or other family members can raise the fee.
  • Experience: Senior agents and lawyers often charge more.
  • Support level: Some quotes include replies to Home Affairs and interview help. Others do not.

A low price is not always a bad sign. However, check the scope before you say yes.

Government Fee vs Migration Agent Fees for a Partner Visa

The agent fee is not the visa fee. You pay two separate bills. The government charge goes to the Department of Home Affairs. The professional fee goes to your agent.

What Is the Government Partner Visa Fee in 2026?

The charge for the 820/801, 309/100 and 300 visas is the same. It covers both stages when you apply together.

The visa charge is also non-refundable. You do not get it back if the visa is refused. For this reason, many people want a careful check before they lodge.

What Do Partner Visa Migration Agent Fees Include?

A fair quote should say clearly what you get. Ask for it in writing.

What Is Usually Included in the Agent Fee

Most partner visa fees cover the core work:

  • A check of your eligibility and the best visa path
  • A document and evidence checklist
  • Help with forms and your relationship statement
  • Lodgement with the Department of Home Affairs
  • Replies to requests from the Department

What Is Often Unclear

Some items are included by one agent and charged by another. Ask about each one.

  • Stage 2: Is the permanent stage (801 or 100) in the same fee?
  • Interviews: Will the agent help if a case officer calls you?
  • Extra requests: Who pays for more work if Home Affairs asks for more proof?
  • Refusal: Is a review at the Administrative Review Tribunal (ART) included?

Fixed Fee, Staged Payments or Hourly: How Agents Bill

The way an agent charges matters as much as the price. It decides how much you can plan for.

Fixed Fee

The agent quotes one price for a set scope. This is the most common model for a partner visa. It is easy to budget, so ask what the price covers.

Staged Payments

You pay in parts. For example, one part when you start and one when the agent lodges. Staged payments also protect you if your plans change.

Hourly Rates

You pay for each hour the agent works. This can be fair for a small task. However, it can grow without a limit. If an agent bills hourly, ask for an estimate and a cap.

"No Visa, No Fee" Offers

Some firms offer to charge a reduced fee, or none, if the visa is refused. Terms differ and often have conditions. Read the fine print and ask what is refunded.

What About Full Payment Upfront?

Be careful if an agent wants the full amount before agreeing the scope. Ask for a written fee agreement first.

An agent cannot promise a visa outcome. Only the Department of Home Affairs decides.

Is It Worth Paying Migration Agent Fees for a Partner Visa?

It depends on your case. You do not need an agent to apply. However, the government fee is high and it is not refunded. A small mistake can be costly.

Can You Apply for a Partner Visa Without an Agent?

Yes. Many couples lodge on their own through ImmiAccount. This can work well if your case is simple and you have clear, well-organised proof.

It also means you carry the risk. You must understand the rules, build the evidence and reply to Home Affairs on time.

When an Agent Can Help the Most

An agent is worth a close look if any of these apply:

  • Your relationship is short, or you have not lived together for long
  • You have had a visa refused or cancelled
  • You are on a bridging visa, or your visa is about to end
  • You have children, or a complex family situation
  • You have a past character or health issue
  • You are short of time and want the paperwork done right

What an Agent Cannot Do

An agent can organise your case and explain the rules. They cannot guarantee a result. Be wary of anyone who does.

Only a registered migration agent or a lawyer can give you paid visa advice in Australia.

How to Compare Partner Visa Migration Agent Fees and Avoid Red Flags

A lower price is not always a better deal. A higher price is not always better service. Use the steps below to compare quotes fairly.

Step 1: Check the Agent Is Registered

Search for the agent on the official register of the Office of the Migration Agents Registration Authority (OMARA). Look for their MARN, which is their registration number. Lawyers are checked through their state law society.

Step 2: Ask for a Written, Itemised Quote

The quote should show the agent fee, GST, and the government charge as separate lines. It should also list which stages and tasks are included.

Step 3: Ask These Questions

  1. Does the fee cover both stages, or only stage 1?
  2. What happens if Home Affairs asks for more evidence?
  3. Is a review after a refusal included?
  4. How and when do I pay?
  5. What is refunded if I change my mind or the visa is refused?

Red Flags to Watch For

  • No price or price range anywhere, and no written fee agreement
  • Open-ended hourly billing with no estimate or cap
  • Full payment demanded before the scope is agreed
  • A promise or guarantee that your visa will be granted
  • A quote far below others, with a very narrow scope
  • Pressure to sign on the spot

If you see two or more of these, pause and get a second opinion.

Migration Agent Fees for Partner Visa Australia: FAQs

How Much Is the Fee for a Partner Visa?

The government charge is A$11,710 for the main applicant since 1 July 2026. An agent adds a professional fee, often A$4,000 to A$7,000 for both stages. Medicals, police checks and translations are extra.

Will the Partner Visa Fee Increase in 2026?

It already did. The charge rose about 25% on 1 July 2026, from A$9,365 to A$11,710. Home Affairs usually adjusts charges each 1 July, so check before you lodge.

Is It Better to Use a Migration Agent for Australia?

It can be, especially for complex cases such as a short relationship, a past refusal or a bridging visa. Simple cases can be lodged without an agent. No agent can guarantee a result.

How Much Do Immigration Consultants Charge in Australia?

Fees depend on the visa. For a partner visa, published prices run from about A$2,600 to A$8,000 for both stages. A first consultation is free or costs up to about A$160.

Is the Agent Fee Separate from the Government Fee?

Yes. You pay the agent for their work. You pay the government charge to the Department of Home Affairs. A good quote shows both clearly.

Do I Pay Again for the Permanent Stage?

You do not pay a second government fee. Some agents charge separately for stage 2, so ask whether it is in your quote.

Can I Pay a Migration Agent in Instalments?

Often yes. Many agents use staged payments, such as part at the start and part at lodgement. Agree the schedule in writing.

Are Cheap Migration Agents Safe?

A low price can be fine, but check the scope. Confirm the agent is registered, ask what is included and be wary of any guarantee.

Do I Get My Agent Fee Back if My Visa Is Refused?

It depends on the agent's written terms. The government visa charge is not refunded after a refusal.

Is the First Consultation Free?

Some agents offer a free first consultation. Others charge about A$99 to A$160 for 45 minutes and may credit it to your fee.

Can I Negotiate Migration Agent Fees?

You can ask. However, a lower price often means a narrower scope. Compare what is included before you compare the price.

Do I Need a Migration Agent to Apply for a Partner Visa?

No, it is not required. Many couples apply on their own. An agent can help if your case is complex or you want the paperwork checked.

In Short

Migration agent fees for a partner visa in Australia usually run about A$4,000 to A$7,000 for both stages. The government charge is separate: A$11,710 for the main applicant since 1 July 2026. Medicals, police checks and translations cost extra.

Compare the full cost, not only the price. Ask what each quote includes, get it in writing, check the agent's registration and be wary of any promise of a result. A good agent will explain every cost before you commit.
