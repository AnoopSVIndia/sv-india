Skilled Independent Visa (Subclass 189): Points, Eligibility and How to Get Invited

The Skilled Independent visa (subclass 189) gives skilled workers permanent residence in Australia without an employer, state or family sponsor. You enter a points-based contest through SkillSelect, so the real work is building a competitive profile before you lodge anything. This guide explains how that works, step by step.

The Basics

What Is the Skilled Independent Visa (Subclass 189)?

A permanent visa for skilled workers who are invited through the Department of Home Affairs' SkillSelect system.

The subclass 189 is Australia's main points-tested, independent skilled migration visa. "Independent" means no employer sponsors you, no state or territory government nominates you, and no relative sponsors you. Instead, you submit an Expression of Interest (EOI) in SkillSelect, the Department of Home Affairs' online system, and the Department ranks you against other candidates by points. The highest-ranked profiles in each occupation are invited to apply.

Because the visa is permanent, it lets you live and work anywhere in Australia, study, enrol in Medicare, and later apply for Australian citizenship once you meet the residence requirements. You can also include eligible family members. That freedom is the reason competition is strong, and why a clear plan matters more than a quick application.

Eligibility

Subclass 189 Visa Eligibility Requirements

You need to meet every requirement below. Missing one is enough to stop an application, however high your points are.

  • Eligible occupation: your nominated occupation must be on the Medium and Long-term Strategic Skills List (MLTSSL), the occupation list that applies to the 189.
  • Positive skills assessment: the relevant assessing authority must confirm your skills for that occupation.
  • Age: you must be under 45 when you are invited to apply.
  • English: at least Competent English at the time of invitation, shown through an approved test such as IELTS, PTE Academic, TOEFL iBT, OET or Cambridge C1 Advanced, or an accepted passport.
  • Points: a score of at least 65 on the points test, as claimed in your EOI and proven at application.
  • Invitation: an invitation to apply issued through SkillSelect, which you must act on within 60 days.
  • Health: meet the Australian health requirement, normally through a panel physician examination.
  • Character: provide police certificates and meet the character requirement.

Occupation lists change. The Department of Home Affairs updates its skilled occupation lists from time to time, and occupations are identified by ANZSCO code. An occupation that was eligible last year may not be now, and being on a list does not by itself mean you will be invited. We check the current list and your assessing authority before we advise you.

Points for skilled employment from outside and inside Australia are combined and capped at 20 in total. PTE Academic equivalents are 50, 65 and 79 in each component for Competent, Proficient and Superior English. Points tables are set in law (Schedule 6D of the Migration Regulations 1994), so confirm the current version with a registered agent or on the Department of Home Affairs website before you rely on any figure here.

Points test reform: the Australian Government has announced that the permanent skilled points test will be redesigned, but as at 26 October 2026 no new points table is in force. The table above and the 65-point pass mark still apply. Be cautious of claims online about a higher pass mark or a start date, and rely on the Department of Home Affairs for confirmed changes.

Why 65 points is rarely enough

The pass mark is a minimum, not a promise. SkillSelect ranks all EOIs in an occupation, and each invitation round is limited by occupation ceilings and the size of the migration program. In recent rounds the lowest score invited has varied widely by occupation, from the 65-point floor in some occupations to well above that in others, so check how your own occupation has fared in the results Home Affairs publishes for each round. When your occupation's cut-off is higher than your score, the useful question is which points you can still add, and how fast.

Ways applicants raise their score

The most common gains come from retaking an English test for Proficient or Superior results, a Professional Year, a credentialled community language such as Punjabi or Hindi where it qualifies, and timing the EOI so employment milestones are counted. We map these against your own profile and rank them by cost, time and risk.

Step Zero

Skills Assessment for the 189 Visa

Your skills assessment is the first formal gate, and the longest lead time you control.

A skills assessment is an independent check that your qualifications and work experience meet Australian standards for your nominated occupation. Each occupation is linked to a specific assessing authority, for example Engineers Australia for engineering occupations, the Australian Computer Society for ICT roles, VETASSESS for many professional and trade occupations, CPA Australia or CA ANZ for accountants, and the Australian Nursing and Midwifery Accreditation Council for nursing and midwifery.

Each authority sets its own documents, fees and timeframes. Choosing the wrong occupation or authority is one of the most expensive mistakes in the whole process, because a negative or mismatched outcome affects your points claim and your timeline. We help you match your degree and job duties to the right ANZSCO occupation before you pay for an assessment.

Match

Pick the right occupation

Your job duties, not your job title, decide which ANZSCO code fits, and the closest match is not always the most competitive one.

The Process

How to Apply for the Subclass 189 Visa

A typical path from first conversation to a visa grant.

01

Free points and occupation check

We calculate your points honestly, confirm your occupation is eligible, and tell you whether the 189 is realistic or whether the 190 or 491 suits you better.

02

Complete your skills assessment

Lodge with the correct assessing authority and respond to any requests for more evidence.

03

Take your English test

Book IELTS, PTE Academic or another approved test and, where it makes sense, aim for the higher band that adds points.

04

Submit your Expression of Interest

Create your SkillSelect EOI with accurate claims. Everything you claim must be provable later, so we check each claim against your documents first.

05

Receive an invitation

Invitations are issued in rounds. If your score or occupation is not reached, we review your EOI and look for points you can still add.

06

Lodge within 60 days

Submit your online application through ImmiAccount with identity, qualification, employment, English, health and character evidence. Having documents ready before the invitation is the single best way to protect this deadline.

07

Health checks, biometrics and grant

Complete medicals and any requested checks, then respond quickly to Department requests until a decision is made.

Get Organised

Documents to Prepare Before You Are Invited

Not every applicant needs every item, but this list covers what most 189 files require.

  • Passport and national ID for every person on the application.
  • Positive skills assessment outcome letter.
  • English test result report that is still valid.
  • Degree certificates and academic transcripts.
  • Employment references showing duties, hours and dates.
  • Payslips, tax returns or bank statements supporting employment claims.
  • Police certificates from each country you have lived in for the required period.
  • Relationship evidence and partner documents, if applicable.
  • Birth certificates for dependent children.
  • Credentials supporting extra points, such as a NAATI certificate or Professional Year completion.

Timing

How Long Does the 189 Visa Take?

There are two clocks. The first is the wait to be invited, which depends on your points, your occupation and when the Department holds its next round. Home Affairs holds only a few rounds a year, not on a fixed monthly timetable, and an EOI can wait a long time if its score is below the cut-off for your occupation. The second clock starts when you lodge: the Department publishes current processing time ranges, which change with demand and with how complete your file is.

Rather than quote a figure that may be out of date, we check the Department's published processing ranges when we advise you. A complete, well-evidenced application that answers requests quickly generally avoids avoidable delays.

Fit Check

Is the Skilled Independent Visa Right for You?

The 189 tends to suit you if

  • Your occupation is on the current list and has a positive assessment or a clear route to one.
  • You are under 45, ideally 25 to 39, with Proficient or Superior English.
  • Your points are comfortably above 65, or you can realistically raise them.
  • You want freedom to settle in any state without a nomination commitment.

Consider another route if

  • Your score sits near the minimum in a crowded occupation.
  • You are close to the age cut-off and cannot add many points.
  • Your occupation is not on the list, or has no assessing authority route.
  • An employer or state is willing to sponsor or nominate you.
Avoid These

Common Subclass 189 Mistakes That Cost Applicants a Year

Over-claiming points

Points in your EOI must be provable when you apply. A claim you cannot back up can lead to refusal, and later visa applications may be affected.

Waiting on the minimum score

Submitting at 65 and hoping rarely works in a competitive occupation. Check how your occupation's invited scores compare to yours first.

Picking the wrong occupation

A code that matches your title but not your actual duties can fail the assessment, wasting the fee and months of time.

Expiring English results

English test results are valid for a limited period. An expired result at invitation can cost you the points or force a retest.

Missing the 60-day window

You cannot extend the deadline. Gathering police certificates and medicals after the invitation arrives is the usual reason files run late.

Ignoring age milestones

Points fall at 33 and 40, and eligibility ends at 45. Age is the one variable you cannot improve, so plan your timeline around it.

Common Questions

Subclass 189 Visa FAQs

How many points do I need for the subclass 189 visa?

The minimum pass mark is 65 points. Passing is not the same as being invited: SkillSelect ranks every EOI, and the score that actually receives an invitation varies by occupation and by round, often well above 65.

What is the age limit for the Skilled Independent visa?

You must be under 45 when you are invited to apply. Points for age peak between 25 and 32 and fall after 33 and again after 40.

Do I need a job offer or sponsor for the 189 visa?

No. The 189 is a points-tested visa with no employer sponsorship, state or territory nomination, or family sponsorship. You compete on your points score and occupation.

How long do I have to apply after I am invited?

You have 60 days from the date of your invitation to lodge the visa application. The invitation cannot be extended, so prepare your documents before the invitation arrives.

What is the difference between the 189, 190 and 491 visas?

The 189 needs no sponsor and gives permanent residence with freedom to live anywhere in Australia. The 190 needs state or territory nomination and carries a commitment to that location, and the 491 is a provisional regional visa with a pathway to permanent residence. Both nominations add points to your score.

Is the points test changing?

The Australian Government has announced a redesign of the permanent skilled points test, but as at 26 October 2026 no new points table is in force. The current Schedule 6D table and the 65-point pass mark still apply to the 189.

Can I include my partner and children?

Yes. Eligible family members can be included in the application, each with their own visa application charge. A partner can also add points if they hold a suitable skills assessment and have competent English.

Can I get the visa if I am already in Australia?

Yes. You can lodge the 189 while in Australia or from overseas, as long as you hold an invitation and meet the criteria. Your current visa conditions and expiry date still matter, so check them before you plan your timeline.
