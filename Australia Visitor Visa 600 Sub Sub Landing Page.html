Visitor Visa Guide

Visitor Visa (Subclass 600): Streams, Genuine Visitor Test, Costs and How to Apply

The Visitor visa (subclass 600) lets you visit Australia temporarily for a holiday, to see family and friends, or for short business activities. It does not allow work and cannot lead directly to permanent residence. This guide explains the streams, the genuine visitor requirement, current charges and the mistakes that cause refusals.

The Basics

What Is the Visitor Visa (Subclass 600)?

A temporary visa for visits, not for living or working in Australia.

The Visitor visa (subclass 600) is the main Australian visa for short visits. People use it for a holiday or tourism, to visit family and friends, to attend a wedding or event, or to take part in short business activities such as meetings and conferences. The Department of Home Affairs grants it for a stay of a few months up to a year, with the exact period and entry rights shown in your grant notice.

It is a temporary visa. You cannot work on it, you cannot treat it as a way to move to Australia, and a long run of back-to-back visits can raise questions about whether you are a genuine visitor. If you want to study, work or settle, there are other visas built for that, and we explain the options below.

The Key Test

The Genuine Visitor Requirement

Most refusals turn on this point, not on forms.

Ties

Reasons to Return Home

Employment, study, family, property and community ties show you have a life to return to. Evidence should match what you say in your application.

Purpose

A Clear, Credible Reason

A specific purpose with realistic dates and plans helps. A vague or inconsistent story invites questions.

History

Travel and Visa Record

Past trips where you complied with conditions and returned on time help. Long periods already spent in Australia can work against you.

After Grant

Visitor Visa Conditions You Must Follow

Breaking a condition can lead to cancellation and problems with future visas.

Common Visitor visa conditions
ConditionWhat it means
No work (8101)Generally applies to Tourist, Sponsored Family and Frequent Traveller visas.
Business activities only (8115)Business Visitor visas allow limited business activity, not paid employment.
Study limit (8201)Study or training is limited to a short period, up to 3 months in total.
No further stay (8503)Usually prevents you from applying for most other visas while in Australia. Waivers are limited.
12 months in 18 (8558)You must not spend more than 12 months in Australia in any 18-month period.
Health insurance (8501)May be imposed, often for longer stays or older visitors. Check your grant notice.

Conditions are listed in your visa grant notice and in VEVO. Read them before you travel, and never assume a visa that allowed one thing last time allows it again.

Fit Check

Is the Visitor Visa Right for You?

The Visitor Visa May Suit You If

  • You are visiting for a holiday, family or short business purpose.
  • You have strong ties and a clear plan to return home.
  • You can fund the trip and have a credible itinerary.
  • Your passport is not eligible for an ETA or eVisitor.

Consider Another Route If

  • You plan to work, or study for more than a short period.
  • You want to stay long term or move to Australia.
  • You need care or want to provide care to a relative.
  • Your real purpose does not match a visit.

Costs

Visitor Visa 600 Cost and Processing Time

Charges are set per applicant and change each 1 July.

Visitor visa (600) base application charges from 1 July 2026 (AUD)
StreamCharge per applicant
Tourist, applying outside AustraliaAUD 250
Tourist, applying inside AustraliaAUD 630
Business VisitorAUD 250
Sponsored FamilyAUD 250
Frequent TravellerAUD 1,845

Every applicant, including children, pays a separate charge. A further temporary application charge can apply to some onshore Tourist applicants, and a security bond may be requested for Sponsored Family applications. Health examinations, biometrics, police certificates, translations and professional fees are extra. Charges are usually not refunded once a decision is made. Because charges can be adjusted, confirm the figure for your stream on the Department of Home Affairs pricing page before you lodge.

Processing times change month to month and differ by stream and country. Recently published figures show most Tourist applications decided in about a month, Business Visitor applications faster, and Sponsored Family applications slower. Use the Home Affairs processing times tool for the current estimate, and lodge well ahead of your travel date.

Get Organised

Documents to Prepare for a Visitor Visa Application

  • A valid passport, and passports of any family members applying with you.
  • Recent bank statements and evidence of income or savings.
  • Evidence of employment, study or business at home, such as a leave letter.
  • Proof of family, property or community ties in your home country.
  • A simple itinerary or invitation, with dates and where you will stay.
  • For Sponsored Family, the sponsor's identity, status and relationship evidence.
  • Previous travel history and visa records.
  • Health examination or police certificates if requested.
Avoid These

Common Visitor Visa Mistakes

Weak Ties Evidence

Not showing why you will return home is the most common reason for refusal.

Inconsistent Stories

Dates, funds or purpose that do not match across documents raise concern.

Working While Visiting

Paid work breaches the visa conditions and can lead to cancellation.

Spending Too Long in Australia

Repeated or lengthy visits can suggest you are living here, not visiting.

Applying Onshore Too Late

An onshore application must be lodged before your current visa ends.

Overlooking Conditions

Conditions such as 8503 and 8558 limit what you can do and apply for later.

Common Questions

Visitor Visa (Subclass 600) FAQs

What is the Visitor visa (subclass 600)?

The Visitor visa (subclass 600) is a temporary visa for people who want to visit Australia for tourism, to see family or friends, or for short business visits. It has several streams, including Tourist, Business Visitor, Sponsored Family, Approved Destination Status and Frequent Traveller. It does not lead to permanent residence.

How long can I stay in Australia on a Visitor visa?

The length of stay is set in your visa grant letter and usually ranges from 3 to 12 months, depending on your purpose, travel history and circumstances. Always check the grant notice for your permitted stay and whether you can enter more than once, rather than assuming.

How much does a Visitor visa (600) cost?

From 1 July 2026 the base application charge for the Tourist stream is AUD 250 when you apply from outside Australia and AUD 630 when you apply from inside Australia. Business Visitor and Sponsored Family are also reported at AUD 250, and Frequent Traveller at AUD 1,845. Each applicant pays separately, and charges change each 1 July. Confirm the figure for your stream on the Home Affairs pricing page.

How long does a Visitor visa take to process?

Processing times change monthly and vary by stream. Recently reported Home Affairs figures show most Tourist applications decided in about a month, with Sponsored Family taking noticeably longer. Check the Home Affairs processing times tool for the current figure and lodge well before you travel.

Can I work or study on a Visitor visa?

No. Tourist and Sponsored Family visas do not allow work. The Business Visitor stream allows limited business activities such as meetings and conferences, but not paid work for an Australian employer. Study or training is generally limited to a short period, typically up to 3 months under the visa condition.

Can I apply for a Visitor visa from inside Australia?

Only the Tourist stream can generally be lodged in Australia, and only if your current visa does not carry a condition such as 8503 (no further stay). You must apply before your current visa ends. Business, Sponsored Family, Frequent Traveller and Approved Destination Status applications must be lodged outside Australia.

What is the genuine visitor requirement?

The Department must be satisfied you intend a genuine temporary stay. It looks at your ties to your home country, your travel history, your reason for visiting, how long you plan to stay and whether you can fund the trip. No single document guarantees a grant, so a clear and consistent application matters.

Do I need a Visitor visa, an ETA or an eVisitor?

It depends on your passport. Holders of certain passports can apply for an Electronic Travel Authority (subclass 601) or an eVisitor (subclass 651) instead of a subclass 600 visa. If your passport is not eligible, or your plans do not fit, you apply for the Visitor visa (600). Check your passport on the Home Affairs website.
