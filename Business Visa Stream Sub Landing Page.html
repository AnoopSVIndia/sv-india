Business Visitor Stream (Subclass 600): Short Term Business Travel to Australia

If your trip is for meetings, negotiations, a conference, or exploring a business opportunity, the business visitor stream is usually the right pathway, provided you are not being paid by an Australian business for local work. This page explains exactly what condition 8115 allows and does not allow.

Who It Suits

What Counts as a Business Visitor Activity?

The line between visiting for business and working in Australia is exactly what the Department checks most closely.

  • Attending business meetings, negotiations or contract discussions.
  • Attending a conference, trade show, or industry event as a delegate.
  • Exploring a potential business opportunity or investment.
  • Conducting inspections, audits, or short technical assessments for your overseas employer.
  • Signing an agreement negotiated overseas, which is generally allowed, unlike ongoing service delivery in Australia.
The Key Rule

What Condition 8115 Actually Means

Most business visitor stream refusals or later problems trace back to a misunderstanding of this single condition.

Condition 8115 generally prevents a business visitor from engaging in work in Australia other than the specific business visitor activities described above. In practice, this means you cannot be paid by an Australian business for local work, provide services directly to Australian clients as if you were locally employed, or fill a role that would otherwise need a work visa. Activities that support a genuine overseas business relationship, such as negotiating and signing a deal, are usually fine. Ongoing hands on work delivered from within Australia usually is not. If you are unsure which side of that line your trip falls on, it is worth having your specific itinerary reviewed before you travel.

Get Organised

Document Checklist for Business Visitors

  • A letter from your overseas employer confirming your role, the purpose of the trip, and that you remain employed and paid overseas.
  • An invitation letter from the Australian business, conference organiser, or event you are attending.
  • A clear itinerary covering meeting dates, locations, or the conference program.
  • Evidence of your ongoing ties to your home country, such as continued employment and income there.
  • Financial evidence covering the cost of the trip.
  • Travel and health insurance for the length of your stay.

Common Questions

Business Visitor Stream FAQs

Can I sign a contract while I am in Australia?

Generally yes, signing an agreement that was negotiated overseas is considered a business visitor activity, rather than local work.

Can I attend a job interview on this visa?

Attending an interview is generally acceptable, but starting local employment or being paid for ongoing work in Australia is not permitted under this stream.

Can my Australian client pay me for work done while I am there?

No. Being paid by an Australian business for services delivered locally would breach condition 8115. Any payment should come from your overseas employer for your overseas role.

How long can a single business visit last?

Stays are commonly granted for shorter periods than the tourist stream, often around three months, though the exact period is confirmed on your individual grant notice.

Can I get a visa that allows multiple trips over a few years?

Multiple entry business visitor visas are often available for frequent business travellers, subject to individual assessment.
