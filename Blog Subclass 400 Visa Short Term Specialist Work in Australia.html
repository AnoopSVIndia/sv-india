Subclass 400 Visa: Short-Term Specialist Work in Australia

Quick Answer: The subclass 400 visa lets a person with rare skills do short, one-off work in Australia. Home Affairs calls it short-term, highly specialised work. You usually stay up to 3 months, and up to 6 in limited cases. No sponsor is needed, but you need an invitation from an Australian organisation. The charge is A$535 for the main applicant from 1 July 2026. It does not lead to PR.

Some jobs cannot wait for a long visa. A machine arrives from overseas and needs a specialist to set it up. A project needs one expert for a few weeks.

That is what the 400 visa is for. It is fast, and it is short.

But it is also easy to misuse. This guide explains who can apply and what counts as "specialised". It also covers the 2026 cost, the time it takes, how it differs from the 482, and what to do if the job runs long.

What Is the Subclass 400 Visa?

The subclass 400 visa is called Temporary Work (Short Stay Activity). Its main use is the Highly Specialised Work stream. Home Affairs describes it as short-term, highly specialised work, where the skills are not readily available in Australia.

It is a visa for the person, not a sponsored job. There is no sponsor or nomination step. But an Australian organisation must invite or support you.

What Does "Highly Specialised" Mean?

It means you bring skills, knowledge or experience that Australian workers cannot readily provide in the time needed. The work must also be non-ongoing. It has a clear end.

Strong examples include:

  • Installing or repairing specialised equipment bought from overseas.
  • A short project that needs one expert, such as a technical audit.
  • A short-term specialist contract for a defined task.

Weak examples include:

  • Routine or day-to-day jobs.
  • Roles that fill a staff gap.
  • Work that has no clear end date.

Other Streams of the Subclass 400 Visa

There may also be an Invited Participant stream and an Australia's Interest stream for special cases, such as certain events or exceptional circumstances. Check the current streams on the Home Affairs website before you plan.

Work the Subclass 400 Visa Does Not Cover

  • Entertainment and performing work: this falls under a different visa, subclass 408.
  • Workplace training: this falls under the training visa, subclass 407.
  • Long-term or ongoing jobs: these need a visa such as the 482.
  • Business meetings only: these may suit a visitor visa instead.

Subclass 400 Visa Eligibility: Who Can Apply?

Home Affairs looks at your skills, the job and your own background. Here are the main rules.

Requirements for the Applicant

  • You have specialised skills, knowledge or experience that help an Australian business.
  • Those skills cannot reasonably be found in the Australian workforce.
  • The work is non-ongoing and tied to a specific task or project.
  • An Australian organisation has invited or supported you.
  • You are outside Australia when you apply and when the visa is decided.
  • You meet health and character requirements.
  • You can support yourself and have health insurance.
  • You have not had a past visa cancelled or refused in Australia.
  • You genuinely intend to leave when the work ends.

What the Employer or Host Must Show

  • A real, temporary need for the specialist.
  • A finite project with a clear end.
  • That the skills cannot reasonably be sourced locally in the time available.
  • That the visa will not take opportunities from Australian citizens or permanent residents.

Evidence to Gather

  • Passport and identity documents.
  • An invitation or support letter from the Australian organisation.
  • A contract or letter of offer.
  • An updated CV, licences and qualifications.
  • A plain description of the work, with start and end dates.
  • Proof of why the skills are hard to find locally.
  • Proof of funds and health insurance.
  • Proof you will return home, such as a job or a return ticket.

Common Red Flags

Case officers may look closely at these:

  • A vague job description.
  • Routine duties dressed up as "specialised".
  • A role that extends past its original scope.
  • The same person coming back again and again for similar work.
  • Payment arrangements that do not make sense.

So be specific. Show why this person, and why now.

Can You Apply from Inside Australia?

No. You must apply from outside Australia. Applying from inside Australia is a known cause of delay or refusal.

Which Visa Fits Your Situation?

Use this quick guide:

  • A short, one-off specialist task: the 400 may fit.
  • An ongoing role or a longer stay: a 482 or another sponsored visa.
  • Meetings or talks, but no work: a business visitor visa.
  • Performing or entertainment work: subclass 408.
  • Workplace training: the training visa, subclass 407.

Do not use a 400 to avoid sponsorship. Home Affairs can check whether the work is really short and specialised. If the job is ongoing, a 400 can lead to a refusal or a cancellation.

Can You Move from a 400 to a 482 or Another Visa?

A 400 does not lead to PR. You may be able to apply for another visa later. But some 400 grants carry a "no further stay" condition, known as 8503. That limits other applications while you are in Australia.

So plan the next step before you travel. Ask your agent to check your grant letter.

How to Apply for the Subclass 400 Visa

The application is online and quite quick. The hard part is the business case, not the form.

Step-by-Step Application Process

  1. Check the work fits. It must be short, non-ongoing and highly specialised.
  2. Get an invitation or support letter. The Australian organisation writes it and names the role and dates.
  3. Plan the stay. If you need more than 3 months, build a strong business case.
  4. Gather your evidence. Use the checklist above.
  5. Apply online through ImmiAccount. Do this from outside Australia.
  6. Pay the charge. See the cost section below.
  7. Add family members, if eligible. They apply on the same form.
  8. Respond fast to any request. Home Affairs may ask for health exams or police certificates.
  9. Wait for the grant. Do not book non-refundable flights before then.
  10. Travel inside your entry window. Follow your visa conditions.

How to Write a Strong Business Case

  • Say what the job is, in plain words.
  • Explain why local workers cannot do it in time.
  • Give start and end dates.
  • Link the skills to the person's real record.
  • State what happens when the work ends.

After the Grant

Your grant notice sets the stay period, conditions and arrival deadline. The visa is linked to your passport, so there is no sticker. Keep the grant email. Arrive within the stated window, because the stay clock starts at your first entry and does not reset if you leave and return.

Other Costs to Plan For

  • Health examinations, if requested.
  • Police certificates, if requested.
  • Document translations.
  • Health insurance for the whole stay.
  • Flights and local living costs.
  • Agent fees, which vary by provider.

Fees are generally not refunded if the visa is refused. So make sure the application is complete before you lodge.

Is the 400 Cheaper than a 482?

Yes, by a lot. A 482 visa charge is A$4,015 from 1 July 2026, and the employer also pays a nomination charge and a levy. But the 400 only suits short work. Price should never decide the visa. The type of work should.

How Long Does a Subclass 400 Visa Take?

Home Affairs does not give a fixed time. As a guide:

  • Decision-ready applications take about 2 to 4 weeks.
  • About 90% are decided within roughly 20 days to 6 weeks, depending on the month.
  • Incomplete applications, or ones that need health or character checks, take longer.

These are estimates, not promises. So apply at least two months before the work starts.

What Slows an Application Down

  • A vague job description.
  • A missing invitation letter.
  • Weak proof that the skills are rare.
  • Missing health or police documents.
  • Slow replies to Home Affairs.

Subclass 400 Visa Conditions, Family and Rules to Follow

The 400 comes with strict conditions. Breaking them can lead to cancellation.

Common Visa Conditions

These are the main conditions:

  • Condition 8107: work only for the named organisation and in the named role.
  • Condition 8303: do not take part in activities that harm the community.
  • Condition 8503 (no further stay): may apply. It limits other visa applications while you are in Australia.
  • Condition 8101: included family members cannot work.

Check your grant letter. It lists the conditions that apply to you.

Rules You Should Remember

  • Do the approved work only.
  • Keep valid health insurance.
  • Do not stay past your approved period.
  • Leave when the work is done.
  • Changes to the job or employer generally need approval first.
  • The stay is counted from your first entry, and it does not restart when you leave and return.

Can You Extend a Subclass 400 Visa?

Generally no. Extensions are rare. Plan the whole job inside the stay. If the project grows, ask your agent about a different visa before your time ends.

Can You Bring Your Family?

Yes, eligible family can apply with you. They must meet health and character rules. They usually cannot work, and they may study for up to three months. You also need to show you can support them.

Time Spent in Australia: The 12-Month Rule of Thumb

Since 2024, Home Affairs has closely reviewed time spent on 400 visas in any 12-month period. Generally only one 400 is approved in 12 months. Treat repeat visits with care. If you need to return often, a different visa may fit better.

Subclass 400 vs Subclass 444: Not the Same Visa

People often mix these up. Subclass 444 is the Special Category visa for New Zealand citizens. It is not a permanent visa. It is a temporary visa, but New Zealanders can stay indefinitely while they remain citizens.

Since 1 July 2023, New Zealand citizens on a 444 can apply for citizenship directly, after about four years in Australia. The 400 has no such path.

Why Subclass 400 Visa Applications Get Refused

Most refusals link to one thing. Home Affairs is not sure the work is short and highly specialised.

Common Refusal Reasons

  • The work looks ongoing, not one-off.
  • The skills do not look rare or specialised.
  • The invitation or contract is missing or vague.
  • The application was made from inside Australia.
  • Health, character or funds requirements are not met.
  • A past visa was cancelled or refused.
  • The stay looks longer than the task needs.

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using the 400 for a normal job. Routine work belongs on a sponsored visa.
  • Copying a generic job description. Be specific about the task and the dates.
  • Asking for six months with no reason. Three months is usual. Longer stays need a strong case.
  • Booking flights first. Wait for the grant.
  • Planning to extend. Extensions are rare.
  • Ignoring conditions. Work only in the named role.
  • Using an old fee guide. The charge changed on 1 July 2026.

What to Do If Your Visa Is Refused

Read the refusal letter first. It lists the reasons, and it says if you can ask for a review. For temporary visas lodged from overseas, review rights are often limited, so ask an agent.

Then fix the problem. You may be able to lodge a new application with a clearer business case. If the job is really ongoing, a sponsored visa may fit better.

Frequently Asked Questions About the Subclass 400 Visa

What Is the Difference Between Visa 400 and 482?

The 400 is for short, one-off specialist work, with no sponsor. You usually stay up to 3 months. The 482 is for an ongoing role, needs an approved sponsor, lasts up to 4 years and can lead to PR. The charge is A$535 for the 400 and A$4,015 for the 482.

How Long Does a Subclass 400 Visa Take?

Decision-ready applications take about 2 to 4 weeks, and about 90% are decided within roughly 20 days to 6 weeks. Incomplete cases take longer. Apply at least two months before the work starts.

How Much Is a Subclass 400 Visa?

From 1 July 2026, the charge is A$535 for the main applicant and each extra adult, and A$135 for each child under 18. Health checks and other costs are extra.

Is Subclass 444 a Permanent Visa?

No. Subclass 444 is the Special Category visa for New Zealand citizens, and it is described as temporary. But New Zealanders can stay indefinitely while they remain citizens. Since 1 July 2023, they can apply directly for citizenship after about four years.

How Long Can I Stay on a Subclass 400 Visa?

Usually up to 3 months. In limited cases, up to 6 months with a strong business case. The stay counts from your first entry and does not restart if you leave and return.

Can I Work for Any Employer on a 400 Visa?

No. You can only do the approved work for the named organisation. Changing the job or employer generally needs approval first.

Can I Apply for a Subclass 400 Visa from Inside Australia?

No. You must be outside Australia when you apply and when the visa is decided.

Can a 400 Visa Lead to PR?

No. It is a short-stay visa with no path to permanent residence. If you want to stay longer, look at a sponsored or skilled visa instead.

Can I Extend a Subclass 400 Visa?

Generally no. Extensions are rare, so plan the whole job within the approved stay.

Can My Family Come with Me on a 400 Visa?

Yes, eligible family members can apply with you. They usually cannot work, and they must meet health and character rules.

In Short

The subclass 400 visa is a quick, short-stay visa for specialist work that has a clear end. It needs no sponsor, but it needs an invitation, strong evidence and about A$535 from 1 July 2026. It does not lead to PR and it is rarely extended.

Make sure the job is truly short and specialised. Apply from outside Australia, and give yourself time before the work starts. If you are unsure whether the 400 or a 482 fits, speak with a MARA-registered agent before you lodge.
