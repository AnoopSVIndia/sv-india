Permanent Residence (Skilled Regional) Visa 191: Requirements, Evidence and the Move From 491 or 494

The Permanent Residence (Skilled Regional) visa (subclass 191) is the permanent residence step for people who hold a Skilled Work Regional (491) or Skilled Employer Sponsored Regional (494) visa. There is no points test or Expression of Interest, but there are firm rules on timing, regional living and evidence. This guide explains them.

The Basics

What Is the Permanent Residence (Skilled Regional) Visa 191?

The visa that turns a regional provisional visa into permanent residence.

The subclass 191 visa is a permanent visa for people who have lived and worked in regional Australia on a Skilled Work Regional (491) or Skilled Employer Sponsored Regional (494) visa. It began accepting applications in November 2022. You do not need a nominating state or employer for the 191, you do not take a points test and there is no SkillSelect Expression of Interest. You lodge an application online through ImmiAccount once you meet the requirements.

The 191 gives you permanent residence, which means you can live, work and study anywhere in Australia, not only in a regional area, enrol in Medicare and sponsor eligible relatives. It is the main reason many people choose the regional route in the first place, so planning for it from the day your 491 or 494 is granted is the best way to avoid problems.

Eligibility

Eligible visaYou must hold a Skilled Work Regional (491) or Skilled Employer Sponsored Regional (494) visa when you apply.

Regional Living

Designated Regional Areas and Condition 8579

Where you live and work while on the 491 or 494 is the heart of the 191.

The 191 is built on the idea that you stay in regional Australia while on your provisional visa. Designated regional areas are defined by postcode, and the Department of Home Affairs publishes the list. Sydney, Melbourne and Brisbane are generally excluded. You can move between designated regional areas, but moving to a non-regional area can put your visa at risk and may stop you qualifying for the 191.

Keep clear records of your regional address, such as lease or ownership documents, utility bills and employer letters. Time overseas is not itself a breach, but you should be able to show that your home and your work remained in a designated regional area. If you are on a 494, you must also work in your nominated occupation for your sponsoring employer, apart from limited exceptions, and your sponsor and employment records matter.

Evidence

ATO Notices of Assessment and Other Evidence

The most common reason a 191 application stalls is missing or incomplete evidence.

  • ATO notices of assessment: for three relevant income years during your provisional visa, including any amended notices. Lodge your tax returns on time each year, because you cannot produce a notice for a year you did not lodge.
  • Evidence of regional residence: lease or ownership documents, utility bills, bank statements and similar documents showing a regional address.
  • Evidence of work: payslips, employer letters and, for a 494, details of your sponsor and nominated occupation.
  • ATO debt evidence: proof of payment or an approved payment arrangement for any ATO debt.
  • Identity and relationship documents: passports and, for family members, marriage, de facto or birth certificates.
  • Health and police checks: health examinations for you and your family members, and police certificates for relevant countries.

Check which three income years the Department accepts for your visa period, because the rule counts years relevant to your provisional visa. Your agent will confirm this against your grant date.

Family

Including Your Partner and Children in a 191 Application

Your partner and dependent children can be included in your 191 application. Family members who were on your 491 or 494, and in many cases those you added afterwards, can usually be included, and you can generally add family members before a decision is made. Each person must meet the health and character requirements, and you will need relationship and birth evidence, such as marriage, de facto or birth certificates.

Children who turn 18 or 23 during the provisional period may face extra dependency requirements, so check your children's dates early. If a partner meets the criteria in their own right, they may be able to apply as the main applicant. We check your family's position before you lodge.

After the Grant

What the 191 Visa Gives You

Permanent

Live, work and study anywhere

You are no longer limited to a designated regional area and can change employer, state or city.

Benefits

Medicare and sponsorship

You can enrol in Medicare and may sponsor eligible relatives for permanent residence, subject to their own requirements.

Travel and citizenship

Five-year travel facility

Your travel facility lasts five years from grant. After that you need a Resident Return visa to re-enter as a permanent resident, unless you become a citizen. You may be eligible for citizenship once you meet the residence rules.

Timing

How Long Does the 191 Visa Take?

The Department of Home Affairs publishes processing times for the 191. Recent migration agent reports say the Department finalises about 90% of applications within around 10 months, and that it is currently working on applications lodged earlier in 2026. This is a guide and not a promise. A complete application, with all evidence and checks ready at lodgement, is the most useful thing you can do to avoid delay.

Check the Department's live processing tool before you plan travel or other major decisions. The Department does not provide individual progress updates while an application is being assessed, and it may ask you for more information through ImmiAccount.

Avoid These

Common 191 Visa Mistakes

Lodging too early

The three years run from the grant date of your provisional visa, and an early application is not valid.

Leaving the regional area

Living or working outside a designated regional area can breach condition 8579 and put your pathway at risk.

Missing tax notices

If you did not lodge a tax return for a year, you cannot produce a notice of assessment for it.

Ignoring ATO debt

Unpaid debt should be repaid or covered by an approved payment arrangement before you lodge.

Changing employer on a 494

494 holders must generally work for the sponsoring employer in the nominated occupation, so check before any change.

Overlooking family dates

Children who turn 18 or 23 during the provisional period can face extra requirements.

Common Questions

Subclass 191 Visa FAQs

What is the subclass 191 visa?

The Permanent Residence (Skilled Regional) visa (subclass 191) is a permanent visa for people who have held a Skilled Work Regional (491) or Skilled Employer Sponsored Regional (494) visa and met the residence and evidence requirements. It lets you live, work and study anywhere in Australia.

Who can apply for the 191 visa?

You must hold a 491 or 494 visa, have held it for at least three years, have complied with its conditions, including living and working in a designated regional area, and provide the required Australian Taxation Office (ATO) notices of assessment. No points test, nomination or Expression of Interest is needed.

Is there a minimum income for the 191 visa?

Home Affairs removed the minimum taxable income threshold for the 191 in June 2023, according to recent migration agent guides. You still need ATO notices of assessment for three relevant income years, and any ATO debt should be paid or covered by a payment arrangement. Check the current rule on the Department of Home Affairs website before you lodge.

Can I apply before three years have passed?

No. The three years are counted from the grant date of your 491 or 494 visa, and an application lodged too early is not valid. Check your exact date before you lodge.

Can I include my partner and children?

Yes. Your partner and dependent children can be included if they meet the family-member rules and the health and character requirements. Family members can usually be added before a decision is made. Children who turn 18 or 23 during the provisional period may face extra dependency requirements.

How much does the 191 visa cost?

The visa application charge from 1 July 2026 is reported as AUD 630 for the main applicant, AUD 315 for each additional applicant aged 18 or over and AUD 160 for each child under 18. Charges change each 1 July and the rate on the lodgement date applies, so confirm the amount before you pay.

How long does the 191 visa take?

The Department of Home Affairs publishes processing times for the 191, and recent reports say about 90% of applications are finalised within 10 months. This is a guide and not a promise, so check the Department's live tool and make sure your application is complete when you lodge.

What can I do after I get the 191 visa?

You become a permanent resident, with the right to live, work and study in Australia, enrol in Medicare, and sponsor eligible relatives. Your travel facility lasts five years from grant, after which you need a Resident Return visa to re-enter as a permanent resident, unless you become a citizen. You may be able to apply for citizenship if you meet the residence requirements.
