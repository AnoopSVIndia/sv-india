Visitor Visa Refusal Australia: What to Do Next

Quick Answer: If your visitor visa was refused, read the refusal letter first. Then choose between applying again with stronger evidence or, if the letter says you have review rights, asking the Administrative Review Tribunal to review the decision. A visitor visa refusal is upsetting. But it is not always the end of your plans. Many people are granted a visa on a later try.

This guide explains why refusals happen, what to do next, and whether you can reapply or appeal. It also answers whether you get money back.

What to Do After a Visitor Visa Refusal

Your First Steps

Take these steps in order:

  1. Read your refusal letter slowly, from start to end.
  2. Note the exact reasons and the clause numbers given.
  3. Check whether the letter says you can ask for a review, and by what date.
  4. Check your current visa status and when your stay must end.
  5. Match each reason with the evidence you could provide next time.
  6. Get advice before you lodge anything new.

Do not rush a new application. A repeat with the same documents usually gets the same result.

If You Are Outside Australia

Your main choice is usually to reapply with better evidence. Most refusals made outside Australia cannot be reviewed. Some Sponsored Family stream refusals can be reviewed if the sponsor applies, so check your letter.

If You Are in Australia

Your time limits matter most. Check your visa end date and any review deadline in your letter. Also read the section 48 rules below, because they can limit what you can apply for while you are onshore.

Why Was Your Visitor Visa Refused?

Genuine Temporary Entrant Concerns

This is the most common reason. The officer was not satisfied that you will visit temporarily and then go home. Weak ties to your home country, such as no job, property, study or close family, can lead to this.

Not Enough Money

You must show you can pay for your trip and your return. Officers look at bank statements, pay slips, tax records or sponsor support. Gaps or sudden large deposits can raise doubts.

Unclear Plans or Visa History

A vague purpose, a missing itinerary or inconsistent answers can cause a refusal. So can past refusals, overstays or breaches of visa conditions.

False or Misleading Documents

Giving false or misleading documents or information is serious. It can lead to refusal under Public Interest Criterion 4020 and may block future visa applications for a period.

Health, Character and Missed Requests

You can also be refused if you do not meet the health or character requirements. Missing a deadline for more information, biometrics or a health exam is another common cause.

Visitor Visa 600 Refused: Can I Apply Again?

Can I Reapply for a Visitor Visa After Refusal?

Usually, yes. A refusal does not stop you from lodging a new Visitor visa (subclass 600) application. But a new application is judged on its own evidence. So applying again with nothing new will likely give the same result.

How Long After a Visitor Visa Refusal Can I Apply?

There is no set waiting period in the law for most people. Some advisers suggest waiting a few weeks to several months. The time you need depends on how long it takes to fix the problems in your refusal letter.

If your refusal involved false or misleading information, a longer bar can apply. Get advice first.

How to Make Your Next Application Stronger

  • Answer every refusal reason with specific, dated evidence.
  • Show your ties to home, such as work, study, property and family.
  • Update your bank records and explain any large deposits.
  • Add a clear, day-by-day travel plan.
  • Be honest about the earlier refusal. You must declare it.

The Section 48 Bar If You Are in Australia

If your visa was refused while you were in Australia, section 48 can stop you from applying for most visas onshore, including a Visitor visa. In that case you generally need to leave and apply from outside Australia. Some visas are exceptions, so get advice before you lodge.

New Rule for Visitor Visas from 28 November 2026

Regulations registered on 13 November 2026 make condition 8503 (No Further Stay) mandatory for visitor visas, ETAs and eVisitors lodged on or after 28 November 2026. If you are granted a visa after that date, you will generally not be able to apply for another visa onshore. Check the Home Affairs site for the latest position.

Can You Appeal a Visitor Visa Refusal?

When a Visitor Visa Refusal Can Be Reviewed

Sometimes. The Administrative Review Tribunal (ART) can review certain refusals. It replaced the AAT in October 2024. Whether you can ask for a review depends on the visa stream and where you were when you applied.

Your refusal letter must say if the decision can be reviewed.

When It Cannot Be Reviewed

Most visitor visa refusals made outside Australia cannot be reviewed. For those, the usual path is a new application with better evidence.

Some Sponsored Family stream refusals are different. A sponsor who is an Australian citizen or permanent resident may be able to ask for a review.

Time Limits for a Visitor Visa Refusal Appeal

The time limit is strict. It is stated in your refusal letter. Many migration decisions have a 28-day limit, but some are shorter. A late application is invalid.

The ART also charges a fee. Check the ART website for the current amount.

What About Court?

If the ART upholds the refusal, you may be able to go to court. The court only checks for a legal error. It does not look at the facts again.

Do You Get Money Back If a Visa Is Refused?

The Visa Application Charge

Usually, no. Home Affairs says it does not usually refund the visa application charge, even if the application is refused or withdrawn. So plan for the cost of a second attempt.

Other Fees

Home Affairs also says fees are not refunded if your application, nomination or sponsorship was unsuccessful. This includes a change of mind.

Do not cancel or reverse a payment to try to get your money back. Home Affairs warns this can make an application invalid and may leave you with a debt to the Commonwealth. That debt can block future visas.

If you think you may qualify for an exception, ask Home Affairs directly.

Common Mistakes After a Visitor Visa Refusal

  • Reapplying straight away with the same documents.
  • Missing the review deadline in your refusal letter.
  • Hiding the refusal on your next application form.
  • Sending a generic reply that does not match the refusal reasons.
  • Lodging an onshore application when section 48 applies.
  • Using false or altered documents to "fix" a weak case.
  • Relying on a guide written before the 28 November 2026 visitor visa change.

Visitor Visa Refusal Australia FAQs

Can I Reapply for an Australia Visitor Visa if It Is Refused?

Usually, yes. There is no general ban on reapplying. But you should fix the reasons in your refusal letter first. A repeat with the same evidence will likely fail.

How Long After a Visitor Visa Refusal Can I Apply in Australia?

There is no set waiting period for most people. But if you were refused while in Australia, section 48 can limit what you can apply for onshore. You may need to apply from outside Australia.

What Should I Do if My Visitor Visa Was Refused?

Read your refusal letter, note the reasons and any review deadline, and check your visa status. Then decide whether to reapply with stronger evidence or seek review if the letter allows it.

Do You Get Money Back if a Visa Is Rejected in Australia?

Usually not. Home Affairs says it does not usually refund the visa application charge, even if the application is refused.

Can You Appeal a Visitor Visa Refusal?

Sometimes. Most refusals made outside Australia cannot be reviewed. Some onshore refusals and some Sponsored Family stream refusals can be taken to the Administrative Review Tribunal. Check your letter.

Do I Have to Declare a Previous Refusal?

Yes. You must declare previous refusals in future applications. Leaving it out can cause new problems.

Will a Refusal Affect My Future Applications?

It can. The refusal stays on your record. A strong, honest application can still succeed later.

Can My Sponsor Appeal for Me?

In some Sponsored Family stream cases, yes. An Australian citizen or permanent resident sponsor may be able to seek review. Check the refusal letter.

Does the New 8503 Rule Affect Me?

It can. From 28 November 2026, a new visitor visa will generally carry condition 8503. This means you generally cannot apply for another visa onshore. Check the Home Affairs site for the latest.

In Short

A visitor visa refusal is not always final. Read your refusal letter, check your deadlines, and fix the exact reasons before you act. Reapplying is often possible, and some refusals can be reviewed. But the visa application charge is usually not refunded.

Take your time, keep your evidence honest, and get advice on your own case. A stronger, well-prepared application is your best next step.
