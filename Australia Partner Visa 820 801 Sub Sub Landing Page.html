Partner Visa (Subclass 820/801): Eligibility, Evidence, Cost and the Two-Stage Process

The Partner visa (subclass 820/801) lets the spouse or de facto partner of an Australian citizen, permanent resident or eligible New Zealand citizen apply from inside Australia for temporary, then permanent, residence. It is one application with two stages, and it is decided on how genuine your relationship is. This guide explains how to prepare a strong application.

The Basics

What Is the Partner Visa (Subclass 820/801)?

One application, two stages, and a decision based on a genuine relationship.

The subclass 820 and the subclass 801 are the onshore Partner visas. You lodge a single application and pay a single visa application charge, and it covers both stages. The 820 is a temporary visa granted while the Department of Home Affairs assesses your relationship. The 801 is the permanent visa, which is generally assessed about two years after you lodged the combined application, once the Department is satisfied that your relationship is still genuine and continuing.

The 820 and 801 are for people who are in Australia. If your partner and you are overseas, the equivalent pair is the Partner (Provisional) visa subclass 309 and the Partner (Migrant) visa subclass 100, which follow the same two-stage logic. If you are engaged and overseas, the Prospective Marriage visa (subclass 300) lets you enter Australia to marry, after which you can apply onshore for the 820 and 801. Married couples, de facto couples and same-sex couples are assessed against the same standards.

Eligibility

Partner Visa 820/801 Eligibility Requirements

You need to meet every requirement below, and your sponsor must meet theirs.

  • Relationship: you are the spouse or de facto partner of your sponsor, in a genuine, continuing and exclusive relationship.
  • Sponsor: your sponsor is an Australian citizen, an Australian permanent resident or an eligible New Zealand citizen, and their sponsorship is approved by the Department.
  • Location: you are in Australia when you lodge the application, and when the 820 is decided.
  • De facto partners: generally at least 12 months in the relationship before you lodge, unless the relationship is registered or an exception applies.
  • Visa status: you hold a substantive visa or a bridging visa that allows an onshore application, or you meet the exceptions in the law. Some visas, such as those carrying condition 8503 (No Further Stay), block an onshore application unless the condition is waived.
  • Health: you and any included family members meet the Australian health requirement.
  • Character: you provide police certificates and meet the character requirement.
  • No debts: you have no outstanding debts to the Australian Government.

*Registering a de facto relationship with a state or territory relationship register, where one is available, can meet the 12-month requirement. Register availability differs by state and territory, so confirm it before you rely on it.

Your Evidence

Relationship Evidence for a Partner Visa: What the Department Looks At

Strong applications show the relationship in real life, across all four areas.

The four areas the Department considers
AreaWhat it coversExamples of evidence
Financial aspectsHow you share money and financial responsibilitiesJoint bank accounts, shared bills, a joint lease or loan, evidence of paying for things together
Nature of the householdHow you live together and share domestic lifeShared address on mail and records, household arrangements, shared chores and responsibilities
Social aspectsHow others see you as a couplePhotos, joint invitations, travel together, statements from friends and family (Form 888)
Nature of commitmentYour commitment to a shared lifeMessages and calls, plans for the future, how you support each other, wills or insurance naming each other

Statutory Declarations on Form 888

Statements from friends and family who know you as a couple are a standard part of the social evidence. They are usually made on Form 888 by Australian citizens or permanent residents, and applications commonly include at least two. Statements work best when they describe specific events the witness saw, not general praise.

Evidence Is About Quality

A smaller set of consistent, dated evidence usually works better than a very large unorganised pile. We help you choose what shows your relationship most clearly, and spot gaps, such as a short time at the same address, so you can explain them honestly.

Your Partner

Partner Visa Sponsor Requirements

Your partner's own eligibility matters as much as yours.

Your sponsor must be an Australian citizen, an Australian permanent resident or an eligible New Zealand citizen, usually aged 18 or over. They lodge a sponsorship application, and the Department must approve it before your visa can be granted. The Department also looks at the sponsor's character, so serious convictions can affect the outcome.

There are limits on how often a person can sponsor a partner. Generally a sponsor can sponsor no more than two partners in their lifetime, and a new sponsorship is generally not allowed within five years of a previous one. Compelling and compassionate circumstances can sometimes waive these limits, so a sponsor with a previous partner visa history should get advice before you invest in an application.

While You Wait

Bridging Visa A and Your Rights While the Partner Visa Is Processed

Lodging on time keeps you lawfully in Australia.

If you lodge an onshore Partner visa application while you hold a substantive visa, you normally receive a Bridging Visa A that starts when your current visa ends. A Bridging Visa A generally lets you work and study, but it does not let you leave and return to Australia. If you need to travel, you must apply for a Bridging Visa B before you go, with its own fee, and you should get advice first because leaving without one can end your right to return on that visa.

Once the 820 is granted you have full work rights. Because bridging visa conditions depend on your visa history, we check yours before you lodge.

Get Organised

Documents to Prepare for a Partner Visa Application

Not every couple needs every item, but this list covers what most files require.

  • Passports and identity documents for you, your partner and any children.
  • Your marriage certificate, or evidence of your de facto relationship or relationship registration.
  • Evidence of your sponsor's citizenship, permanent residence or New Zealand status.
  • Joint financial records, such as bank statements, bills, a lease or a loan.
  • Evidence of living together, such as mail, tenancy records and household documents.
  • Photos, travel records and messages that show the relationship over time.
  • Form 888 statutory declarations from friends and family.
  • Police certificates for each country where you have lived for 12 months or more in the last 10 years.
  • Health examination results for you and any included family members.
  • Birth certificates for any children.

Timing

How Long Does the Partner Visa Take?

The Department of Home Affairs publishes processing times for the 820 and the 801 separately, and they change as demand and workloads change. Figures published at the end of September 2026 and reported by migration practitioners vary, with about half of onshore 820 applications decided within roughly 18 to 22 months, and nine in ten within roughly 30 to 32 months. The 801 clock does not start until the stage becomes assessable, about two years after you lodged the combined application.

We do not quote one number because published figures differ between sources and the Department updates them regularly. Check the live processing times tool before you plan around a date. Complete, well-organised evidence is the main part of the timeline you control, and complex cases can take longer.

If Things Change

If the Relationship Ends or Circumstances Change

The 801 generally cannot be granted if your relationship has ended before the decision. There are limited exceptions, including where the sponsor has committed family violence against you, where the sponsor has died, or where you share a child and have ongoing residence, contact or maintenance obligations. The family violence provisions were widened in 2024, and they use their own evidence rules.

If your relationship is in difficulty, you are experiencing family violence, or you are unsure about your options, speak to us or a qualified professional early. Waiting can reduce your choices. If you are in danger, contact emergency services first.

Avoid These

Common Partner Visa Mistakes That Cause Delays or Refusals

Thin or Mismatched Evidence

Dates, addresses and details that do not match across documents raise doubts. Check everything lines up before lodging.

Lodging a De Facto Application Too Early

Without 12 months, registration or an exception, an application can fail. Check the timeline first.

Ignoring the Sponsor's History

Sponsorship limits and character issues can stop an application, so check them before you pay.

Weak Form 888 Statements

General praise helps little. Ask witnesses to describe specific events they saw.

Travelling Without a Bridging Visa B

Leaving Australia on a Bridging Visa A without a Bridging Visa B can end your right to return on that visa.

Letting Evidence Go Stale

The 801 stage needs recent evidence. Keep collecting joint records through the whole two years.

Common Questions

Partner Visa (820/801) FAQs

What is the difference between the 820 and the 801 partner visa?

The 820 is the temporary stage and the 801 is the permanent stage of the same onshore application. You lodge once and pay one visa application charge. About two years after you lodge, the Department assesses whether the relationship is still genuine and continuing before it considers the 801.

How much does the partner visa cost in 2026?

From 1 July 2026 the charge for the main applicant is AUD 11,710, with AUD 5,860 for each additional applicant aged 18 or over and AUD 2,935 for each applicant under 18. It covers both the 820 and the 801. The charge is generally not refunded if the application is refused, so confirm the current amount in ImmiAccount before you lodge.

Do I have to be in Australia to apply for the 820?

Yes. The 820 is the onshore application, so you must be in Australia when you lodge and when the 820 is decided. If you are overseas, the equivalent pair is the Partner (Provisional) visa subclass 309 and the Partner (Migrant) visa subclass 100.

Do we need to have been together for 12 months if we are not married?

De facto partners generally need to show a relationship of at least 12 months before lodging, unless an exception applies. Registering the relationship with a state or territory register, where one is available, can meet this requirement, and some compelling circumstances may also be considered.

Who can sponsor me for a partner visa?

Your sponsor must be an Australian citizen, an Australian permanent resident or an eligible New Zealand citizen, usually aged 18 or over. The sponsorship must be approved by the Department. There are limits on how many partners a person can sponsor, so we check your sponsor's history early.

Can I work and study while I wait for the partner visa?

Generally yes. When you lodge an onshore application while holding a substantive visa, you normally receive a Bridging Visa A, which generally lets you work and study. It does not let you travel and return, so you need a separate Bridging Visa B before leaving Australia. The 820, once granted, gives full work rights.

What evidence does the Department look at?

It looks at the genuineness of the relationship across four areas: the financial aspects, the nature of the household, the social aspects and the nature of your commitment to each other. Statutory declarations from friends and family on Form 888 are a common part of the social evidence.

What happens if our relationship ends before the 801 is decided?

Generally the 801 cannot be granted if the relationship has ended. There are limited exceptions, including family violence by the sponsor, the death of the sponsor, or where you share a child and have ongoing obligations. Get advice early if your circumstances change.
