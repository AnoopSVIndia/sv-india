Carer Visa (Subclass 116 and 836): Who Qualifies, How the Queue Works and What to Consider First

The Carer visa is a permanent visa for a person who must provide long-term care to an Australian relative with a medical condition. It is one of the hardest family visas to obtain. Places are capped, applications join a queue, and the Department of Home Affairs currently estimates 16 years for a new application. This guide explains the rules honestly so you can decide.

The Basics

What Is the Carer Visa (Subclass 116 and 836)?

A permanent visa built around one person caring for another.

The Carer visa lets a person move to Australia permanently to give long-term care to a relative who has a medical condition, where nobody else can reasonably provide that care. There are two versions. The Carer visa (subclass 116) is for applicants who are outside Australia when they apply, and the Carer visa (subclass 836) is for applicants who are in Australia. The eligibility rules are otherwise the same.

The visa is for a capable carer. It is not for a person who is unwell and wants to be cared for in Australia, and it is not a way to reunite with family for emotional support. The Department of Home Affairs looks closely at the medical evidence, the carer's ability and whether other relatives or services in Australia could provide the care instead.

The Queue

The Carer Visa Queue: What the Department of Home Affairs Says

This is the most important fact on the page, so we give it first.

Carer visa capping and queuing, as published by the Department of Home Affairs
ItemCurrent position
Capped and queuedCarer applications are capped and queued. Once the year's places are used, remaining applications wait for a place in a future program year.
How applications are assessedApplications are assessed in lodgement date order against core criteria, including health and character. Eligible applications receive a queue date, and applications that do not meet the core criteria are refused.
Queue release dateAs at 31 August 2026, applications with a queue date up to 31 December 2023 have been released for final processing.
Estimated time for a new application16 years, based on the current number of places each year. The Department generally updates this estimate yearly.
AcknowledgementApproximately 5 weeks for a new application.
Progress enquiriesThe Department does not respond to individual status enquiries. It writes to you when a queued application is released.

Because the wait is so long, the care need, the sponsor's circumstances and the family's plans can all change while you are in the queue. If the person who needs care dies or no longer needs care during a pending application, the Department must be told. Please read this section before you pay any charge.

Eligibility

Carer Visa 116 and 836 Eligibility Requirements

Both the carer and the person who needs care must meet the rules.

Main eligibility requirements

The Hard Parts

The Medical Assessment and the No-Other-Carer Test

Most refusals turn on these two points, not on paperwork.

Medical

A Real, Long-Term Condition

The condition must be long-term or permanent and need significant care for at least two years. Loneliness, depression or companionship alone does not meet the test. A medical assessment is required, and agent guides note that Bupa Medical Visa Services is commonly involved.

Alternatives

Others Cannot Reasonably Care

You must show that other relatives in Australia and local community, health or welfare services cannot reasonably provide the care. Family preference is not enough, and neither is a relative simply being busy or unwilling, without evidence.

The carer

A Capable Applicant

The visa is for a capable carer. It is not for an overseas applicant who is sick and needs care, and it does not work as an emergency visa.

Fit Check

Is the Carer Visa Right for You?

The Carer Visa May Suit You If

  • A close relative in Australia has a serious, long-term medical condition.
  • You are a capable carer who understands the care needed.
  • You have strong evidence that no one else can reasonably provide it.
  • You can plan around a very long wait.

Consider Another Route If

  • You need to be in Australia soon or in an emergency.
  • The need is companionship or emotional support only.
  • Other relatives or services could provide the care.
  • You qualify for a partner, skilled, employer-sponsored or student visa.

After the Grant

What the Carer Visa Gives You

Permanent

Permanent Residence

The Carer visa is a permanent visa, so a granted applicant can live in Australia permanently.

Benefits

Work, Study and Medicare

Holders can generally work and study in Australia and enrol in Medicare, subject to the rules that apply to permanent residents.

Later steps

Travel and Citizenship

A travel facility generally applies for five years from grant, and you may be able to apply for citizenship if you meet the residence requirements.

Costs

Carer Visa Cost

Expect a significant government charge, and plan for more than one stage.

The Carer visa has a high visa application charge, and a second charge can apply if your application passes the first stage. Charges are set by the Australian Government, usually change each 1 July and depend on the lodgement date. Because published agent figures differ and some are undated, we do not quote a single amount here. Check the Department of Home Affairs visa pricing tool or ImmiAccount for the current charge for you and each family member before you decide.

Health examinations, police certificates, medical reports and professional fees are separate, and the visa application charge is generally not refunded if an application is refused or if the wait outlasts your plans. This is another reason to get a candid assessment before you lodge.

Get Organised

Documents to Prepare for a Carer Visa Application

  • Passports and identity documents for you and any family members on the application.
  • Evidence of the relationship between you, the sponsor and the person who needs care.
  • Medical reports and evidence of the condition, and of the care needed day to day.
  • Evidence about other relatives in Australia and why they cannot reasonably provide the care.
  • Evidence about community, health and welfare services considered and why they are not suitable.
  • Sponsor's proof of citizenship, permanent residence or eligible New Zealand status, and financial evidence.
  • Police certificates and health examination documents for you and your family.
  • For onshore applicants, details of your current substantive visa.
Avoid These

Common Carer Visa Mistakes

Expecting a Fast Outcome

The Department estimates 16 years for a new application. It is not an emergency visa.

Applying While Unwell

The visa is for a capable carer, not for an applicant who needs care.

Weak Medical Evidence

A condition that does not clearly need significant care for at least two years can fail the test.

Ignoring Other Carers

Not showing why relatives and local services cannot reasonably help is a leading reason for refusal.

Not Telling the Department of Changes

A change in the care need, or the death of the person who needs care, must be reported.

Overlooking Other Visas

Another visa may suit you better, so check the options before you commit to a long queue.

Common Questions

Carer Visa (Subclass 116 and 836) FAQs

What is the Carer visa (subclass 116 and 836)?

The Carer visa is a permanent visa for a person who needs to provide long-term care to an Australian relative, or to a relative's family member, who has a medical condition. Subclass 116 is for applicants outside Australia and subclass 836 is for applicants inside Australia.

Who can sponsor me for a Carer visa?

The person who needs care, or a family member of the person who needs care, must generally be an Australian citizen, permanent resident or eligible New Zealand citizen aged 18 or over. The sponsor must show the care need and may need to show financial support for the carer. We check the exact sponsor rules for your case.

What medical condition qualifies for a Carer visa?

The condition must be long-term or permanent and cause a level of impairment that means the person needs significant care and assistance in daily life for at least two years. A medical assessment is required. Companionship, loneliness or emotional support alone does not meet the test.

Why does the Carer visa have such a long wait?

The Carer visa is capped and queued. Demand is higher than the number of places each year, so eligible applications are given a queue date and released in order. The Department of Home Affairs currently estimates 16 years for a new application and, as at 31 August 2026, was releasing applications with a queue date up to 31 December 2023.

Can I apply if I am the one who is unwell?

No. The Carer visa is for a capable applicant who will care for someone else. It is not a visa for an overseas applicant who needs care or treatment themselves.

What if other relatives or services could provide the care?

You must show that the care cannot reasonably be provided by other relatives in Australia or by community, health or welfare services. Family preference, or relatives being busy or unwilling, is not enough on its own, so evidence is critical.

Can I include my partner and children?

Family unit members may be able to be included if they meet the health and character requirements. Check the family member rules for your case before you lodge.

What are my options if I cannot wait for a Carer visa?

Options depend on your situation. They can include a temporary visitor visa for family visits, a temporary Sponsored Parent visa, a partner, skilled or employer-sponsored visa if you qualify, or a student visa. We explain the realistic options for you.
