Most couples become eligible two years after they lodge the combined application. It is not automatic, because you must prove your relationship is still genuine and continuing.

You lodged your partner visa. Now the long wait begins. Most couples ask the same question: what should we do with all this time?

This guide answers it. First, you will learn what the permanent partner visa is, when you can apply and how long it takes. Then you get a simple plan for what to collect, and when.

The plan works for both the 801 and the 100. Follow it and the permanent stage will feel calm instead of rushed.

What Is a Permanent Partner Visa?

A permanent partner visa lets the spouse or de facto partner of an Australian citizen, permanent resident or eligible New Zealand citizen live in Australia for good. It is the final step of the two-stage partner visa process.

You apply for both stages at the same time. You pay one government fee. However, Home Affairs decides each stage separately.

Is a Partner Visa 820 a Permanent Resident Visa?

No. The 820 is the temporary first stage. It lets you live, work and study in Australia while Home Affairs assesses your case. You become a permanent resident only when the permanent visa is granted.

Partner Visa 801 vs 100: Which One Is Yours?

Both visas give the same rights. The only difference is where you applied. Your permanent visa follows your temporary visa, so you do not choose it.

This guide uses "permanent partner visa" for both. Where a point only applies to one, we say so.

When Can You Apply for the Permanent Partner Visa?

In most cases, you become eligible two years after the date you lodged the combined application. The clock starts at lodgement. It does not start when your temporary visa is granted.

For example, say you lodged on 1 November 2024. Your usual eligibility date would be 1 November 2026. A late grant of the temporary visa does not push that date back.

Can You Get the Permanent Partner Visa Sooner?

Sometimes. You may not need to wait the full two years if your relationship was already long when you lodged. As a general guide, that means:

  • you had been together for three years, or
  • you had been together for two years and had a dependent child with your partner.

These rules have detail and exceptions. Check your own dates with a registered migration agent before you plan around them.

Do You Get the Permanent Partner Visa Automatically?

No. Reaching the two-year mark only makes you eligible for assessment. Home Affairs looks at your relationship again before it grants permanent residency.

For this reason, a break-up, weak evidence or old documents can still lead to a refusal at this stage.

Do You Have to Wait for an Invitation?

Usually you do not need to wait to be chased. Many agents advise sending your updated evidence close to the two-year mark. Home Affairs may also contact you through your ImmiAccount. Check it often.

Permanent Partner Visa Timeline: What to Do and When

The wait is long, but it is also your chance to build strong proof. Use this plan from the day you lodge. Dates below count from your lodgement date.

Months 0 to 3: Set Up Your System

First, make one safe folder for everything, on paper and online. Next, save a copy of your lodged application and all the evidence you sent.

Then watch your ImmiAccount. Keep your address, passport and contact details up to date. Tell Home Affairs about any change.

Months 3 to 12: Build a Steady Paper Trail

At this stage, you simply live your life together and keep the records. Do not wait until the last month.

  • Keep joint bank statements and shared bills.
  • Save your lease or mortgage papers with both names.
  • Keep post addressed to both of you at one address.
  • Take dated photos at events, trips and family gatherings.

Months 12 to 18: Add Proof of Your Shared Future

Now your evidence should show that the relationship has grown. Save travel records, holiday bookings and joint plans. Keep messages and emails from any time you are apart.

Also check your list of friends and family. You will need two people who know you both well. They must be 18 or over, and Australian citizens or permanent residents, to complete Form 888.

Months 18 to 22: Refresh the Hard Documents

By this point, you can start the slower tasks. Check that your passport is valid. Ask your sponsor to confirm they are still an Australian citizen, permanent resident or eligible New Zealand citizen.

In addition, you and your partner should each write a fresh personal statement. Cover how you share money, home life and plans. Be honest and specific.

The Last 2 Months Before Your Eligibility Date: Get Ready to Submit

First, order a new Australian Federal Police check. Also get police certificates from any country where you lived for 12 months or more in the last 10 years since turning 16.

Next, ask your two witnesses to sign their Form 888 declarations. Then check whether you owe any debt to the Australian Government, and sort it out.

Finally, put your updated evidence in order. Many applicants send it close to the two-year mark.

After You Submit: Respond Fast

Home Affairs may ask for more documents or a new medical check. Answer by the deadline in the request. Late replies are a common cause of delay.

What Evidence Do You Need for the Permanent Partner Visa?

Home Affairs looks at the same four areas it checked at the first stage. It wants new proof that covers the time since you lodged. Do not just resend old papers.

Strong evidence is recent, specific and consistent. It should match what you told Home Affairs the first time.

Permanent Partner Visa Document Checklist

Exact requirements vary by case. Most applicants need these items:

  • Updated relationship evidence from your temporary visa period
  • Two Form 888 statutory declarations
  • A new Australian Federal Police check, plus overseas police certificates if needed
  • Passport photo page, and birth and marriage certificates
  • Sponsor details and proof of their citizenship or residency
  • A health examination, if Home Affairs asks for one
  • Proof you have repaid, or arranged to repay, any debt to the Australian Government

If children are on your case, check the rules for adding them. A child who does not hold your temporary visa may first need a Dependent Child visa (subclass 445) and Form 1002. This must happen before the permanent decision.

What If Your Life Changes During the Wait?

Life does not pause for a visa. Here is how common changes can affect you.

If you move house: Update your address in your ImmiAccount. Keep proof of the new shared address.

If you have a baby: Tell Home Affairs. Keep the birth certificate. A child you share can also strengthen your evidence.

If your passport changes: Update the new details through your ImmiAccount.

If you want to travel: Once you hold the temporary visa, you can usually travel. On a bridging visa, check your travel rights first, because leaving can stop you coming back. Also plan carefully around your eligibility date, because you may need to be in Australia when the permanent visa is decided.

If your relationship ends: Get advice straight away. In some cases you may still qualify for the permanent visa. These include:

  • your sponsor was violent to you or to a dependent child (family violence),
  • your sponsor has died and you can show your relationship would have continued, or
  • you share custody of, or have access to, a dependent child with your sponsor.

These cases are complex, and each one is judged on its own facts. If this is you, speak to our team in confidence as early as you can.

How Long Does the Permanent Partner Visa Take?

The permanent stage is usually much faster than the first stage. Home Affairs counts the time from your eligibility date, which is normally two years after you lodged.

Home Affairs shares times as two numbers. One shows how long it took to finish 50% of recent cases. The other shows 90%. In September 2026, published figures for the 801 were roughly 5 months and 12 months.

These are not promises. Missing documents, extra checks and complex cases can all make it longer. Always check the live Home Affairs processing times guide for the latest figures.

Home Affairs also asks people not to chase for updates while their case is within the normal range. Use your ImmiAccount instead.

How Much Does the Permanent Partner Visa Cost?

There is no extra government fee for the permanent stage. It was paid when you lodged the combined application.

From 1 July 2026, the combined partner visa charge is A$11,710 for the main applicant. Extra applicants add to this. You may still pay for new police checks, medicals, translations or agent fees.

What Are the New Partner Visa Rules for Australia in 2026?

Two changes matter most for people on the 820/801 path.

  • Higher fee. The charge rose to A$11,710 on 1 July 2026. This affects new applications, not the permanent stage of an existing one.
  • Priority order. Since 25 July 2026, Home Affairs processes under Ministerial Direction 117. Onshore partner visas (820 and 801) get the highest priority.

In the sources we reviewed in November 2026, we found no change to the two-stage system or to the two-year rule. Rules move quickly, so confirm the current position before you rely on this.

Common Permanent Partner Visa Mistakes to Avoid

Most problems are simple and avoidable. Watch out for these:

  • Sending old evidence that does not cover the last two years
  • Leaving gaps, such as months with no joint records
  • Giving details that do not match your first application
  • Missing a deadline in a Home Affairs request
  • Adding a child too late, or in the wrong way
  • Letting your witnesses' details or your sponsor's status go out of date

A small error can mean delays or even a refusal. For that reason, many couples have their file checked before they submit.

What Do You Get with a Permanent Partner Visa?

Once granted, you become a permanent resident from that day. You can:

  • live, work and study in Australia without time limits,
  • enrol in Medicare,
  • join free English classes through the Adult Migrant English Program, if eligible,
  • sponsor eligible family members, and
  • travel in and out of Australia for five years from the grant date.

After five years, you need a Resident Return visa to keep returning as a permanent resident. You can check your travel dates on VEVO.

Can You Become an Australian Citizen After the Permanent Partner Visa?

Yes, if you meet the rules. In general, you need at least four years of lawful residence in Australia. The last 12 months must be as a permanent resident. You must also meet the character requirement and pass the citizenship test.

Time spent outside Australia is limited, so check your own dates with the Department's residence calculator.

Permanent Partner Visa FAQs

Is a partner visa 820 a permanent resident visa?

No. The 820 is temporary. You become a permanent resident when the 801 is granted.

How long does it take to get a permanent partner visa?

You usually wait two years from lodging to become eligible. After that, recent published times for the 801 have been roughly 5 to 12 months. Check the live Home Affairs guide.

What are the new partner visa rules for Australia in 2026?

The fee rose to A$11,710 on 1 July 2026. Since 25 July 2026, onshore partner visas get top priority under Ministerial Direction 117. We found no change to the two-year rule.

Do I get the permanent partner visa automatically after two years?

No. You become eligible for assessment, and Home Affairs checks your relationship again.

Do I need to pay again for the permanent stage?

No government fee is due again. You may pay for police checks, medicals or agent help.

Can I get the permanent partner visa in less than two years?

Sometimes. A three-year relationship, or two years with a child, at the time you lodged may help.

What happens if we break up before the permanent visa is granted?

You may still qualify in limited cases, such as family violence. Get advice quickly.

Do I need an invitation to submit my documents?

Not usually. Many couples send updated evidence near the two-year mark. Check your ImmiAccount for any request.

Is the partner visa 801 the same as the 100?

They give the same rights. The 801 follows the 820 (applied inside Australia). The 100 follows the 309 (applied outside).

Can I work and study while I wait?

Yes, once you hold the temporary visa. On a bridging visa, your work rights depend on the visa you hold.

Can I include my child in the permanent stage?

Yes, in many cases. A child may need a Dependent Child visa (subclass 445) and Form 1002 before the decision.

Do I need a migration agent or lawyer?

No, but many couples use one to check their evidence. Only a registered migration agent or lawyer can give you visa advice.

In Short

The permanent partner visa is the second stage of the application you already lodged: the 801 if you applied in Australia, or the 100 if you applied from outside. Most couples become eligible two years after lodging, and the grant is not automatic.

Use the wait to build clear, honest proof of your life together. Save records from day one, keep your ImmiAccount current, refresh your hard documents in the last few months and reply to Home Affairs on time.
