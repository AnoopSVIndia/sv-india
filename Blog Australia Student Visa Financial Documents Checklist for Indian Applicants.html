Quick Answer: You must show you can pay for your first-year course fees, 12 months of living costs and travel. For a single student, living costs are A$29,710. Prove it with clear bank statements, a loan sanction letter with proof of payout, or a parent's income papers such as tax returns, plus proof of your relationship.

Money is one of the top reasons for student visa trouble. Missing papers, new lump-sum deposits and unclear sponsors all raise questions.

This matters even more now. Home Affairs data shows student visa refusals rose to 24.2% in June 2026. A clean, well-matched money file helps you stand out.

Below, you get the amounts, a full checklist for Indian applicants, the sources that work and the ones that do not. You will also see how to use a parent's papers and the mistakes to avoid.

How Much Money Do You Need to Show for an Australia Student Visa?

Home Affairs asks you to show you have enough money for three things. These are your travel, your first 12 months of living costs and your course fees. If family members come with you, their costs count too.

The amounts below have applied since 10 May 2024. They were still current in November 2026. Always check the Home Affairs site before you lodge.

Living Costs for 12 Months

Who

Amount per year (A$)

You (main student)

29,710

Spouse or de facto partner

10,394

Each dependent child

4,449

School costs for each school-age child

13,502

Add Your Course Fees and Travel

You also need your first 12 months of tuition. Take away anything you have already paid, and show the receipt. Then add your return flights.

Here is a simple example for one student. Tuition of A$20,000, living costs of A$29,710 and flights of about A$2,000 come to about A$51,710. Your own total will depend on your course and your plans.

The Annual Income Option

You can also show that your parent or partner earns enough. The income must be at least A$87,856 in the 12 months before you apply. If family members come with you, it is at least A$102,500.

This route needs official tax documents, such as a tax assessment. Bank statements and employer letters alone are not accepted for it. You must also prove your relationship to that person.

Australia Student Visa Financial Documents Checklist for Indian Applicants

Use this checklist as a guide. You do not need every line. Pick the route that fits your family, then gather the papers for it.

Route

Documents to Gather

Core papers for everyone

Letter of offer and Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE), receipts for any fees already paid, a clear estimate of tuition, living costs and travel

Savings

Several months of bank statements in the name of the account holder, showing the bank name and account number, plus a record of how the money built up

Fixed deposits

Deposit certificate with the maturity date, and source-of-funds papers if it is new

Education loan

Sanction letter from a recognised bank showing the amount, interest rate and repayment terms, plus proof the money has been paid out

Parent or partner income

Latest income tax return and tax assessment, relationship proof such as a birth or marriage certificate, and ID papers

Parent or partner savings

Their bank statements, payslips or business records, and proof of where the money came from

Sponsor declaration

A signed letter or affidavit saying the sponsor will pay your costs

Large deposits or asset sales

Sale deed, bonus letter, loan record or transfer proof that explains the source

Scholarship

Official award letter showing what it covers

Papers not in English

Accredited English translations with the originals

Two checks save you trouble later:

  • Make sure each name, date and amount matches across all papers.
  • Check your provider's country and bank page. Some ask for bank-certified statements, not just online copies.

Which Sources of Funds Are Accepted for the Student Visa?

The key idea is genuine access. Home Affairs must be satisfied that you can really use the money for your study. So the source, the history and the person behind it all matter.

Sources That Usually Work

  • Savings held by you, your partner or your parents
  • Loans from a bank or financial institution in your name or your family's, with proof of payout
  • A parent's or partner's annual income, shown with official tax papers
  • Scholarships from governments, education providers or recognised organisations
  • Provident fund money you can freely access
  • Support from an employer, if the course fits your job

Sources That Raise Questions

  • A big deposit with no savings history
  • Recent "gifts" or loans from friends or third parties
  • A loan that is unusually large or was taken out long before you applied
  • An education loan that has not been paid out yet
  • Money that cannot be moved out of your country for study under local rules

Sources That Do Not Work

  • Business loans
  • Business funds, unless the people who can commit them are clearly shown and documented
  • Informal loans from friends or family
  • Cryptocurrency

An education loan is strong when the money has actually been released. If part of it is still to come, show how you will cover the rest.

Can You Use Your Father's Bank Statement for a Student Visa?

Yes, in most cases. Parents, a spouse or a de facto partner can support you. A third party, such as a family friend, is checked much more closely.

The officer wants to see that your sponsor can really pay and wants to help.

What a Parent Sponsor Should Provide

  • Proof of your relationship, such as your birth certificate
  • Their ID papers
  • Bank statements that show steady income and savings
  • Income papers, such as payslips, tax returns or business records
  • A signed declaration saying they will fund your study
  • Proof of where any large deposit came from

Make Sure the Story Makes Sense

The sponsor's income and savings should fit the amount you need. If the income shown only just matches the visa requirement, the officer may ask more questions.

It also helps to show past support, such as school or college fees they paid for you. Combined income from both parents can count under the income option.

Savings or Income Option?

Choose the route that your papers prove best. If you use the income option, remember it needs official tax papers. Bank statements and employer letters alone will not do.

How Much Bank Balance and How Many Months of Statements?

There is no single "magic balance". Your funds must cover the total of your tuition, living costs and travel. For a single student, that is the A$29,710 living amount plus your fees and flights.

Is a 3 Month Bank Statement Enough?

Home Affairs does not set a fixed number of months. In practice, three to six months of statements is a common range. Some education providers ask for at least six months.

A longer record is safer. It shows how your savings built up, which is more convincing than a balance on a single day.

Handling Large Deposits

A sudden big deposit just before you apply is a red flag. Explain it with real papers. Examples are a sale deed for a property, a bonus letter, a loan record or a clear bank transfer.

Fixed Deposits and Provident Funds

A fixed deposit can count if you can access the money when you apply. If it is new, show where the funds came from. A provident fund can count too, if you have free access to it.

Education Loans in Detail

Use a sanction letter from a recognised lender. It should show the approved amount, interest rate and repayment terms. Proof that the money has been paid out makes it much stronger.

Financial Document Mistakes That Can Cause a Student Visa Refusal

Most money problems are easy to avoid. Watch for these.

  • Showing only a balance certificate. It does not show how the money built up.
  • A big deposit with no explanation. Add papers for every large credit.
  • Counting on a loan that is not paid out. Show proof of release, or another source for the first 12 months.
  • Using an unclear sponsor. Give relationship proof, income papers and a signed declaration.
  • Forgetting dependants. Partner and child costs raise the total you must show.
  • Mismatched papers. Names, dates and amounts must agree everywhere.
  • Missing translations. Papers not in English need accredited translations.
  • Ignoring your Genuine Student answers. Your money story must fit your study plan and family background.
  • Using fake or edited papers. This can lead to a refusal and a ban on future visas. Never do it.

Australia Student Visa Financial Documents FAQs

How Much Bank Balance Is Required for an Australian Student Visa?

You must show enough for your first-year tuition, 12 months of living costs and travel. For a single student, living costs are A$29,710. Add your fees and flights for your total.

Is a 3 Month Bank Statement Enough for an Australia Student Visa?

Home Affairs does not set a fixed number of months. Three to six months is common, and some providers ask for six. A longer record that shows steady savings is safer.

Can I Use My Father's Bank Statement for a Student Visa?

Yes, parents can sponsor you. Add proof of your relationship, their income papers, a signed declaration and proof of any large deposit's source.

How to Show Proof of Funds for an Australian Student Visa?

Use bank statements, a loan sanction letter with proof of payout, or a parent's tax papers under the income option. Add receipts for fees already paid and a clear total of your costs.

Can I Use an Education Loan as Proof of Funds?

Yes, if it is from a recognised lender and you can really use it. Proof that the loan has been paid out makes it much stronger than a sanction letter alone.

Do Fixed Deposits Count as Proof of Funds?

Yes, if you can access the money when you apply. If the deposit is new, show where the funds came from.

What If I Have a Large Deposit in My Account?

Explain it with real papers. A sale deed, bonus letter, loan record or transfer proof can work. Never leave a large deposit unexplained.

How Much Parent Income Is Needed for the Income Option?

The annual income must be at least A$87,856 in the 12 months before you apply. With accompanying family, it is at least A$102,500. Official tax documents are needed.

Do I Need to Show Money for My Spouse and Children?

Yes, if they come with you. Add A$10,394 for a partner and A$4,449 for each child. School-age children also need school costs of A$13,502 each.

Do I Need a Migration Agent to Prepare My Financial Documents?

It is not required. But a registered agent can check your papers for gaps before you lodge. Always check that the agent is on the MARA register.

In Short

To meet the money rules, show enough for your first-year fees, 12 months of living costs and travel. For a single student, living costs are A$29,710. Back it up with clear bank history, a paid-out loan or a parent's tax papers, and explain every large deposit.

Start early and keep every paper consistent. Check the latest amounts on the Home Affairs site before you lodge. If your money story is complex, get your documents checked first.
