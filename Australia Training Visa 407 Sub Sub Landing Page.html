Training Visa (Subclass 407): Sponsorship, Training Plan and the 2026 Rules

The Training visa (subclass 407) lets a sponsored person take part in structured, workplace-based occupational training or professional development in Australia, for up to two years. It is not a work visa, and since 11 March 2026 the sponsor and the nomination must be approved before you can lodge. This guide explains how it works and where it fits.

The Basics

What Is the Training Visa (Subclass 407)?

A temporary visa for genuine, structured workplace training with an approved sponsor.

The Training visa (subclass 407) is a temporary visa for people who are sponsored to complete workplace-based occupational training or professional development in Australia. Training can be needed for registration or licensing, to improve skills in an eligible occupation, or to build capacity that benefits an organisation overseas. The visa can be granted for up to two years, and it is not a direct pathway to permanent residence.

The key idea is that the training is the purpose, and any work you do is part of that training. The visa does not let you take any job you like. A sponsor, which is an organisation approved as a Temporary Activities Sponsor, nominates a program of training for you, and that nomination includes the training plan. The Department of Home Affairs assesses whether the training is genuine, structured and suited to you.

Latest Change

The March 2026 Rule: Sponsor and Nomination Must Be Approved First

The government changed the order of the 407 process for applications made on or after 11 March 2026.

Before and after the 2026 change
ItemBefore 11 March 2026From 11 March 2026
Order of lodgementThe sponsorship, the nomination and the visa application could be lodged together.The sponsor must already be approved, and the nomination must be approved, before a valid visa application can be lodged.
NominationApproval was needed before the visa was granted.For sponsors other than Commonwealth agencies, the nomination must be approved, must not have ceased and must be identified in the visa application.
Commonwealth agency sponsorsExempt from the nomination requirement.Still exempt from the nomination requirement, but the agency must be an approved sponsor.
Effect on applicantsYou could lodge early and stay on a bridging visa while processing continued.You may need to wait for approvals first, which can mean planning another visa or travel in the meantime.

The Migration Amendment (Training Visas—Sponsorship Requirements) Regulations 2026 made this change. The Government's stated reasons include integrity concerns about the visa being used to extend an onshore stay or to bypass skilled migration pathways, a rise in onshore applications since mid-2024, and nominations that did not meet the legal requirements. Applications that look like ongoing work, or that have disproportionate training periods or low pay, can attract closer checking.

Eligibility

Training Visa 407 Eligibility Requirements

Both the sponsor and the applicant must qualify.

  • Sponsor: your sponsor must be an approved Temporary Activities Sponsor, or a Commonwealth Government agency where that pathway applies.
  • Nomination: an approved nomination of a program of occupational training for you, unless the sponsor is a Commonwealth agency.
  • Age: you must be at least 18 when you apply.
  • Genuine temporary stay: you must show you genuinely intend to stay temporarily and take part only in the approved activities.
  • English: functional English, shown through an approved test or an accepted passport, and signing the Australian values statement.
  • Health and insurance: meet the Australian health requirement and hold adequate health insurance for you and any family members.
  • Character: provide police certificates and meet the character requirement. Prior visa refusals, cancellations or debts to the Australian Government can affect your application.
  • Onshore applicants: you must hold an eligible temporary substantive visa and meet its conditions. Some visa types cannot be used to apply onshore.

Requirements differ by sponsor type and training, and the Department can request more evidence. We check your visa history, your sponsor's approval and the training plan before anyone lodges.

Sponsor and Nomination

How Sponsorship and Nomination Work for the 407

Under the current rules, the sponsor does most of the work before you lodge.

The three stages in order
StageWho does itWhat happens
1. Sponsorship approvalThe sponsoring organisationThe organisation applies to be an approved Temporary Activities Sponsor. A standard business sponsor approval used for the 482 and 186 does not by itself allow a 407 sponsorship.
2. NominationThe sponsorThe sponsor nominates a program of occupational training for you, including a detailed training plan. The Department must approve it.
3. Visa applicationYouOnce the sponsor and the nomination are approved, you lodge a valid visa application that identifies the nomination.

A sponsor cannot recover sponsorship or nomination charges from you. The Department does not publish processing times for sponsorship and nomination applications, so sponsors should lodge early and prepare decision-ready documents.

Training Plan

What a Genuine Training Plan Looks Like

The training plan is the heart of the application and the sponsor's legal responsibility.

The Department expects the training to be structured, genuine and tied to a specific occupation. A strong plan sets out what you will learn, how it will be taught and supervised, how progress is measured, and how the training will end with a clear outcome, for example registration, a licence or a defined skill. It also shows that the arrangement is a real training program and not a way to fill a vacancy.

Structure

A Real Schedule

Weekly activities, supervision and stages of progression, with named trainers who have suitable qualifications and industry experience.

Purpose

A Clear Outcome

A defined reason for the training, such as registration or a specific skill, and a link to your background and plans.

Fairness

Appropriate Pay and Duration

Training of a reasonable length with suitable remuneration. Disproportionate durations or very low pay can trigger refusal or closer checks.

Conditions

Work Rights and Conditions on a Training Visa

The visa is for training, and the conditions say so.

What a 407 does and does not allow
AllowedNot allowed
Work that is directly part of the approved training planGeneral employment outside the training program
Workplace-based training for skills, registration or professional developmentRegular work to fill staff shortages or supplement a workforce
Travel in and out of Australia while the visa is validUsing the visa as a route to ongoing work or permanent residence

Holders are generally subject to condition 8102, which limits work to the approved training activity. Family members who are included may have different work rights, set by the conditions on their visas. Breaching your conditions can lead to cancellation and affect future applications.

Get Organised

Documents to Prepare for a Training Visa Application

The exact list depends on the sponsor and the training.

  • Passport and identity documents for every person on the application.
  • The sponsor's approval details and the approved nomination reference.
  • The approved training plan and details of the trainers and supervisors.
  • Evidence of your qualifications and work history that link to the training.
  • English test results or evidence of an exemption.
  • Evidence of adequate health insurance for you and any family members.
  • Police certificates for each country where you have lived for the required period.
  • Details of your current visa and its conditions, if you are applying in Australia.
  • Relationship and birth documents for any family members you include.

Timing

How Long Does the Training Visa Take?

There are now three steps in sequence: sponsor approval, nomination approval and then the visa application. The Department does not publish processing times for sponsorship or nomination applications, and published times for the visa itself vary between sources, from a few months to well over a year. Because of the nomination-first rule, the full timeline is longer than it used to be.

We do not quote a single figure. Check the Department's live processing times for the visa and plan for months, not weeks. If your current visa is about to expire, talk to us early, because you may need another visa while the approvals are pending.

Fit Check

Is the Training Visa Right for You?

The 407 Tends to Suit You If

  • You need structured workplace training for registration, a licence or a defined skill.
  • An approved sponsor will nominate a genuine training program for you.
  • You plan to use the skills afterwards, in Australia or overseas.
  • You can wait for the sponsor and nomination to be approved first.

Consider Another Route If

  • You would really be doing a paid job you can already do, which points to the 482.
  • You want to study a course, which points to the Student visa.
  • You want a clear route to permanent residence.
  • You need to start soon and cannot wait for approvals.
Avoid These

Common Training Visa Mistakes That Lead to Refusal

Lodging Before Approval

An application lodged before the sponsor and nomination are approved is not valid under the current rules.

Using the Wrong Sponsor Approval

A business that sponsors 482 workers still needs Temporary Activities Sponsor approval for the 407.

A Vague Training Plan

Shadowing or general work experience is not enough. The plan needs structure, supervision and an outcome.

Training That Is Really a Job

Plans that look like filling a vacancy or offering ongoing work can be refused or delayed.

Ignoring Visa Expiry

The new sequence takes longer. Plan early if your current visa is about to end.

Charging the Worker

Sponsors cannot recover sponsorship or nomination costs from the worker, and doing so is a compliance risk.

Common Questions

Training Visa (Subclass 407) FAQs

What is the Training visa (subclass 407)?

The Training visa (subclass 407) is a temporary visa for people who come to Australia for structured, workplace-based occupational training or professional development with an approved sponsor. It can be granted for up to two years, and your work is limited to the sponsored training.

What changed for the 407 visa in 2026?

From 11 March 2026, you can no longer lodge a valid 407 visa application until your sponsor is an approved temporary activities sponsor and, unless the sponsor is a Commonwealth agency, the nomination for your training has been approved. Before that, sponsorship, nomination and the visa application could be lodged together.

Who can sponsor me for a Training visa?

The sponsor must be approved as a Temporary Activities Sponsor, or be a Commonwealth Government agency. A standard business sponsor approval, used for the 482 and 186 visas, does not by itself allow an employer to sponsor a 407 applicant.

Can I work for any employer on a 407 visa?

No. The visa is generally subject to condition 8102, which limits your work to the approved training activity. It is not an open work visa and should not be used to fill a job, supplement a workforce or extend a stay.

Does the 407 lead to permanent residence?

No, it is a temporary visa and not a direct pathway to permanent residence. The experience may help with a later skilled application if you meet that visa's own criteria.

How much does the 407 visa cost?

The visa application charge in effect from 1 July 2026 is AUD 535 for the main applicant, AUD 535 for each additional applicant aged 18 or over and AUD 135 for each applicant under 18. The sponsor's nomination and sponsorship fees are separate, and a sponsor cannot recover them from you. Confirm the amounts before you lodge.

How long does a 407 visa take?

Published figures vary, and the Department does not publish processing times for sponsorship or nomination applications. With the nomination-first rule, the total timeline is now longer than before, so employers and applicants should lodge early and expect months, not weeks.

What is the difference between the 407 and the 482?

The 407 is for training and is tied to an approved training plan, with a stay of up to two years and no pathway to permanent residence. The 482 is for filling a skilled job, can last up to four years and can lead to permanent residence through the 186. We help you decide which fits your situation.
