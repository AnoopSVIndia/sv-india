Why This Office Exists

A Local Office for a Very Personal Decision

Applying for an Australian visa from North India usually means sending original passports, bank statements and sponsorship letters to someone you've only spoken to on the phone. Our Chandigarh branch exists so that doesn't have to be true for our clients.

Set up in Sector 35, the office is a two-minute conversation away from most of Chandigarh, Mohali and Panchkula, and a comfortable half-day trip for families travelling in from Ludhiana, Patiala, Jalandhar or Ambala. Most weeks the office sees three recurring kinds of visitors: parents preparing to visit adult children studying or working in Australia on a sponsored visitor visa, students working through university shortlisting and student visa (subclass 500) paperwork, and returning clients who simply prefer to hand over original documents in person rather than by courier.

Every file that moves through this office is checked by a member of our MARA registered migration team before it's lodged, whether the applicant walked in or has only ever spoken to us by phone. The point of the physical office isn't paperwork theatre — it's that some questions (which documents actually count as proof of funds, how strict a particular case officer's post is this quarter, whether a client's specific ties-to-home evidence is strong enough) are easier to answer properly across a desk than over a chat window.

Before You Visit

What to Bring to Your First Appointment

Coming prepared saves most clients a second trip. For a typical visitor or student visa consultation, bring:

  • Your current passport, valid for the length of the intended stay or course.
  • Recent bank statements or payslips, if the visit is about funds or sponsorship.
  • Any invitation, sponsorship letter or admission offer you've already received.
  • Details of previous visa applications or refusals, if any, for either country.
  • A rough idea of your travel dates or intended course start date.
  • A list of questions — the appointment works best when it answers what's actually worrying you.

Common Questions

Chandigarh Office FAQs

What applicants from Punjab, Haryana and Delhi NCR usually ask before their first visit.

Which cities does the SuccessVisa Chandigarh branch serve?

Our Sector 35 office is the main walk-in point for applicants across Chandigarh, Mohali, Panchkula, Zirakpur, Patiala, Ludhiana, Jalandhar, Amritsar, Ambala and Delhi NCR. Many clients travel in for a single document-review appointment rather than needing repeat visits.

Do I need to visit the office in person, or can everything be done online?

Most of the process, including document collection and form preparation, can be handled remotely over phone, email or WhatsApp. Many clients still choose an in-person visit for original document verification, biometric guidance, or a face-to-face review before lodging, which the Chandigarh office is set up for.

Does the Chandigarh branch handle student visas as well as visitor visas?

Yes. While the Chandigarh office is best known for tourist and visitor visa enquiries, our advisors here also support student visa (subclass 500) applications, including course selection guidance and coordination with our education counsellors.

Can you help arrange a visitor visa for elderly parents from Chandigarh?

Yes, sponsored family and parent-visit applications are one of the most common enquiries at this branch. We help prepare sponsorship letters, financial evidence and health-insurance guidance for the applicant.

What are the Chandigarh office hours and how do I book an appointment?

The office is open Monday to Saturday, 9:00 am to 5:30 pm. You can book an appointment online, call or WhatsApp +91 90348-99620, or walk in during office hours, though booking ahead is recommended.

Is SuccessVisa Chandigarh a registered migration agency?

Yes. Applications handled through this office are reviewed by SuccessVisa's MARA registered migration agents, in line with Australian Government policy on who may provide immigration assistance. See the note below for more on this.
