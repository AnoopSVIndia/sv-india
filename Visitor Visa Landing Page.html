The Basics

What Is the Visitor Visa (Subclass 600)?

The Subclass 600 is Australia's general purpose temporary visa for people who want to visit rather than live in Australia. It sits under several streams, each built for a different reason for travel, and each with its own evidence requirements.

The visa is issued by the Department of Home Affairs, the Australian Government agency responsible for immigration and visa policy. Holders are granted a set period to remain in the country, most commonly three, six or twelve months, though the exact period is decided case by case and is always confirmed on the visa grant notice rather than promised in advance. The visa allows sightseeing, holidays, visiting relatives, attending short business meetings, or a mix of these, but it does not allow ongoing paid work in Australia under any stream.

Choose Your Stream

Which Stream Fits Your Visit?

Most applicants fall into one of three streams. Picking the right one before you lodge matters, since each stream is assessed against different criteria.

TS

Tourist Stream

Suited to holidays, sightseeing, cruises, or visiting friends and family without a formal sponsor. You will need to show genuine plans for a temporary visit and enough funds to cover your own travel and living costs while you are here.

Know more
SF

Sponsored Family Stream

For travellers whose visit is formally sponsored by an eligible relative who is an Australian citizen or permanent resident. In some cases the Department may ask the sponsor to lodge a security bond, decided on a case by case basis rather than applied automatically.

Know more
BV

Business Visitor Stream

For short business activities such as attending meetings, negotiations, conferences or exploring a business opportunity. This stream does not permit you to work for an Australian employer or provide services in the local labour market.

Requirements

Core Eligibility Requirements

Regardless of stream, every applicant is assessed against the same core criteria before a stream specific test is applied.

  • Genuine intention to visit temporarily, generally referred to as the genuine visitor or genuine temporary entry criterion.
  • Enough funds to cover travel, accommodation and living costs for the length of the visit, or an eligible sponsor who can demonstrate this on your behalf.
  • Health requirements are met, which can include arranged medical checks for some nationalities or longer stays.
  • Character requirements are met, and the applicant does not have a substantial criminal record.
  • Adequate health insurance is strongly recommended for the length of the stay, since visitor visa holders are personally responsible for their own medical costs in Australia.
  • No outstanding debts to the Australian Government from a previous visa or migration matter.
  • Compliance with the conditions of any visa previously held in Australia.
  • A separate application is lodged for each traveller, including children and anyone else listed in your passport.
Stay Length

How Long Can You Stay, and What Conditions Apply?

The Subclass 600 is a temporary visa. The exact stay period and conditions are set out on your individual visa grant notice, and can differ even between two people applying under the same stream.

  • Most grants allow a stay of three, six or twelve months, though the maximum is not automatic or guaranteed for every applicant.
  • Condition 8531 commonly applies, which requires you to leave Australia before your visa expires.
  • Condition 8503, known as No Further Stay, may be applied to some applications, particularly onshore ones, and would prevent you applying for most other visas while in Australia.
  • The visa does not permit ongoing paid work in Australia under any of the three main streams.
  • Always read your own grant notice closely, since it overrides any general guidance on this page.
Get Organised

Document Checklist

Exact requirements vary by stream and by your personal circumstances, but most applicants should prepare the following.

  • A passport valid for the length of your intended stay, ideally with some months to spare.
  • A recent passport style photograph, if requested by the Department.
  • Evidence of ties to your home country, such as employment, property or family, supporting a genuine intention to return.
  • Financial evidence, such as recent bank statements, payslips, or a sponsor's financial documents.
  • A clear travel itinerary, including flights and accommodation where available.
  • An invitation or sponsorship letter, if a relative in Australia is formally sponsoring your visit.
  • Details of your travel and health insurance arrangements for the trip.
  • Character documents, such as a police clearance, if requested by the Department for your circumstances.

Timing

How Long Does It Take?

Honest answer first: the Department does not guarantee a fixed processing time for any visitor visa application.

Processing depends on your stream, how complete your application is, the volume of applications the Department is handling, and whether extra checks are needed for your circumstances. As a general pattern, a straightforward tourist stream application lodged with all required evidence tends to move faster than a sponsored family application, which often involves extra checks on both the traveller and the sponsor. Peak travel periods, such as school holidays, typically see longer queues. Rather than quote a single number that may be out of date by the time you read this, we check the Department's own published processing time ranges for your specific stream and location before we advise you, and we recommend you do the same through the official Home Affairs processing times tool.

Avoid Common Mistakes

Why Applications Get Refused, and How We Help

Most refusals we see come down to a handful of recurring, avoidable issues.

  • Weak or inconsistent evidence of genuine intention to return home, particularly for first time travellers with limited ties.
  • Financial evidence that does not clearly cover the full length of the proposed stay.
  • Sponsor documents that are incomplete or do not clearly establish the sponsor's relationship and status.
  • Choosing the wrong stream for the actual purpose of travel.
  • Inconsistent details between the application form, supporting letters and previous travel history.

We review every application line by line against the criteria the Department actually assesses, rather than submitting a generic template, which is the main reason our clients see stronger outcomes.

Common Questions

Visitor Visa FAQs

Answers to what people usually ask us before applying.

Can I work on a Subclass 600 visa?

No. None of the three main streams allow ongoing paid work in Australia. Doing paid work on this visa can put your current and future visa applications at risk.

Can I study while on this visa?

Short, informal study or training of up to three months is generally allowed on the tourist stream, but this visa is not designed for a full course of study. If study is your real purpose, a student visa is usually the correct pathway instead.

Can I extend my stay once I am in Australia?

It depends on the conditions attached to your specific visa. If Condition 8503, No Further Stay, applies to your grant, you generally cannot apply for most further visas while onshore. Always check your own grant notice for the exact conditions that apply to you.

What is a security bond, and will I need one?

A security bond is an amount the Department can ask a sponsor to pay as assurance that the visitor will comply with visa conditions and depart on time. It is not charged automatically and is only requested in specific cases, mainly under the sponsored family stream.

Do I need travel or health insurance?

It is not always a strict legal requirement, but it is strongly recommended. Visitor visa holders are personally responsible for their own medical costs in Australia, and treatment without insurance can be expensive.

Can I include my children on the same application?

No. Each traveller, including children and anyone else listed in your passport, needs their own separate visa application.
