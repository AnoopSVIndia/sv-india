DAMA Visa Australia Explained: Regions, Occupations and PR

Quick Answer: A DAMA is an agreement that lets employers in 13 designated regions sponsor overseas workers for jobs the standard program does not cover. In practice, the DAMA visa in Australia is a 482, 494 or 186 visa. Only the employer can start it. It can bring concessions on age, English and pay, and it can lead to PR. The 482 charge is A$4,015 from 1 July 2026.

Many people search for a "DAMA visa". But there is no single visa with that name. DAMA is a type of agreement. The visa comes after.

This matters because the rules differ by region and by job. A job that works in one region may not work in another.

This guide explains how a DAMA works. It lists all 13 regions and shows who is eligible. It also covers concessions, 2026 costs, how long it takes, and how a DAMA can lead to PR.

What Is a DAMA Visa in Australia?

DAMA stands for Designated Area Migration Agreement. It is a formal deal between the Australian Government and a state, territory or regional body. It helps employers in that area hire overseas workers when they cannot fill jobs locally.

The agreement gives access to more occupations than the standard skilled program. It can also relax some visa rules, called concessions.

How a DAMA Works: Two Tiers

Home Affairs describes two levels:

  • Tier 1, the head agreement: a five-year deal with a Designated Area Representative (DAR). A DAR can be a state government, a chamber of commerce or a council. It sets the occupations and terms.
  • Tier 2, the labour agreement: an individual deal between Home Affairs and an endorsed employer. It generally runs for five years.

So the order is simple. The employer gets endorsed first. Then the employer signs a labour agreement. Only then can workers be nominated.

Which Visas Come Under a DAMA?

Approved employers can sponsor workers on three visas:

  • Subclass 482 (Skills in Demand): a temporary work visa.
  • Subclass 494 (Skilled Employer Sponsored Regional, Provisional): a regional visa with a path to a permanent one.
  • Subclass 186 (Employer Nomination Scheme): a permanent visa.

The agreement is the door. These visas are what the worker holds.

Can a Worker Apply for a DAMA on Their Own?

No. Only employers can apply for endorsement and a labour agreement. A worker needs a job offer from an endorsed employer first.

This is the biggest point many readers miss. Start by finding the right employer, not the right form.

Who Is a DAMA For?

It suits skilled and semi-skilled workers who want to live and work in a regional area. It also suits employers who struggle to hire locally. Some jobs on a DAMA list are not available through the standard program.

What Has Changed in 2026?

Recent updates include:

  • Townsville and a statewide Western Australia DAMA are now in place.
  • The Goldfields DAMA has been extended only to 31 December 2026.
  • A New South Wales DAMA has been proposed, but is not yet in place.
  • Home Affairs says it is supporting a move to state and territory-led DAMAs.

Because a region can end or change, check its status before you accept a job.

How to Find Out If Your Job Location Is in a DAMA

Your workplace must be inside the designated area. For example, the Goulburn Valley covers Greater Shepparton, Moira and Campaspe. The South West covers 12 local areas, from Busselton to Nannup.

In Western Australia, Perth is split from regional areas, and the two have different rules. Always check the exact postcode with the DAR.

Do Regions Differ from Each Other?

Yes, a lot. Each region sets its own:

  • Occupation list.
  • Concessions.
  • Endorsement steps and fees.
  • Number of places.

So never assume what works in one region works in another.

DAMA Occupations, Concessions and Who Is Eligible

This is the part most readers want. Which jobs qualify? What rules bend? And who can actually apply?

Which Occupations Are Covered?

Every DAMA has its own occupation list. The list can include jobs that are not on the standard skilled lists. Some are semi-skilled roles.

The lists often cover areas such as:

  • Community and aged care, including carers and youth workers.
  • Trades, such as plumbing.
  • Hospitality and kitchen roles.
  • Health support roles, such as dental hygienists and veterinary nurses.
  • Transport, including truck driving.
  • Office and accounts roles.

The exact list differs by region. In Western Australia, metropolitan Perth focuses on skill levels 1 to 3, with some level 4 roles. Regional WA has a broader list. Always download the current list from the region you plan to work in.

Salary can bend too. The standard threshold for 482 and 186 is A$79,423 from 1 July 2026. Some DAMAs allow a lower pay level, but the employer must still pay at least the market rate. In the Northern Territory it can be 85% of the threshold. Please confirm with the region.

Who Is Eligible for a DAMA?

There are two sides: the employer and the worker.

The employer must:

  • Be based in the designated area.
  • Have operated for at least 12 months and be financially viable.
  • Show it cannot fill the role locally.
  • Get endorsement from the DAR.
  • Offer pay and terms no less favourable than for Australian workers.

The worker must:

  • Have a job offer from an endorsed employer.
  • Match the occupation, with the skills and experience required.
  • Meet the English, age, health and character rules.
  • Pass a skills assessment if one is required.

Is a Skills Assessment Needed?

Sometimes. Some jobs need one, and some do not. Where it is needed, an approved assessing body checks your qualifications and work history. Some bodies charge about A$1,146 to A$1,261 for DAMA assessments. Outcomes are often valid for three years.

Ask your employer or DAR before you pay.

Jobs and Situations That Do Not Qualify

  • Fly-in fly-out roles, in some regions such as WA.
  • Industries already covered by an Industry Labour Agreement.
  • Roles where the employer has not tried to hire locally.
  • Jobs outside the designated area.

How to Apply for a DAMA Visa in Australia

The process has four stages. The employer leads the first two. The worker joins at stage three.

Step-by-Step Application Process

  1. Find the right region and job. Check the occupation list for the region.
  2. Employer gets DAR endorsement. The employer applies to the regional body. In WA, the endorsement is valid for 12 months.
  3. Employer applies for a labour agreement. This goes to Home Affairs through ImmiAccount.
  4. Employer lodges the nomination. This names the worker and the job.
  5. Worker lodges the visa application. Family members can apply too.
  6. Worker completes health, police and skills checks. Do these early.
  7. Wait for the decision. Do not start work until the visa is granted.

What Employers Must Prove

  • A real attempt to recruit Australians first. For 482 and 494 applications, WA asks for at least two ads, each running 28 days.
  • Financial records, such as accountant letters and statements.
  • An organisation chart that is under six months old.
  • A signed declaration of no adverse findings in the past five years.

What Workers Should Prepare

  • A valid passport and identity documents.
  • Qualifications and transcripts.
  • Work history with payslips or tax records.
  • English test results, if needed.
  • A skills assessment outcome, if needed.
  • Health and police checks.

Tips for a Smoother Application

  • Talk to the DAR before you apply. Many offer free matching or advice.
  • Match your job title and duties to the occupation list.
  • Keep every date consistent across forms.
  • Use accredited translations for non-English documents.
  • Apply early. Endorsement does not last forever.

What If an Endorsement Is Declined?

In WA, a declined business can ask for a review within 28 calendar days. The review only looks at the original material. Other regions have their own rules, so ask the DAR.

Who Pays What?

Employers usually handle the endorsement, labour agreement, nomination and levy. Workers usually pay their own visa charge, health checks and skills assessment. Western Australia and South Australia charge no endorsement fee at present. Other regions may charge.

Agent fees are extra. Some firms quote thousands of dollars for the full DAMA process, so ask for a clear breakdown.

Does a DAMA Visa Lead to PR?

Yes, it can. But it is not automatic. A DAMA visa leads to PR only if you and your employer follow the right pathway.

The 482 to 186 Pathway

This is the main route. You start on a temporary visa. After enough time with your employer, they can nominate you for a permanent one.

  1. Get endorsed and sponsored on a 482, in a DAMA region.
  2. Work in the approved role for your employer.
  3. Reach the time requirement. South Australia says a 482 holder is eligible for 186 nomination after at least two years. In some regions it is three years. Confirm your own timing.
  4. Employer nominates you for the 186 under the labour agreement.
  5. You apply for the 186. If granted, you become a permanent resident.

You must still be working for the same employer under the labour agreement.

The 494 to 191 Pathway

The 494 is a five-year regional visa. South Australia says it leads to subclass 191, a permanent regional visa. Check the current rules with Home Affairs before you rely on this.

Rules That Can Stop You

  • No automatic PR. You must meet the 186 rules and the employer must nominate you.
  • Age. The standard 186 limit is 45. DAMAs may raise it, up to 55 in some regions.
  • The DAMA must still be valid. Your nomination must come through the agreement.
  • Western Australia: the permanent pathway is only for existing 482 and 494 holders whose first nomination came through the WA DAMA.
  • Your job must continue. Losing the job can end the path.

What If Your Region Ends?

Some DAMAs end or change. The Goldfields DAMA, for example, is extended only to 31 December 2026. Ask your DAR what happens to workers already on a visa. Plan for the risk before you move.

DAMA Risks, Common Mistakes and What to Check First

A DAMA can open doors. But it also ties you to one employer and one area. Know the risks before you accept a job.

Risks Workers Should Weigh

  • You depend on one employer. The visa is linked to the labour agreement, so changing jobs is not simple.
  • You depend on one region. The visa is tied to the designated area.
  • Rules can change. Occupation lists, concessions and even regions can change or end.
  • PR is not guaranteed. Your employer must nominate you, and you must meet the rules.
  • Regional life is different. Rent, transport and services vary a lot.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Looking for a DAMA "visa" form. There is no such form for workers. Start with an endorsed employer.
  • Assuming your job is on every list. Each region has its own list.
  • Paying for a job offer. Be careful of anyone who sells sponsorship. A genuine employer pays its own sponsorship costs.
  • Missing the endorsement window. Endorsements can expire. In WA it is 12 months.
  • Using an old guide. Fees, thresholds and regions changed in 2026.
  • Ignoring the PR rules from day one. Ask about the 186 path before you sign.

What Employers Should Check First

  • Does your location sit inside the designated area?
  • Is the occupation on that region's list?
  • Can you show you tried to hire locally?
  • Are your records, accounts and organisation chart ready?
  • Does your pay meet the rules?

Questions to Ask a Possible Employer

  • Are you endorsed under a DAMA, or will you apply?
  • Which visa will you sponsor me on?
  • Will you support a 186 nomination later, and when?
  • What happens if the agreement ends?

Good employers answer clearly. Be wary if they will not.

Frequently Asked Questions About the DAMA Visa in Australia

How Long Does a DAMA Visa Take?

Allow about 6 to 12 months from endorsement to visa grant, and some cases take longer. Endorsement takes about 2 to 4 weeks. Visa decisions vary by subclass, from a median of about 44 days for a 186 to about 6 months for a 482. These are not guarantees.

Does a DAMA Visa Lead to PR?

It can. The usual route is a 482 followed by a 186 nomination from your employer, often after two to three years. The 494 can lead to a permanent regional visa. PR is not automatic, so ask about the pathway before you sign a contract.

Who Is Eligible for DAMA?

The employer must be in a designated area, operate for at least 12 months, prove it cannot hire locally and get endorsement from the regional body. The worker needs a job offer, the right skills and experience, and must meet English, age, health and character rules.

How Much Does It Cost to Get a DAMA Visa?

From 1 July 2026, the base visa charge is A$4,015 for a 482 and A$6,140 for a 494 or 186. Employers also pay nomination charges, a levy and sometimes an endorsement fee. Skills assessments and agent fees are extra. Confirm the amounts with Home Affairs.

Can I Apply for a DAMA Visa on My Own?

No. Only employers apply for endorsement and a labour agreement. You need a job offer from an endorsed employer first.

Which Regions Have a DAMA in 2026?

Home Affairs lists 13: Far North Queensland, Townsville, Adelaide City, South Australia Regional, East Kimberley, Pilbara, South West, The Goldfields, Western Australia, Goulburn Valley, Great South Coast, Orana and the Northern Territory.

What Is the Difference Between a DAMA and a Standard Employer-sponsored Visa?

A DAMA gives access to more occupations and can relax age, English, pay and experience rules. In return, you work for an endorsed employer in a set region.

What Is the Age Limit for a DAMA Visa?

The standard limit for permanent and regional visas is 45. Some DAMAs raise it, up to 55 in some regions. Check your region's concession.

Can I Bring My Family on a DAMA Visa?

Yes, eligible family members can usually apply with you. They pay their own visa charges and meet health and character rules.

Can I Change Employer or Region on a DAMA Visa?

It is not simple. The visa is tied to the labour agreement and the area. A new employer would generally need to be covered by an agreement too. Get advice before you move.

In Short

A DAMA is an agreement that lets employers in 13 regions sponsor workers on a 482, 494 or 186 visa. Only employers can start it. It can bring concessions on age, English and pay, and it can lead to PR through a 186 nomination, but not automatically.

Start with the region and the occupation list, then find an endorsed employer. Check the 2026 fees and the end date of your region. If your case is complex, speak with a MARA-registered agent before you accept a job.
