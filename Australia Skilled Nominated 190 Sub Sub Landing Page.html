Skilled Nominated Visa (Subclass 190): State Nomination, Points and How to Apply

The Skilled Nominated visa (subclass 190) gives skilled workers permanent residence in Australia when a state or territory government nominates them. The nomination adds 5 points to your score, but each state has its own occupation list, rules and timing. This guide explains how the 190 works and how to build a profile a state will back.

The Basics

What Is the Skilled Nominated Visa (Subclass 190)?

A permanent visa for skilled workers who are nominated by an Australian state or territory government and then invited through SkillSelect.

The subclass 190 is a points-tested state-nominated skilled migration visa. You submit an Expression of Interest (EOI) in SkillSelect, the Department of Home Affairs' online system, and a state or territory government can choose you for nomination if your occupation and background match its priorities. A nomination adds 5 points to your score. Once you are nominated, the Department invites you to lodge the visa application.

The 190 is a permanent visa, so you can work and study in Australia, enrol in Medicare, travel for five years from the date of grant, sponsor eligible relatives and later apply for Australian citizenship once you meet the residence rules. The trade-off is that the state that nominates you expects you to live and work there for an initial period. That is why choosing the right state matters as much as the points.

Eligibility

Subclass 190 Visa Eligibility Requirements

You need to meet every requirement below. A state's own rules sit on top of these national criteria.

  • State or territory nomination: a nomination from the state or territory government, recorded in SkillSelect.
  • Eligible occupation: an occupation the nominating state or territory invites for the 190. Each jurisdiction publishes its own list, and it is usually narrower than the federal skilled occupation lists.
  • Positive skills assessment: the relevant assessing authority must confirm your skills for that occupation.
  • Age: you must be under 45 when you are invited to apply.
  • English: at least Competent English, shown through an approved test such as IELTS, PTE Academic, TOEFL iBT, OET or Cambridge C1 Advanced, or an accepted passport. Some states ask for more.
  • Points: at least 65 points on the points test, which includes the 5 points for nomination.
  • Invitation: an invitation to apply issued through SkillSelect, which you must act on within 60 days.
  • Health and character: meet the Australian health requirement and provide police certificates for the character requirement.

Lists and criteria change. State occupation lists and nomination criteria are updated often, and being on a list does not guarantee nomination. We check the current state criteria and your assessing authority before we advise you.

Why 65 Points Still May Not Get You Nominated

Because nomination adds 5 points, a profile with 60 points on the table above reaches the 65-point pass mark. That makes the 190 an option for candidates who cannot compete for a 189 invitation. But the pass mark is only a minimum. States choose from the profiles that meet their own criteria, and many prefer higher scores, current employment in the state, or occupations facing local shortages. When your score is close to the minimum, the useful questions are which state values your occupation and what you can add before applying.

State Nomination

How State and Territory Nomination Works for the 190

Nomination is where the 190 differs most from the 189, and where most of the planning happens.

Each of the eight state and territory governments runs its own skilled nomination program within the places the Australian Government gives it each program year. The 2026-27 Migration Program includes 35,500 places in the State/Territory Nominated category, up from 33,000 in 2025-26, shared between the eight jurisdictions. Each one decides its own occupation list, minimum requirements, opening dates and how it selects people.

Some states select directly from SkillSelect EOIs. Others ask you to submit a Registration of Interest or a separate application first. Programs open and close during the year as allocations are used, so timing can matter as much as eligibility.

After Nomination

The State Commitment: What You Agree to When You Are Nominated

Read this before you accept a nomination, because the commitment is part of the deal.

When a state or territory nominates you, you are normally asked to confirm that you intend to live and work there. The usual period is about two years after the visa is granted, and some jurisdictions ask for longer, so confirm the exact terms in the state's own documents. It is a commitment you make to the state, and not a condition on the permanent visa itself. Leaving early does not cancel your permanent residence, but it can affect how that state treats future nominations for you or your family, and it can undermine the case for your own ties to the state.

This is why we ask clients to think about where they actually want to live and work before choosing a state. A nomination that fits your career, family and budget is worth more than one that is simply easier to get.

Step Zero

Skills Assessment for the 190 Visa

Your skills assessment is the first formal gate, and the longest lead time you control.

A skills assessment is an independent check that your qualifications and work experience meet Australian standards for your nominated occupation. Each occupation is linked to a specific assessing authority, for example Engineers Australia for engineering occupations, the Australian Computer Society for ICT roles, VETASSESS for many professional and trade occupations, CPA Australia or CA ANZ for accountants, and the Australian Nursing and Midwifery Accreditation Council for nursing and midwifery.

Each authority sets its own documents, fees and timeframes. For the 190, check that the occupation you assess for is also on the nominating state's list, because an assessment for an occupation the state does not invite will not help your nomination. We help you match your degree and job duties to the right ANZSCO occupation before you pay for an assessment.

Get Organised

Documents to Prepare Before You Apply

Not every applicant needs every item, but this list covers what most 190 files require.

  • Passport and national ID for every person on the application.
  • Positive skills assessment outcome letter.
  • English test result report that is still valid.
  • Degree certificates and academic transcripts.
  • Employment references showing duties, hours and dates.
  • Payslips, tax returns or bank statements supporting employment claims.
  • Police certificates from each country you have lived in for the required period.
  • Relationship evidence and partner documents, if applicable.
  • Birth certificates for dependent children.
  • Proof of living or working in the state, such as a lease, employment contract or study records, if the state asks for it.

Fit Check

Is the Skilled Nominated Visa Right for You?

The 190 Tends to Suit You If

  • Your occupation is on a state's list and you have, or can get, a positive skills assessment.
  • You are under 45 with Competent English or better.
  • You would be happy to live and work in the nominating state for at least a couple of years.
  • Your points reach 65 only with the 5 nomination points.

Consider Another Route If

  • You want to settle in a particular state that does not invite your occupation.
  • Your score is high enough for a 189 invitation, which carries no state commitment.
  • You are open to regional life and need more points, in which case the 491 may fit.
  • An employer is willing to sponsor you.
Avoid These

Common Subclass 190 Mistakes That Cost Applicants a Year

Choosing a State by Ease Alone

A nomination you cannot honour is costly. Pick a state where you can realistically live and work after grant.

Over-Claiming Points

Points in your EOI must be provable when you apply. A claim you cannot back up can lead to refusal, and later visa applications may be affected.

Assessing the Wrong Occupation

An assessment for an occupation the state does not invite will not support your nomination, and the fee and months are wasted.

Missing a Program Window

States open and close programs during the year. Being ready with your assessment and English result lets you move when a program opens.

Missing the 60-Day Window

You cannot extend the deadline after your invitation. Gathering police certificates and medicals late is the usual reason files run behind.

Ignoring Age Milestones

Points fall at 33 and 40, and eligibility ends at 45. Age is the one variable you cannot improve, so plan your timeline around it.

Common Questions

Subclass 190 Visa FAQs

How many points do I need for the subclass 190 visa?

The minimum is 65 points, and that total includes the 5 points added by state or territory nomination. So without nomination you would need 60 points on the points test. Passing the mark does not guarantee nomination, because each state sets its own criteria and opening dates.

Do I need a job offer for the Skilled Nominated visa?

Not for the visa itself. The 190 has no employer sponsor. You do need a nomination from a state or territory government, and some states look for current skilled employment in that state as part of their own criteria.

What is the age limit for the 190 visa?

You must be under 45 when you are invited to apply, the same as the 189. Many states also check age at the time of nomination.

How long do I have to apply after I am invited?

You have 60 days from the date of the invitation to lodge your visa application. Prepare your documents before the invitation arrives, because the deadline cannot be extended.

Do I have to live in the nominating state after I get the 190?

States usually expect you to live and work in the nominating state or territory for a period after grant, commonly about two years, and some ask for longer. It is a commitment you make to the state rather than a formal visa condition, but ignoring it can affect future nominations for you or your family. Confirm the exact terms with the state you apply to.

What is the difference between the 189, 190 and 491 visas?

The 189 needs no sponsor and adds no points. The 190 needs state or territory nomination, adds 5 points and is permanent from the day it is granted. The 491 is a five-year provisional regional visa that adds 15 points and has a pathway to permanent residence.

Is the points test changing?

The Australian Government has announced changes to the permanent skilled points test, but as at 26 October 2026 no new points table is in force. The current Schedule 6D table and the 65-point pass mark still apply to the 190.

Can I include my partner and children?

Yes. Eligible family members can be included in the application, each with their own visa application charge. A partner can also add points if they are under 45 and have a suitable skills assessment and Competent English.

Can I apply for the 190 from inside Australia?

Yes. You can lodge the visa application in Australia or from overseas once you hold an invitation. Many states prefer or require applicants who already live and work in the state, and some run separate offshore streams, so check each state's pathway before choosing.
