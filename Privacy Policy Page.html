Privacy Policy

SuccessVisa is an Australian migration and education consultancy with offices in Australia, India and Fiji. This policy explains what personal information we collect, why we use it, who we share it with, how long we keep it and the rights you have under the laws that apply where you live.

Last updated: 26 October 2026Applies to: Australia | India | FijiRegistration: MARN 2318228
Who We Are

About This Policy

In this policy, SuccessVisa, we, us and our mean SuccessVisa (an Australian entity) and its branches in Parramatta, Toongabbie, Melbourne CBD, Werribee, Chandigarh and Suva. Our migration services are provided with MARA registered migration agents (MARN 2318228).

This policy applies to our websites (including successvisa.in and successvisa.com.au), our booking and contact forms, our messaging channels and the personal information you give us when you use our migration, education and related services. By using our website or giving us your personal information you acknowledge this policy. Where the law requires your consent, we will ask for it separately.

Where this policy sets different rules for Australia, India or Fiji, the rules for the country where you live, or where the relevant branch is located, apply in addition to the general rules.

Country by Country

The Laws We Follow in Each Country

The privacy laws that apply to your information depend on where you are and which branch you deal with.

Australia

Privacy Act 1988 (Cth)
  • We handle personal information in line with the Australian Privacy Principles (APPs) in the Privacy Act 1988, to the extent the Act applies to us.
  • As registered migration agents we must also keep client information confidential and secure under the Migration Agents Code of Conduct.
  • Notifiable Data Breaches scheme, Spam Act 2003, Do Not Call Register Act 2006 and the statutory tort for serious invasions of privacy may also apply.
  • Complaints can go to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC) after you have complained to us.

India

DPDP Act 2023 and IT Act 2000
  • We process personal data of people in India in line with the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and the DPDP Rules, 2025, as they come into force in phases.
  • Until the phased commencement is complete, the Information Technology Act, 2000 and the 2011 Sensitive Personal Data or Information Rules continue to apply.
  • Telecom marketing rules, including do-not-disturb preferences, apply to our calls and messages.
  • You can complain to us first and then, once operating, to the Data Protection Board of India.

Fiji

Constitution, clause 24 and sector laws
  • Fiji does not yet have a comprehensive data protection statute. Clause 24 of the 2013 Constitution protects personal privacy, including confidentiality of personal information.
  • The Cybercrime Act 2021 and the Online Safety Act 2018 may also apply to how information is accessed and shared.
  • In October 2025 the Fiji Cabinet endorsed a National Privacy and Personal Data Protection Policy as the basis for future legislation.
  • We apply the same core standards in Fiji as elsewhere and will follow any new Fiji law when it takes effect.

India timeline: the DPDP Rules, 2025 were notified on 13 November 2025 and commence in phases over roughly 12 to 18 months, with the main obligations on organisations scheduled for 13 May 2027. Australia's Parliament is also considering further Privacy Act reforms, including a draft Privacy Amendment (Personal Data Protection) Bill 2026 that is not yet law. We review this policy as these laws change.

How We Use It

Why We Use Your Information

  • To provide services: assess eligibility, advise on visas, courses and insurance, prepare and lodge applications and keep you informed.
  • To meet legal duties: keep records, protect client documents and respond to regulators, including obligations under the Migration Agents Code of Conduct.
  • To manage our business: process payments, handle complaints, train staff, improve our website and services, and manage risk.
  • To communicate: reply to enquiries, send appointment reminders and, where you have agreed or the law allows, send updates and offers.

Under the APPs we collect personal information that is reasonably necessary for our functions, and we use it for the purpose we collected it or a related purpose you would reasonably expect, unless you consent or the law allows otherwise. In India we process personal data for the purposes you have consented to or for the limited legitimate uses the DPDP Act allows. In Fiji we use your information for the purposes explained here and in line with our duty of confidentiality to clients.

Sensitive Information

Health, Character and Other Sensitive Information

Visa and student applications often require sensitive information.

Many applications require health examinations, insurance details, police certificates, character declarations or biometric information. In Australia, the Privacy Act generally requires your consent before we collect sensitive information, and we ask for it when we take your instructions. In India we ask for your consent for the specific purpose and you can withdraw it, although this may stop us from continuing your application. We aim to keep sensitive information to the minimum needed and to limit who in our team can see it.

Overseas Transfers

Sending Information Between Australia, India, Fiji and Other Countries

Your matter may involve people and systems in more than one country.

Because we operate in Australia, India and Fiji, your information may be accessed or stored in those countries and in other countries where our service providers and cloud systems operate, as well as in the country where your visa or course application is lodged. Before disclosing information overseas, we take reasonable steps to make sure the recipient handles it in a way consistent with the APPs, as required by APP 8, unless an exception applies, such as your consent or a legal requirement.

For personal data about people in India, we follow the DPDP Act rules on transfers outside India, as they apply. These permit transfers except to countries the Government restricts. For information about people in Fiji, we apply the same safeguards.

Security

How We Protect and How Long We Keep Information

We take reasonable steps to protect personal information from misuse, interference, loss and unauthorised access. These steps include restricting access to staff who need it, securing paper and electronic records, using passwords and other access controls, taking care with portable devices and third-party storage, and training our team. Migration agents must keep client documents secure under the Migration Agents Code of Conduct.

We keep personal information for as long as we need it to provide services, meet our legal and professional record-keeping duties, resolve disputes and enforce our agreements. When we no longer need it and the law allows, we take reasonable steps to destroy or de-identify it. In India, once the purpose is complete and you withdraw consent, we will erase your data unless the law requires us to keep it.

If There Is a Data Breach

If a breach is likely to cause serious harm, we will assess it and notify affected people and, where required, the OAIC under Australia's Notifiable Data Breaches scheme. For people in India, we will report breaches to the Data Protection Board and affected individuals as the DPDP Rules require when those provisions apply.

Marketing

Marketing Messages and Your Choices

We may send you information about our services if you have asked for it, agreed to it or the law otherwise allows. In Australia, we follow the Spam Act 2003 and the Do Not Call Register Act 2006, and every commercial message will say who we are and how to unsubscribe. In India, we follow the telecom regulator's rules on commercial calls and messages, including do-not-disturb preferences. You can opt out at any time by using the unsubscribe link or contacting us. Opting out of marketing does not stop messages about your matter.

Cookies

Cookies and Website Information

Our website stores a small preference, such as your India or Australia region choice, in your browser using local storage and a cookie so that the site opens in the right region next time. Our pages also load fonts from Google Fonts, which means your browser connects to Google's servers and shares information such as your IP address. If we use analytics, advertising or messaging tools, they may set their own cookies, and we will describe them and, where required, ask for your consent. You can block or delete cookies in your browser settings, though the site may not work as intended.

Children

Children and Minors

We collect personal information about children, such as dependants on a visa application, from their parent or guardian. Our website is not intended for children to use alone. In India, the DPDP Act treats anyone under 18 as a child and requires verifiable consent from a parent or lawful guardian before we process a child's data, and we will not track or target children for marketing. In Australia and Fiji we deal with a parent or guardian on behalf of a child.

Automated Tools

Automated Decisions and Artificial Intelligence

We do not use automated systems to make final decisions about your application. Decisions are made by the relevant government or education provider. If we begin using automated decision-making or AI tools that significantly affect individuals, or that handle your personal information, we will update this policy to describe them, as Australian privacy law will require from 10 December 2026, and we will keep a registered migration agent responsible for your advice.

Third Parties

Links to Other Websites

Our website links to government, education provider and insurer websites and to messaging services such as WhatsApp. Those sites have their own privacy policies and we are not responsible for them. Please read their policies before you give them information.

Updates

Changes to This Policy

We may update this policy when our practices or the law change, including as India's data protection rules take effect and as Australian and Fiji privacy laws develop. The date at the top shows when it was last updated. If we make a material change, we will tell you in an appropriate way.
