New Ministerial Direction 123 Changes How Visa Character Decisions Are Weighed

From 31 October 2026, decision-makers apply Ministerial Direction 123, which replaces Direction 110. It applies to decisions made on or after that date, even if the application was lodged earlier. Reports say the four primary considerations are now community protection, family violence, the expectations of the Australian community and the best interests of minor children in Australia, while length of residence, family ties and employment move to secondary. The character test in section 501 of the Migration Act itself is not changing.

What it means for you: If you or a family member have criminal convictions, family violence matters or other adverse information, tell your agent at the start. Refusal or cancellation is not automatic, and each case turns on its own facts.

Jobs and Skills Australia Publishes Its 2026 Occupation Shortage List

Jobs and Skills Australia has released its 2026 Occupation Shortage List. Its release says occupation shortages continue to ease overall, and it includes new analysis of what is driving the gaps that persist. The list is a labour market assessment. It does not by itself change which occupations are eligible for a particular visa.

What it means for you: Check your occupation against the Home Affairs list for your specific visa, not the shortage list alone, before you plan a skilled application around it.

Student Visa Rules Changed on 2 October 2026: Family Members and Onshore Applications

New rules for the Student visa (subclass 500) and Student Guardian visa (subclass 590) took effect on 2 October 2026. In short, most applicants can no longer include family members, and most temporary visa holders can no longer apply for a Student visa from inside Australia.

  • Family: exemptions apply to doctoral students, Pacific and ASEAN nationals, fully funded foreign government scholarship holders, and DFAT or Defence sponsored students. Family cannot be added after a visa is granted.
  • Onshore: this includes Working Holiday Maker and Skills in Demand visa holders. Exemptions include moving to a higher level course, up to 12 more months with the same provider, and provider default. Otherwise, apply from outside Australia and be outside Australia when the visa is granted.
  • Existing applications: applications lodged before 2 October are assessed under the earlier rules, and current Student visa holders are not affected.

What it means for you: Planning to add a partner, change provider or switch from another visa? Check your options before you lodge, because the rules now depend on your exact circumstances.

Source: Department of Home Affairs, Changes to Student visa application rules · Study Australia, Student visa changes: October 2026 update · Checked 30 October 2026Student visa guide

Visitor Visa Tourist Stream: 21 and 33 Days in the Latest Home Affairs Processing Times

Home Affairs published updated processing times for the Visitor visa (subclass 600) tourist stream on 29 September 2026. Half of applications were decided within 21 days and 90% within 33 days. Times move with demand and passport country, and sponsored family stream cases can take longer because both the visitor and the sponsor are assessed.

What it means for you: Processing times are a guide, not a promise. Lodge early and have your evidence complete before you submit.

Source: Department of Home Affairs, global visa processing times · Checked 30 October 2026Visitor visa guide

Skilled Visa Processing Order Reset by Ministerial Directions 121 and 122

From 19 September 2026, Direction 122 sets the processing order for skilled visas including the 186, 187, 189, 190, 191, 489, 491, 494, 887 and 888, replacing Direction 119. The top tier is primary applicants in construction, healthcare, teaching, agriculture, aquaculture, fishing, resources, and law enforcement or defence roles, then other onshore applicants, then offshore single applicants. Employer sponsored visas no longer rank above independent visas. Direction 121 sets a new processing queue for the Skills in Demand (482) visa.

What it means for you: Priority changes the order in which cases are processed, not how they are assessed. It does not guarantee a grant or a timeframe.

Source: AHC Lawyers, 21 September 2026 · Checked 30 October 2026Skilled visa services

Quick Answers

Visa News FAQs

A few questions about how to use these updates.

Where can I find official Australian visa updates?

The Department of Home Affairs website publishes visa changes, processing times and SkillSelect results. Ministerial directions are published on the Federal Register of Legislation, and Jobs and Skills Australia publishes occupation shortage data. We link to the source on every update so you can check it yourself.

Does a new rule apply to an application I have already lodged?

It depends on the rule. For example, Student visa applications lodged before 2 October 2026 are assessed under the rules in place when they were lodged, while the new character direction applies to decisions made on or after 31 October 2026 whenever the application was lodged. Ask a registered migration agent about your own application.

How often is this page updated?

We add an update when a change is announced or takes effect. Each item shows its date, its status and the day we last checked it against the source, and the page shows when it was last reviewed.

Is this page migration advice?

No. These are short summaries of public changes, written to help you understand what is happening. They do not consider your personal circumstances, so book an appointment with a registered migration agent before you act on any of them.
